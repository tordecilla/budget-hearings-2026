At a glance

  • Vaping tests and health follow-up: Members questioned the testing process, the division of work between trade and health agencies, and enforcement against unregistered sellers. The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) described its testing and enforcement role, while Senator Pia S. Cayetano sought follow-up with the Department of Health (DOH) and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) on health expertise and results.
  • Space capability and exploratory launch: The Philippine Space Agency (PhilSA) defended domestic capability, locally useful space data and skills, and a planned pilot rocket launch. Commercial discussions and possible launch-site development remained exploratory, alongside questions about civilian uses and public oversight.
  • Pax Silica, residents and utilities: The Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA) described private-sector funding and development plans, while members questioned protection and support for affected residents. The exchange recorded a commitment against relocation or displacement before funding and rights safeguards are in place; power and water needs, project scope and access for nearby communities remained under discussion.
  • Electricity costs, reliability and transition: Senators challenged electricity prices and the effect of legacy contracts, and discussed oversight and plans for the energy mix. Agency responses covered outages and corrective action, expected service stabilization, grid and generation constraints, and the effects of weather and renewable supply.

Department of Trade and Industry (DTI)

Senator Pia S. Cayetano: Vape-liquid testing laboratories, standards and expertise: Senator Pia S. Cayetano questioned the use of Shenzhen Element Testing Company Limited and whether its laboratory could be treated as a gold-standard tester. Sponsor Senator Paolo Benigno "Bam" Aquino IV relayed that the Department of Health (DOH) had identified the Tobacco Laboratory Network (TobLabNet) standard, and said the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) accredits laboratories able to test against it. Aquino said products had initially been tested in Shenzhen, while local facilities in Cavite and Quezon City now test most products. Cayetano noted that only one local facility, La Primera Testing Center in Quezon City, tested liquid rather than devices, and said she would verify the standard with DOH. She questioned DTI's ability to assess health safety without medical expertise; Aquino agreed DTI officials were not health experts and said they relied on TobLabNet's tests and equipment criteria. Cayetano recounted her understanding of Supreme Court rulings placing tobacco under the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and argued that inhaled vape liquid likewise required health-regulator scrutiny. She said she would check the standards with DOH and FDA; Aquino said DTI had been assigned the task and had chemical engineers rather than medical or addiction specialists.

Flavor-test results, youth use and seller enforcement: Asked how many submissions had failed testing, Aquino estimated that about 20 flavors had been rejected from more than 200 submissions, mainly for high formaldehyde levels; he said the 20 rejected flavors came from one company. He said the official lacked per-brand or per-company detail and committed to provide the data later. Aquino identified 17 registered brands. Cayetano said she would submit names of companies she found operating and obtain samples to check whether they were registered. Cayetano cited reported vaping among ages 10 to 19 rising from 3% in 2015 to 39% in 2023. Aquino said DTI could close unregistered shops and had issued notices of violation and formal charges in some cases.

Evidence for adult cessation benefits and youth appeal of flavors: Cayetano cited the U.S. FDA's rejection of 55,000 flavors and a later total of about one million, arguing that manufacturers should have to show that flavors help adult smokers quit enough to outweigh the risk of attracting youth. Aquino said DTI could reject flavors marketed to or attractive to minors under the existing law. Cayetano argued that the youth-targeting restriction did not address whether other flavors should be allowed without evidence that their adult cessation benefits outweigh youth risks; she described the original statutory wording as limited to unflavored or tobacco flavors, with menthol later allowed. No change to the rule was decided in this exchange.

Philippine Space Agency (PhilSA)

Senator Imee R. Marcos: Sovereign satellite data and Philippine space applications: Marcos questioned why the Philippines needed its own satellite capability when U.S. National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) imagery might be available free and the country already paid for a remote-sensing facility she said was rarely used. Aquino said relying on foreign satellites and companies limited Philippine control and required payment. He described the Philippine Space Agency (PhilSA) law as directing the country to develop local infrastructure and expertise and launch satellites for weather, defense, agriculture, environmental monitoring, natural-resource management and disaster-risk reduction. Marcos questioned the added value; Aquino said space data could help farmers and government agencies. Aquino also described Carabao mangoes and other endemic foods as subjects of space-food research to test whether they could serve astronauts from other countries.

Cagayan launch platform exploration and site feasibility: Marcos asked about reports of a commercial launch site in Cagayan and whether islands would be taken over for PhilSA. Aquino said PhilSA welcomed engagement with SpaceX and Rocket Lab to explore a Philippine launch platform. He cited the Philippines' Pacific location and said the Cagayan Economic Zone Authority (CEZA) had relevant infrastructure and topography, including an airport and seaport, for a potential site. Marcos questioned the plan given the unfinished Lal-lo airport; Aquino said a functioning platform could generate rent for the country. The discussions were exploratory, not a completed agreement or site transfer.

Civilian-use assurances and Senate information request: Marcos questioned whether the proposed platform was a defense effort, citing SpaceX's role in Ukraine and its reported appearance in the Taiwan Strait, and urged a thorough investigation. Aquino characterized the effort as civilian and exploratory, said there was no official indication it would be used for warfare or defense, and agreed that such use should be checked. Marcos asked PhilSA to share further information with the Senate and noted that any treaty would require Senate ratification.
