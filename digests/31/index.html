At a glance

  • Agencies and officials: Rep. Alexandria "Queenie" Gonzales chaired the hearing. Secretary David M. Gomez presented for PCO and its attached agencies and corporations; the exchanges included IBC President Jose C. "Jimmy" Policarpio Jr., PTV General Manager Lino S. Cayetano, PCO Undersecretary Ramon A. Ilagan, PIA Assistant Director-General for External Affairs Kristine Venet Q. Andal, and DBM Assistant Director Lulu P. Vispo.
  • Budget fault lines: The proposed PCO family budget was about ₱2.62 billion. Members challenged IBC's zero subsidy, PTV's ₱138 million allocation against its ₱1.7 billion request, PIA's omitted ₱20 million temporary-office rental request, and the unfunded ₱128 million expansion of Bagong Pilipinas Studios.
  • Commitments and unresolved funding: Gomez promised a consolidated restoration submission, including IBC's roughly ₱50 million arbitration obligation. He assured members that Congress TV would move to PTV if needed after privatization, while the broader employee and program arrangements still depended on the final plan. PIA committed to initial work on its donated Region X office site if funding became available. Members pledged to pursue amendments; no restored appropriation was approved in this hearing.

Agency presentation

A smaller communications budget: Gomez presented a proposed ₱2.62 billion budget for the PCO family, including corporate subsidies, and about ₱2.48 billion excluding those subsidies. He described an expanding demand for accurate, accessible government information despite reduced resources. The agency allocations he outlined included ₱784 million for PCO proper, ₱697 million for PIA, ₱499 million for the Bureau of Broadcast Services, ₱240 million for RTVM, ₱157 million for the News and Information Bureau, and ₱80 million for the Bureau of Communication Services. PTV would receive ₱138 million for revitalization and digitalization; IBC would receive no subsidy because it remained classified for privatization. APO Production Unit would operate from corporate earnings.

Public information, media literacy and digital production: PCO's program combined presidential coverage and coordinated agency messaging with freedom of information, fact-checking workshops, regional and provincial communication, public radio, and government print and audiovisual production. Its priorities included media literacy, an anti-fake-news reporting desk with referrals to appropriate agencies, digital platforms and audience analytics, and additional Bagong Pilipinas Studios. Gomez also proposed energy-saving equipment and transport measures. He framed these activities around simpler internal processes, decisions informed by data, measurable accountability, and stronger staff capabilities.

Utilization, staffing and performance measures: Gomez reported that PCO proper had obligated 87.51% of released appropriations in 2025 and disbursed 84.79% of obligations, with procurement and leadership transitions contributing to delays. As of August 15, 2026, he reported 36.72% obligation and 83.49% disbursement rates after the return of funds, citing energy-emergency austerity and reduced ASEAN activities. He committed to faster procurement and a second-semester catch-up plan. Although PCO reported exceeding physical targets, it was revising indicators to better capture digital reach, engagement and public feedback. The presentation also reported staffing vacancies and a fourth consecutive unmodified COA opinion on PCO's financial statements.

PCO proper's operating constraints: The proposed ₱784 million for PCO proper comprised approximately ₱289 million in personnel services and ₱495 million in operating expenses, with no capital outlay. Gomez said DBM had omitted proposed Tier 2 projects, including activities already funded in 2026, while personnel requirements included newly approved contractual positions. Most of the proposed funding was agency-specific; a small share covered automatic retirement and life-insurance contributions.

02

Rep. Roberto Nazal

Why PIA increased while other offices were cut: Nazal asked why the department's proposed allocation fell while PIA's increased. Gomez attributed part of PCO proper's reduction to the end of the Philippines' ASEAN hosting year, and said the major reason for PIA's increase was retrofitting its deteriorated, 44-year-old building. On vacant leadership and other critical posts, he said some appointments were being processed and others were covered by an optimization plan submitted to DBM.

Keep IBC operating until a sale can be completed: Nazal questioned IBC's zero subsidy and proposed a transition fund. Gomez agreed: the station's privatization plan, DBP valuation and GCG bidding timetable still had to be settled, making completion before year-end uncertain. Policarpio described improvements to facilities, payments to retirees, roughly ₱5 million a month from lottery coverage, and a remaining arbitration obligation of about ₱50 million. He said income remained insufficient and that IBC had reduced its request from more than ₱600 million to around ₱480 million, concentrating on salaries, operations and Congress TV. The exchange supported interim funding without abandoning the privatization plan.

PTV reach and information people can use: Cayetano sought restoration of PTV's ₱1.7 billion request, contrasting the ₱138 million allocation with his estimate that a competitive broadcaster would require ₱5–7 billion. He reported reaching about 30% of households; equipment repairs could bring that to around 42%, with three additional towers potentially taking it above 50%. He also stressed digital content rather than treating towers as the only route to audiences. Nazal urged clearer explanations of services such as YAKAP and the P3 lending program, including where and how people could obtain assistance. Ilagan said PCO coordinated agency messaging and used PIA's local-language networks to reach communities.

03

Rep. Ma. Victoria Co-Pilar

Protect employees and preserve Congress TV through privatization: Co-Pilar urged a transitional IBC budget to preserve operations and the station's value pending privatization. She asked whether PTV would absorb IBC employees and programs, particularly Congress TV. Gomez initially said the outcome depended on GCG guidelines and whether the buyer took over employees, assets and programming together; he offered to share the finalized plan with the committee. He then gave a specific assurance that Congress TV would move to PTV if needed, and pledged to find ways to protect employees who were not absorbed. This did not settle a general transfer arrangement for all staff or programs.

Rep. Brian Poe

IBC's request covers operations and a legal obligation: Poe challenged the drop from a ₱122 million current program to zero and asked about IBC's approximately ₱479 million additional funding request. Gomez said privatization might not be completed by December 31 and appealed for funds to sustain salaries and operations. Rep. Ziaur-Rahman "Zia" Alonto Adiong

Efficiency alone cannot deliver every planned expansion: Adiong asked how a reduced budget could support digital modernization and rising demand for accurate information. Gomez proposed streamlining processes and making better use of existing resources. Chair Gonzales pressed him on whether the allocation was adequate; he said PCO could remain effective but could do substantially more with additional resources. In response to her questions about interrupted projects, he identified an unfunded ₱128 million request for approximately three to five additional Bagong Pilipinas Studios using existing agency premises. He described the studios as production and editing facilities available to government media and private content creators.

Restoration request deferred to the amendment process: Adiong highlighted IBC's roughly ₱50 million arbitration obligation and the omitted temporary-office costs during PIA rehabilitation. Rep. Antonino B. Roman III

IBC's service expansion and employees at risk: Roman asked Policarpio to explain IBC's progress and the consequences of losing funding. Policarpio described a larger station network, 23 internally produced programs, government and congressional coverage, and payments to more than 350 retirees. He reported approximately ₱89 million in annual savings from retirements and a workforce of 456 including contract-of-service personnel. The remaining roughly ₱50 million arbitration obligation was included in IBC's request and, he said, could not be paid without funding. Roman backed continued support for IBC and also urged attention to PIA's poor working environment.

02:10:21 · asr-00330202:10:46 · asr-00331202:16:14 · asr-00346202:18:31 · asr-003503

DBM explains zero funding; the arbitration award goes into the restoration submission: In his additional interpellation after the suspension, Roman questioned removing IBC's subsidy before privatization was complete. Gomez said PCO was working toward an open bidding before year-end but acknowledged the risk of delays and agreed that the funding removal was premature. Vispo explained that DBM continued to treat IBC as a corporation under privatization. She said the arbitration award arose after budget preparation and that DBM had not received the award or a funding request for it. She cited prior support from the Pension and Gratuity Fund and said an amendment was for Congress to consider. Roman insisted that government should meet its legal obligations. Following Gonzales's questions about a remedy, Gomez committed to include the arbitration obligation in PCO's consolidated request for omitted or reduced funding. No payment authorization or restored allocation was issued during this exchange.

Closing decisions

PCO hearing terminated; committee proceedings suspended: On Rep. Julienne "Jam" L. Baronda's motion, seconded by Yamsuan, the committee terminated the hearing on the proposed FY2027 PCO budget and its attached agencies and corporations without objection. It then approved suspension of the committee hearing at Andaya Hall on House Bill No. 10858 until September 1, 2026 at 9 a.m. These rulings closed the hearing and set the committee's resumption; they did not themselves enact the requested budget restorations.
