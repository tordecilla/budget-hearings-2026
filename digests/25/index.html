At a glance

  • Funding and capability gaps: DND presented a proposed budget of 324.632 billion and cited 50 billion for modernization. Teodoro said modernization funding was committed to existing obligations, while reduced capital outlay constrained the bases, logistics and support systems needed for incoming equipment.
  • Pensions and veterans' care: Members questioned the sustainability of military and uniformed-personnel pensions and the absence of contributions. Teodoro called for an AFP-specific, actuarially sustainable approach while describing planned veterans' clinics, hospice care and hospital rehabilitation, with staffing and construction obstacles still unresolved.
  • Defense partnerships and accountability: Members scrutinized alliance risks, EDCA and Balikatan, local defense production, drones and foreign influence. Teodoro defended partnerships and deterrence, limited disclosure of sensitive systems and spending, and disputed civilian-status allegations concerning Toboso. An after-action report was promised, not presented.
  • Disaster readiness and local access: Questions covered delayed relief airlift, evacuation sites, earthquakes, flood control, military reservations and damaged bridges. Teodoro emphasized pre-positioned logistics, resilient infrastructure and interagency coordination; several local requests remained under review rather than approved projects.
  • Follow-ups and the briefing's outcome: DND undertook to submit a prioritized restoration letter within the week for the Budget Amendments Review Subcommittee. Other follow-ups included project and spending reports, coordination on military land and DSWD-supported youth-engagement guidance. Bella Suansing presided over termination of the briefing; Mika Suansing's later intervention sought restorations subject to review and fiscal space.

Agency presentation

Adaptive defense and territorial presence: Secretary Teodoro said rapid technological change makes the 15-year modernization framework difficult to keep current, and urged a more adaptive, flexible and operationally secure system. He described sovereignty and territorial integrity as a new mission area: DND must maintain overwatch of a stated 2.4 million-square-kilometer maritime domain, protect Philippine jurisdiction over exploration and exploitation, and build logistics and strategic bases for the wider Pacific and Philippine Rise as well as the West Philippine Sea. He also said internal peace, a continued presence in islands and BARMM election-security support remain necessary foundations for external defense.

Veterans' welfare and health care: Teodoro presented veterans' welfare as a core priority: the President had approved the first stage of a Veterans Memorial Medical Center annex so patients can be moved while the more-than-70-year-old legacy building is rehabilitated, and DND was pursuing Valor Clinics nearer to veterans. He said staffing the clinics with competent, security-vetted personnel was a constraint, but expected three to five to be operating by year-end; the department was also linking preventive, mental-health and geriatric care for veterans and active-duty personnel.

Modernization built around systems, infrastructure and reserves: The Secretary said modernization must start with core systems rather than visible hardware alone. He said DND was seeking larger rotary and palletized airlift capacity, modern corvettes and frigates, greater Army mobility and nationwide storage and access infrastructure; he also sought an accounting of BCDA proceeds and said the AFP, as trust beneficiary, should receive what is due.
