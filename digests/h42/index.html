At a glance

  • Staffing and fiscal autonomy: Members pressed for the organization of newly created courts and the filling of judicial vacancies. Officials explained the staffing-action process and discussed implementing fiscal autonomy, with requests for court and staffing details to follow.
  • Access and protection: Members asked how legal aid reaches indigent and remote communities and sought beneficiary and regional data. They also raised Sharia court access, threats to judges, and safe travel; officials described staffing and training needs, promised reports, and ongoing Philippine Coast Guard (PCG) transport talks.
  • Digitization and court operations: The Judiciary described a broad set of digital court services and infrastructure. Members connected case-flow tools and electronic procedures to timely hearings, access to evidence and service to litigants.
  • Augmentation submissions and hearing close: Members voiced support for the Judiciary budget, including requests for measurable performance and accountability. Committee Chairperson Mikaela Angela B. Suansing invited four courts to submit augmentation items by Wednesday before lunch for public line-by-line review, subject to fiscal space. The committee then terminated the hearing by motion.

Opening priorities

138 unorganized courts, reforms and resource needs: Vice Chair Rufus B. Rodriguez presided over the opening remarks. He said that, as of August 31, 2026, 138 newly created first- and second-level courts remained unorganized and non-operational pending notices of organization, staffing and compensation (NOSCA).
