At a glance

  • Drought readiness and water access: Subcommittee chair Senator Risa Hontiveros opened with the effects of intensifying El Niño drought on livelihoods, health and reliable water, urging the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) to prioritize preparation and response. Her questions examined water projects, restrictions, district support and drought-area targeting. Officials described current programs and future work, but a distinct costed total for Super El Niño response remained pending while related budget items overlapped; some project locations and final beneficiaries also remained to be identified.
  • Environmental planning and contested enforcement: Senators Alan Peter S. Cayetano and Pia S. Cayetano raised environmental education, local differences in climate and ecosystem needs, and flood planning around Laguna Lake. Questions to the Laguna Lake Development Authority (LLDA) tested whether enforcement and permit requirements were applied consistently and how LLDA's role intersected with the Philippine Reclamation Authority (PRA). Officials described their jurisdiction and project processes, while accounts of approvals, consultation and project history remained disputed or qualified; the hearing did not resolve the broader LLDA-PRA dispute.
  • Agency plans and outstanding follow-up: Officials addressed mining-related water risks, landfill fires, waste diversion and air-quality monitoring. The Mines and Geosciences Bureau promised to finalize its Homonhon water-source report that week; DENR promised the Manila Bay cumulative-impact report the following day, while transfer of data and modeling tools and staff training remained pending. The chair also requested written responses from LLDA and the Environmental Management Bureau before plenary.
  • Civil-society requests and plenary next steps: Bulacan Alliance for Reform asked for access to flood-model data and independent review; the People's Budget Coalition raised watershed, expressway and public-project accountability concerns; Mangrove Breakthrough sought research funding, a blue-carbon protocol and budget accountability measures. Hontiveros welcomed continued engagement and possible amendments, said civil-society input would inform the budget's transformation, and endorsed DENR's budget for plenary. The groups' proposed measures remained for consideration.

Opening drought-readiness priorities and public engagement: Opening the hearing, Senator Risa Hontiveros warned that intensifying El Nino drought would affect livelihoods, health and daily life, and said residents were already reporting unreliable or unsafe tap water.
