National budget sponsorship: Rep. Mikaela Angela B. Suansing said the proposed FY 2027 budget was framed amid slower growth, inflation, rising debt obligations, geopolitical conflict, volatile fuel prices, and climate risks that constrained fiscal space. She cited DBCC assumptions of 5–6% GDP growth, 4–5% inflation, a fiscal deficit of 5.1% of GDP, and a debt-to-GDP ratio of 64.6%. She said the House was coordinating with the Senate to open bicameral conference proceedings to the public for a second year, framing transparency, accountability, and participation as continuing budget-process reforms.

Suansing urged the House to treat the power of the purse as a constitutional duty and to make each allocation serve the public. She sponsored the proposed FY 2027 General Appropriations Bill and sought colleagues' support for passage; this was a request, with no vote or appropriation disposition recorded in this unit.

Rep. Roberto Nazal: Rep. Roberto Nazal questioned replacing the long-standing prohibition on impoundment with Section 78, which permits suspension of expenditure when public interest requires. Rep. Mikaela Angela B. Suansing said the provision reiterates Section 38 of Executive Order No. 292 and changes terminology for consistency, adding that it confers no additional executive power. Asked who introduced the change in the Bicameral Conference Committee, she deferred an answer while checking the records; she later said the Department of Budget and Management was willing to leave removal of the provision to Congress, but no House decision was recorded. Nazal challenged the undefined public-interest standard. Suansing acknowledged that the Supreme Court had described it as vague and left its application to the President, and said the Section 78 provision had not been invoked in 2026. She initially distinguished suspension from withholding an initial allotment release. She then explained that General Provision No. 3 makes the General Appropriations Act the allotment authority, subject to specified release conditions, including items with release requirements, lump sums, and special-purpose funds lacking release details. For FISARO items, Suansing said Section 6 of the 2026 veto message could allow non-release if cash-programming, prudent-fiscal-management, or budget-execution conditions were unmet; she also said that section could apply to new or increased congressional items. On savings, she said a project suspended under Section 78 is not thereby abandoned or discontinued, and that savings require a released allotment followed by completion, final discontinuance, abandonment, or efficiencies. Nazal disputed her reading of Araulio, arguing that the ruling bars suspension before funds are released and allotted. Suansing read it as barring declaration of savings before release, distinguishing that from Section 78 suspension of unreleased funds. Their interpretations remained in conflict when the chair ended Nazal's time.
