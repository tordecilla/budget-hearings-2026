At a glance

  • Budget priorities and execution: Members examined how the proposed resources of the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA) would support scholarships, training programs, and delivery. Questions about utilization, delayed implementation, and funding needs shaped the budget discussion.
  • Scholarship reach and outcomes: Members questioned who benefits from scholarships, what learners pay, whether training leads to credentials and work, and how the agency can improve targeting and employment reporting. Officials described scholarship categories, learner support, qualification pathways, and digital tools.
  • Audit and accountability: Senator Erwin Tulfo pressed TESDA and Commission on Audit (COA) officials about disbursement, accounting errors, scholarship eligibility and allowances, and project delays. COA said validation was ongoing; Tulfo opposed a budget increase until the questions were answered.
  • Local training capacity: Senator Juan Miguel "Migz" F. Zubiri sought a durable plan for Bukidnon training centers, including programs, equipment, and trainer needs. TESDA described local planning and repairs; Zubiri offered support after receiving a consolidated needs report, with no allocation adopted in the exchange.
  • Skills for changing work: Members raised concerns about AI disruption, business process outsourcing, semiconductors, and enterprise-based training. TESDA described training pathways and implementation plans while identifying funding and registration barriers; requests and suggestions remained distinct from approved appropriations.
  • Lifelong learning and qualifications: TESDA described using existing agency budgets for early lifelong-learning activities without a dedicated line item. Officials distinguished Philippine Qualifications Framework (PQF) referencing work from lifelong-learning leadership and committed to update the original ASEAN Qualifications Referencing Framework (AQRF) report with new level descriptors by December 2026.
  • Committee action: Members took different positions on the budget: some supported it or offered conditional help, while Tulfo opposed an increase pending answers. The FY2027 proposal was referred to plenary subject to further discussion and requested documents; the committee suspended the briefing.

TVET ecosystem, workforce demand, and training capacity: Benitez framed technical and vocational education and training (TVET) as a tool for workforce preparation amid changing skills needs, technology, and artificial intelligence (AI). TESDA said it was balancing training capacity between areas with jobs and higher-poverty areas, redirecting scholarships toward priority skills, and expanding online and blended delivery; its online bookkeeping course for Sangguniang Kabataan had about 40,000 completers and was delivered without added cost, with 38,600 completions reported through self-paced MOOCs.
