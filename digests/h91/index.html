Education and public-sector compensation proposals

Teacher pay and public-sector PERA: ₱50,000 monthly entry-level pay for public school teachers and a ₱5,000 monthly PERA for government employees were the targets sought by Tinio of ACT Teachers Party List. He said he had filed House Bill No. 203 for public-school teachers and separate measures for private-sector and higher-education teachers, but Congress had not acted on them. He contrasted the requested teacher salary with a stated ₱31,705 monthly public-school teacher salary and police and military compensation he described as more than ₱49,000, including at least ₱31,000 basic pay and a ₱10,000 monthly subsistence allowance. He also said teachers' debt payments can leave some with only ₱5,000 take-home pay.

Tinio said PERA remained ₱2,000 monthly and that the 2027 proposed increase was ₱500, to ₱2,500. He suggested realigning ₱62.6 billion he described as presidential local-government “pork” as one possible source. He contrasted this with ₱40 billion in the FY2026 General Appropriations Act (GAA) for military and police subsistence allowances. He cited a petition with 320,000 signatures supporting the salary and PERA requests. These were Tinio's proposals and characterizations; this speech records no adoption or appropriation decision on them.

Department of Agriculture proposals

Crop development and investment priorities: Mendoza linked dedicated annual funding for palm-oil development to greater mill capacity, production and local value chains, and reliable markets for smallholders.

Near the close of the speech, he urged an increase to a high-value-crops item he described as having “600 million” for banana, mango and coconut. The precise line item remains unclear in the record, and no funding increase was adopted during the speech.

He urged higher funding for research, pest management, climate-resilient farming, planting technology, farmer training, post-harvest facilities, export promotion and market intelligence, with farmers, workers, industry, government and local communities helping set priorities.

Rep. Antonio L. Tinio

Teachers' rights and closing appeal: Tinio condemned police actions against teachers and union members during peaceful protests and training, citing incidents in Malacañang, Bicol and at University of the Philippines unions. He referred to a Philippine National Police (PNP) budget-hearing memorandum describing community-relations activities intended to discourage groups from joining anti-administration protests, and argued that these activities restricted constitutional rights. He closed by urging adequate education funding, higher teacher pay and benefits, respect for teachers' rights and an end to attacks. He also sought removal of the ARAL Program from teachers’ regular workload with proper compensation, and adequate funding for humane, inclusive teacher promotion.

Teachers’ rights and public spending priorities: Co said teachers had faced pressure on their rights and freedom to express their concerns, hardships and calls for better pay. 07

Deputy Speaker Raymond Democrito C. Mendoza

Fruit production risks and market access: Mendoza described joining the Department of Agriculture at Asia Fruit Logistica in Hong Kong in September, which he said marked the Philippines’ return after nearly a decade. With banana, durian, mango and dragon-fruit industry groups, the aim was to open export markets, build partnerships and learn what buyers wanted.

Mendoza said banana producers in Davao del Norte and other major growing areas faced pests, disease, climate stress and gaps in timely intervention. Lower yields reduce farmer income, raise consumer prices and threaten export markets; he described both dessert and cooking banana varieties as vulnerable to supply disruption.

He said mango growers faced cecid fly, locally called kurikong, which causes scab-like lesions and, if unchecked, could threaten as much as 80% of production. Reliance on chemical pesticides raises costs and affects yield and marketability. He called for stronger research, safer interventions and technical support to protect productivity, market access and consumer confidence.

Regional agricultural exports and competitiveness: Citing the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), Mendoza compared 2023 agricultural exports: approximately $48 billion for Indonesia, $43 billion for Thailand, $29 billion for Malaysia, $20 billion for Vietnam and less than $6 billion for the Philippines. He said Indonesia's exports were nearly nine times the Philippine level and Vietnam's almost four times, and argued that sustained investment had helped neighboring countries build research, export industries and global value chains.

Industry institutions and farmer outcomes: Mendoza called for institutions to support industry development, including a Philippine Palm Oil Development Authority and dedicated banana and mango research centers. He said they could provide science, coordination, technology and market intelligence, and turn development roadmaps into programs, investment, jobs and higher incomes. He said the President had promised to certify needed measures as urgent and looked forward to that action.

He framed agricultural success in terms of farmer earnings, Filipino jobs, added value and the country's ability to compete abroad. His closing appeal was for a Philippine fruit industry that builds domestic enterprises and gives farmers and families a better future. His speech was referred to the Committee on Rules on Vargas’s motion, which Emano approved without objection.

Budget-session extension

Senate consent for extended budget deliberations: Rep. Arnan C. Panaligan moved to consider House Resolution No. 1501, a request for Senate consent to extend the House plenary session to October 12, 2026, so the House could consider the FY2027 General Appropriations Bill on third and final reading. Presiding Deputy Speaker Yevgeny Vincente B. Emano heard no objection and approved consideration of the resolution.

After the Secretary-General read its title, Panaligan moved to adopt House Resolution No. 1501. Emano called for ayes and nays, declared that the ayes had it, and announced the resolution approved. Panaligan then moved its immediate transmission to the Senate; Emano heard no objection and approved that motion. The House adopted the request and authorized its transmission. The recorded outcome was adoption of the consent request, rather than Senate consent or final passage of the budget bill.
