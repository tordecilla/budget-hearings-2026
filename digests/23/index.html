At a glance

  • Captured source: The archive begins mid-answer during Rodriguez's interpellation. A DOST PowerPoint presentation occurred earlier, but Suansing's later description of it cannot reconstruct the missing presentation or its figures.
  • Budget and capital works: Members contrasted the proposed DOST budget and attached-agency reductions with project status. PAGASA cited already funded 2026 capital items, FNRI identified an unfinished Phase 4 and a ₱400 million request, and PNRI was described as having completed project phases. The captured hearing approved none of the requested restorations.
  • Grant policy: DBM defended placing ongoing agency R&D grants directly in agency budgets to speed implementation. Solidum and House members argued that fixed placement reduced flexibility for proposal-driven, cross-institutional, and emergency research. DBM differed and deferred to Congress; no re-centralization, safeguard text, compromise, or drafting commitment was adopted.
  • Research to public use: Across evidence kits, agriculture, fisheries, HANDA, and Science for Peace, answers emphasized pilots, coordination, regional implementation, end-user procurement, and continuing local capacity. They often did not supply requested adoption rates, quantified outcomes, funded scale, or delivery dates.
  • AI and data sovereignty: The AI exchanges described NAICRI's grant-funded status, ASTI's shared compute service, H200 installation, commercial data-center rental, regional ambitions, and a DOST data-sovereignty platform. They did not provide a complete funded 26-fold capacity plan, exact processor count, power-source answer, settled DOST-DICT division of responsibility, or implementation timetable.
  • Priority funding requests: Solidum identified ₱800 million as the next-year need for Virology Institute Phases 1 and 2 and a ₱309 million 2027 projection for graduate-stipend alignment. Suansing requested supporting tables and a prioritized institutional-amendment list. These were needs, projections, routing, and requests—not funding approvals, releases, delivery promises, or final restorations.
  • Support and outcome: Several members advocated support or increases for DOST, and Suansing said the department had partners who would advocate for its budget; no amount or appropriation was approved. Co's request for pre-plenary deliberations received only a discussion-and-coordination response. The hearing closed with Briones's termination and suspension motions and Amante's rulings, with proceedings set to resume August 25, 2026 at 9 a.m.

Bella Vanessa Suansing: agriculture, disaster tools, and peacebuilding

Project SARAI, climate-smart agriculture adoption, and El Niño irrigation technology: Suansing asked how Project SARAI—Smarter Approaches to Reinvigorate Agriculture as an Industry in the Philippines—worked, where it operated, and whether soil information guided crop selection. Secretary Solidum described a DOST-PCAARRD research program first implemented by UPLB and now operated regionally, integrating satellite remote sensing, GIS and crop mapping, weather and rainfall data, forecasting, pest and disease monitoring, water and nutrient management, mobile applications, and site-specific advisories. He said SARAI operated in all regions according to locally selected commodities, including urban farming in NCR; soil health and classification helped identify suitable crops. He also cited complementary technologies outside SARAI, including seaweed foliar fertilizer that could raise rice production by up to 30%, and distinguished Smart Aqua work under DOST-PCAARRD. Suansing then pressed DOST to link its completed technologies with the Department of Agriculture and other national programs. Solidum described coordination through PCAARRD governance, regional and provincial offices, and consortia, while acknowledging that utilization of developed technologies might not be 100%. In the context of El Niño, Suansing urged national integration of climate-smart technology. Solidum said pilots and promotion had not accelerated adoption fast enough for climate risks, so DOST was moving implementation to the regional level with DA, LGUs, cooperatives, and farmers. He said DOST and PCAARRD interventions had been proposed for the El Niño task force and described drip and furrow irrigation and a mobile solar-irrigation trailer developed with Nueva Ecija University of Science and Technology. The trailer could move between farms, draw surface or shallow-well water, and reduce diesel use. He expressed hope that LGUs would avail themselves of it; he did not give a funded rollout count, appropriation, adoption deadline, or verified national uptake rate.

HANDA disaster platform, LGU-controlled SOS reporting, and evacuation-route data: After noting DOST's smart flood-management work and the integrated water-resource and flood-mitigation strategy under the 2026 GAA, Suansing asked whether health-authority feedback from HANDA's emergency SOS function ran through LGUs or the DOH central office.
