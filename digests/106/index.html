At a glance

  • Decision: The House approved House Bill No. 10858, as amended, on second reading.
  • Reporting dispute: Members sought clearer disclosure of Budget Amendments and Review Subcommittee (BARC) changes and proponents. Sponsor Rep. Mikaela Angela B. Suansing cited Commission on Audit (COA) publication for House expenditure reports and defended the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) project format; the committee rejected the disclosure amendments.
  • Adopted amendments: The House adopted a sitio-electrification provision for the National Electrification Administration (NEA), changed Personnel Economic Relief Allowance (PERA) wording, and approved amendments involving DPWH, the Department of Health (DOH), and the Department of Transportation (DOTr).
  • Rejected and confirmed proposals: State Universities and Colleges (SUCs) and Legal Education Board (LEB) proposals, agriculture and transport-fuel subsidy proposals, and proposals on violence-against-women desks and occupational safety and health (OSH) were rejected. The sponsor confirmed Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) program additions.

FY 2027 General Appropriations Bill

Rep. Antonio L. Tinio: Rep. Antonio L. Tinio invoked Rule 9, Section 28D, arguing that the Budget Amendments and Review Subcommittee (BARC) had to issue a detailed report identifying all agency realignments and modifications, increases and decreases, and each legislative proponent; publish agency and district allocation reports; and distribute them to House members. He said the amendment period had begun without the separate report and that the Committee on Appropriations chair had indicated BARC would instead present only the amended General Appropriations Bill (GAB) text.

Tinio also challenged the lack of identified funding sources for lump-sum increases and the absence of details on where those additions would go.

After the session resumed, Majority Leader Rep. Manuel “Wowo” L. Fortes Jr. responded that BARC’s proceedings had been livestreamed, its approved amendments incorporated into the substitute text of House Bill No. 10858 under Committee Report 638, and the substitute bill emailed to members and posted on the House website. Fortes therefore argued the point of order was not well taken. Deputy Speaker Janette L. Garin ruled it not well taken on those grounds. Tinio maintained that the rule required a separate BARC report specifying each amendment and its additions and reductions, identifying proponents, publishing the report, and furnishing copies to members.

Deputy Speaker Janette L. Garin stated that the House was in the amendment period and recognized Rep. Mikaela Angela B. Suansing as sponsor of the FY 2027 General Appropriations Bill (GAB). Majority Leader Rep. Manuel “Wowo” L. Fortes Jr. moved, under Section 106 of the rules, to proceed with amendments by substitution because there were no committee amendments; the motion passed without objection. Fortes then moved to substitute the entire House Bill No. 10858 with the bill in Committee Report No. 638, saying copies of the substitute had been distributed. When Garin asked for objections, Rep. Antonio L. Tinio objected and was given five minutes to explain.

Tinio argued that members needed a detailed Budget Amendments and Review Subcommittee (BARC) report and that pointing to the livestream was insufficient. He said his review of the amendments showed a reported total of ₱116.32 billion in increases. He listed over ₱28 billion for the Department of Health (DOH) Medical Assistance to Indigent and Financially Incapacitated Patients (MAIFIP), ₱26 billion for the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) Assistance to Individuals in Crisis Situations (AICS), ₱5.11 billion for the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) TUPAD program, ₱4.09 billion for the Local Government Support Fund (LGSF), and ₱1.44 billion for the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), mainly for scholarships. He grouped these as aid programs that, in his view, involved legislators' discretion in allocating assistance. He also cited a ₱11.49 billion increase to the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH), and said internal changes totaled ₱98.8 billion, including 6,262 individual projects absent from the National Expenditure Program (NEP) but included in the amended House version.

Tinio said the public and most members did not know these amendments because they had not been presented in a report. He argued the substitution would not meet other demands he listed: a substantial increase in the Personnel Economic Relief Allowance (PERA); funding the free higher education shortfall at state universities and colleges for 2026 and 2027; production subsidies for farmers and fishers; and more funding for education and public hospitals. Citing 7.2% inflation, he characterized discretionary aid and infrastructure allocations as 'pork-type' amendments and maintained his objection.

Garin put the motion to a vote, explaining that an aye sustained Fortes's substitution motion and a nay sustained Tinio's objection. The ayes prevailed, and Garin declared House Bill No. 10858 amended by substitution in its entirety.

Rep. Antonio L. Tinio said an earlier budget-deliberation commitment identified 11.78 billion pesos in the Miscellaneous Personnel Benefits Fund (MPBF) specifically for a Personnel Economic Relief Allowance (PERA) increase of 500 pesos per month; he called that increase insufficient amid 7.2% inflation. Section 63 still set PERA at 2,000 pesos per month. Tinio said he would have preferred 5,000 pesos, but while funding was being worked out he formally moved to insert 'at least,' proposing the wording, 'at least 2,000 pesos per month' to allow the Department of Budget and Management (DBM) and Malacanang flexibility to raise it.

Sponsor Rep. Mikaela Angela B. Suansing said 11.794 billion pesos out of the 58 billion peso MPBF was earmarked or targeted for increases in government-employee allowances. A 500-peso PERA increase was an initial possibility, but DBM was considering different disbursement options and the Executive had not decided the form. Suansing supported the amendment because the fixed amount in Section 63 would otherwise limit flexibility. The committee accepted the amendment; Majority Leader Rep. Manuel "Wowo" L. Fortes Jr. moved approval, and Deputy Speaker Janette L. Garin heard no objection and declared it accepted. The accepted wording does not set a fixed increase above the 2,000-peso minimum.

Elago proposed a new FY 2027 General Appropriations Bill (GAB) special provision appropriating ₱482,250,670,000 for a one-time production subsidy: ₱50,000 for each of more than six million farmers and ₱30,000 each for more than two million farm workers and more than two million fisherfolk. Sponsor Rep. Mikaela Angela B. She listed ₱10 billion for Presidential Assistance to Farmers and Fisherfolk, ₱500 million for expanded fertilizer assistance, ₱4 billion for irrigation, ₱150 million for a monorail or tram line for vegetable farmers, ₱4.96 billion for farm-to-market roads, ₱100 million for high-value crops, particularly ube, and ₱330 million for climate-resilient farm productivity support. Eligible vehicles would buy diesel at ₱55 per liter, with the subsidy taking effect when the actual average pump price exceeded ₱55 per liter. Suansing referred to the proposal as ₱599 billion, while Elago's proposal states ₱498.96 billion. Suansing said the National Expenditure Program (NEP) contained ₱0 for the transport-sector fuel subsidy and that BARC recommended adding ₱1 billion, saying that previously unused 2024 and 2025 funding had been used up during the Middle East fuel-price crisis.
