At a glance

  • Department and principal officials: Justice Secretary Fredderick A. Vida led the DOJ presentation, with Undersecretary Majken Anika S. Gran-Ong delivering the detailed budget briefing. Frequent respondents included NBI Director Melvin Matibag, BI Commissioner Joel Anthony M. Viado, BuCor Director General Gregorio Pio P. Catapang Jr., Solicitor General Darlene Marie B. Berberabe, PAO Chief Persida V. Rueda-Acosta, PPA Administrator Bienvenido E. Benitez Jr., LRA Administrator Gerardo P. Sirios, and OADR Executive Director Jose Maria Palaganas.
  • Budget fault line: The department presented a wide gap between its request and the NEP recommendation. Members repeatedly sought restoration for DOJ proper, NBI and BI systems, cybercrime, anti-trafficking and child-protection work, PAO and prosecution staffing, OSG digitalization, OADR, PPA training, and corrections, but expressions of support did not become committee appropriations during the briefing.
  • Prosecution and accountability: Questions distinguished DOJ's reported prosecution-success and disposition measures from conviction rates, exposed missing treatment of witness-related dismissals, and pressed for disaggregated graft, terrorism-financing, petition-for-review, and imprisonment data. Confidential-fund increases and liquidation were also scrutinized because the record did not reconcile all yearly components or explain the 2025 surge through public program outputs.
  • Protection and emerging threats: The hearing linked reduced anti-trafficking, cybercrime, and OSAEC funding to AI-enabled grooming, sextortion, online recruitment, rescue capacity, international cooperation, and staff workload. DOJ sought at least prior-year funding for several protection lines, while questions about religious profiling, blacklist safeguards, gender-sensitive investigations, responder support, and technology-facilitated violence produced principles and review promises more often than measurable coverage or outcomes.
  • Immigration and border systems: BI faced sustained scrutiny over e-Gate procurement and unobligated balances, the unsigned CAIS public-private proposal, passenger charges, control of traveler data, and claimed screening capabilities. Separate exchanges covered POGO enforcement, Agnès Callamard's unresolved Philippine restriction status, alleged false identity documents, and a proposed immigration-status roadmap; key figures, safeguards, documents, and final decisions remained pending.
  • Corrections, legal aid, and land records: BuCor described decongestion, regional transfers, new dormitories, discipline, and eventual NBP closure, while members sought family-proximity safeguards, capacity measures, transfer lists, and resolution of missing PDL funds. PAO and PPA cited severe workload and staffing needs, OSG sought building and digital investments, and LRA discussed delayed ancestral-domain, CLOA, free-patent, lost-title, and Friar Lands issues without settling several legal and funding questions.
  • Commitments and unresolved follow-through: Officials committed to provide selected utilization, vacancy, case, transfer, contract, and enforcement records; check particular immigration and land matters; pursue procurement and obligation milestones; and coordinate further hearings or consultations. Recurring limits were missing delivery dates, conditional DBM or GAA approval, incomplete arithmetic, deferred source records, plans still under review, and member support that was not binding committee action.
