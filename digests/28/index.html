At a glance

  • Budget priorities and stricter project checks: Secretary Vivencio Dizon proposed prioritizing unfinished works and maintenance, requiring plans, on-site geotagging and signed implementability certificates. He acknowledged gaps in master planning and offered to publish supporting documents.
  • Making delayed projects usable: Members questioned road and bridge backlogs, incomplete flood defenses and delayed classrooms. Dizon described catch-up targets, advocated multiyear continuity and sought more engineers; financing, approvals and implementing capacity still constrained delivery.
  • Allocation transparency and policy exceptions: Tiangco secured commitments for a nationwide legislative-district breakdown and line-item publication. Amatong and Tan sought continued funding for ongoing convergence projects; Dizon supported an exception if DBM and Congress agreed, without announcing restored appropriations.
  • Audit trails, fees and public safety: Ismula sought master-plan alignment and foreign-assisted project data; DPWH promised follow-up rather than supplying all figures. Cendaña pressed on blacklisting, labor rights, accessibility and construction safety, drawing candid acknowledgements of poor compliance and commitments to provide records and strengthen enforcement.
  • A continuing hearing: The morning ended with a suspension until 1 p.m., with Iris Montes announced as the next interpellator. Her questions and the later committee outcome are in Part 2.

Agency presentation

Proposed budget and principal programs: Dizon presented a PHP 643.95 billion proposed 2027 budget, roughly 20% higher, with about PHP 200 billion for national roads and bridges and PHP 118 billion for foreign-assisted projects. Locally funded flood mitigation, omitted in 2026, would return at PHP 83.84 billion; about PHP 19 billion in ongoing foreign-assisted flood projects was included in the foreign-assisted total. Basic infrastructure included PHP 105 billion for access roads and bridges, PHP 40.97 billion for multipurpose buildings, PHP 1.38 billion for coastal roads and PHP 419 million for local ports. He also identified PHP 510 million for facilities for persons with disabilities and older people, PHP 1 billion in quick-response funding, PHP 7.29 billion for water supply, and PHP 27.3 billion for right of way.

Project readiness, geotagging and signed accountability: Dizon said DPWH had dropped the previous parametric allocation formula in favor of completing unfinished projects, maintaining critical infrastructure and addressing immediate community needs. Proposed projects now required approved programs of work, on-site geotagged photographs, DBM compliance forms and signed certificates of implementability. Implementing offices had to certify right-of-way readiness, specific locations and absence of duplicate funding; continuing projects had to be identified as such. Dizon contrasted this with past submissions containing only titles and costs, and promised to publish supporting documents so Congress and the public could scrutinize them. Projects also needed a planning basis and coordination with development councils or, at minimum, LGU certificates of no objection.

00:09:54 · asr-00014200:13:20 · asr-000179

Fewer projects and revised regional allocations: Dizon described consolidating local projects under the Basic Infrastructure Program and removing separate convergence programs. Tourism, trade and other departments would hold their own priority infrastructure funds, with transfers and agreements if DPWH implemented the work. DPWH also sought to eliminate duplicates and overlapping projects, focus on larger projects and bring workloads closer to implementing offices' capacity; DBM had directed removal of Basic Infrastructure Program projects below PHP 5 million, subject to Congress's consideration. Dizon put the proposed geographic shares at roughly 52% for Luzon, 20% for Visayas and 28% for Mindanao, compared with ten-year averages of 56%, 17% and 27%. He highlighted higher allocations for poorer regions but said the distribution remained imperfect and invited congressional adjustment.

Flood mitigation grounded in plans and completed systems: Dizon emphasized flood mitigation rather than a promise to control all flooding. He acknowledged that many river-basin and drainage master plans were absent, unfinished or outdated, and sought more funding for studies and design. Priorities included closing gaps in unfinished defenses, approved designs, integrated water-resource management, coordination with local governments and actual on-site geotagging. Detention basins and dams could also store water for irrigation and supply. Investigations and COA clearance requirements complicated repairs to implicated projects, for which he sought congressional help. He cited alignment with plans in Quezon City, Metro Cebu and Iligan and continuing foreign-assisted projects, including Pasig-Marikina, ADB-supported river-basin projects and the JICA-supported Cavite project. Claims of reduced flooding in particular areas were his reported observations, not a claim that national flood risk had been eliminated.

Delayed procurement and the September award target: Dizon acknowledged serious 2026 procurement delays. He attributed them to the absence of early procurement after the budget overhaul, roughly PHP 460 billion in capital outlay being placed for later release, allotment releases arriving in late May and June, and stricter requirements for complete plans before bidding. As of August 23, 15,032 of 17,764 projects had been advertised, representing about PHP 349.5 billion or 76% of the capital-outlay value. Awards covered 7,467 projects worth PHP 146.1 billion, about 32% by value. He aimed to have at least 80% awarded by the end of September for starts in the fourth quarter, offered updated figures, and acknowledged that disbursements would remain low despite catch-up efforts.

00:44:57 · asr-00060500:49:51 · asr-000645

Repairing roads and aging bridges before they fail: Dizon sought funding to repair road slips and collapses occurring without a calamity, when quick-response funds could not be used. He cited a savings-funded repair that reopened Buda Road in less than two months and argued that isolated communities could not wait another budget year. He also called for drainage and safety barriers omitted from past roads. Many bridges were 40 to 50 years old or older and needed replacement or retrofitting; overloaded trucks compounded the risks. Savings and augmentation had enabled urgent bridge work, but he said these could not meet recurring nationwide needs.

00:52:20 · asr-00066400:57:45 · asr-000733

Dredging, safer crossings and unfinished public buildings: Dizon described savings-funded flood repairs and renewed river dredging and desilting after years of accumulated sediment. He reported anecdotal improvements in places including Tuguegarao and Maguindanao, while stressing the need to expand the work. He also sought a program to replace makeshift community crossings used by students with durable bridges. Finally, he prioritized finishing existing national and local public buildings, including schools, hospitals and multipurpose facilities, and asked Congress to help determine which unfinished projects should come first.

03

Minority Leader Marcelino C. Libanan

Repairing Maharlika Highway before further widening: Libanan asked whether the PHP 199.58 billion national-road allocation included making more of Maharlika Highway four lanes. Dizon said some widening remained, but he had directed offices to prioritize repairing damaged sections first, especially in areas including Northern Samar and Samar. Widening also required secured right of way to avoid disconnected stretches; it could proceed once existing roads were in order.

01:10:28 · asr-00091001:10:55 · asr-000917

Sorsogon-Samar bridge study, financing and timetable: Libanan sought progress on the Sorsogon-Samar link. Dizon corrected his initial funding statement: its feasibility study had PHP 130 million in the 2026 GAA, not PHP 120 million in the 2027 NEP, and was starting. He described a roughly 20-kilometer island-assisted route intended to avoid the deepest channel and reduce cost. He cited a 42% economic rate of return, compared with 33% for Bataan-Cavite, and agreed to check Libanan's suggestion to include tourism benefits. Libanan separately cited PHP 350 million for a Mindanao-Leyte bridge feasibility study. Dizon expected the Sorsogon-Samar study to finish in 2027; financing and final approval still had to be secured. He envisaged a six-year construction period under the next administration, rather than completion within the current term. Potential lenders had expressed interest, not committed financing. As a separate progress update, he said Bataan-Cavite's approaches were under construction and its main-span award was planned for September.

04

Rep. Tobias "Toby" M. Tiangco

Publishing allocations for every legislative district: Tiangco sought accessible publication of DPWH projects and allocations by legislative district, including party-list representatives, arguing that a 722-page NEP was insufficient for ordinary readers to compare unusually large allocations. He linked unusual allocation growth to ghost and substandard projects and said the promised 2026 district-allocation report had still not been published. Momo initially referred the issue to the appropriations chair but agreed that district summaries should be published. Dizon distinguished DPWH engineering districts from legislative districts and agreed to supply the latter breakdown, publish line items on the DPWH website and upload the NEP with supporting documents. Tiangco asked for the summary before 1 p.m.; Dizon said it could be earlier. At the exit, Tiangco corrected Momo's reference to Navotas alone: the request covered all legislative districts nationwide. The exchange records commitments, not confirmed publication or delivery.

Completing flood defenses through multiyear funding: Tiangco stressed that incomplete flood defenses may provide no protection even after most spending has occurred, and asked DPWH to explain project phases and expected completion clearly. Project-engineer accountability and workloads: Amatong complained about absent project engineers and dismissive responses to defects, citing a tribal hall and a delayed health center in Gutalac. Dizon agreed that reforms must reach project engineers and nonperformers should be replaced. He also described a structural shortage: engineers could supervise 30 projects or more, against an initial stated maximum of seven. He sought additional DBM positions and fewer projects per office. Amatong rejected workload as an explanation for dismissing an obvious defect; Dizon said he was not excusing that individual case.

Keeping continuing hospital projects funded: Amatong appealed against removing hospital funding from DPWH's convergence programs, saying his district's urgently needed hospital had funding in 2026 but had not progressed and was absent from DPWH's next proposal. Dizon explained that most hospital funding already sat with DOH and DBM had directed the remaining DPWH health-facility items there. He supported seeking an exception for continuing projects, rather than new ones, and asked the committee to help obtain DBM's agreement. The policy was presently outside DPWH's control; no restored appropriation was approved.

Rep. Dadah Kiram Ismula

Which flood plans exist and whether projects follow them: Ismula sought the inventory, age and implementation status of flood-mitigation master plans. Project Director Jerry Fano said plans had been prepared for all 18 major river basins, while smaller-basin work continued through regional and district offices. He cited five-year updating guidance from JICA and three-year guidance from DEPDev in light of climate change. Ismula requested direct copies because the website was difficult to use, identification of outdated or incomplete plans, and a table distinguishing projects aligned with plans from those not aligned. Dizon said works must follow available plans and described required coordination between DPWH and city engineers to connect drainage systems. He agreed to supply the requested material but later qualified that mapping projects against master plans would take longer than the same-day ODA information.

Foreign-assisted project delays and commitment fees: Dizon reported 50 priority ODA projects: seven completed, 26 under construction and the remainder in feasibility-study or detailed-engineering stages. He offered the list and said most, if not all, were delayed. Ismula cited DOF's reported PHP 3.4 billion in commitment fees since 2022 and asked for project-specific fees, delays, penalties and projects under unprogrammed appropriations. He offered some ODA information within the day, and Momo directed that the submissions pass through the committee. Ismula stressed the opportunity cost for health infrastructure; no project-level fee table or penalty total was supplied during the round.

Rep. Jose "Bong" J. Teves Jr.

Classroom delays and the catch-up schedule: Teves questioned low completion under DepEd's Basic Education Facilities Fund, citing July 15 figures of 76 of 261 classrooms in Region III, 48 of 297 in Region IV-A and 49 against a 280-classroom allocation in Region V. Dizon accepted the 2025 data and said DPWH aimed to finish all 5,000 classrooms funded by the PHP 13 billion 2025 allocation before that funding expired at the end of 2026. He attributed delays to the late DepEd-DPWH agreement, followed by site validation and bidding. The 2026 agreement had been signed in April rather than the previous August schedule; procurement was underway in August, with construction hoped to begin in September. These remained completion and start targets.

Poor accessibility compliance and dedicated funding: Cendaña sought DPWH's assessment and project-level data on Accessibility Law compliance. Dizon acknowledged very poor compliance nationwide, citing missing or inadequate sidewalks and unsafe access. He described improvements during EDSA rehabilitation and instructions for ramps and sufficiently wide sidewalks, while acknowledging that full compliance remained distant. Enforcing construction-site road safety: Cendaña pressed DPWH on fatal road crashes and commuter safety.
