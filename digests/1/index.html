01

The DBCC briefing: monetary stability, growth, fiscal space, and spending priorities

BSP Deputy Governor Zeno Ronald R. Abenoja reported that inflation rose from 2.4% in February 2026 to 7.2% in April before easing to 6.2% in July. He said the BSP raised its policy rate from 4.25% through two 25-basis-point increases, bringing it to 4.75%, and presented inflation forecasts of 6.4% for 2026 and 4.5% for 2027 while keeping the 3% medium-term target.

Tinio: compensation, NEP integrity, and flood-control priorities

Rep. Antonio L. Tinio asked when civilian employees could expect another salary increase after the fourth tranche and separately asked whether the P2,000 monthly PERA could rise in the 2027 budget. DBM said a compensation study would determine the timing and amount of any salary adjustment, with results expected in the second half of the following year, while a PERA study targeted a recommendation before year-end and possible accommodation in 2027 through the Miscellaneous Personnel Benefits Fund. No increase was committed in this exchange.

Tinio argued that recurring differences between large NEP proposals and materially smaller GAA amounts could enable insertions, citing foreign-assisted DOTR examples of P135 billion in the NEP versus P64 billion in the GAA. DBM responded that its NEP amounts reflected evaluated requirements, were below the agency proposal in the cited case, and that later reductions could delay foreign-assisted implementation; DBM said the submitted NEP was already with Congress and did not identify a further IRATA submission in this record.

Recto: underspending, revenues, and inflation risks

Rep. Ryan S. Recto asked DBM how the government would prevent underspending while capital outlays were proposed to rise 13.8%, noting a 2.3% second-quarter 2026 economic slowdown and his calculation that resources shifted from GOCCs to DPWH represented 27.33%. DBM answered that infrastructure projects in the 2027 NEP had undergone documentary-completeness scrutiny, allowing agencies to begin early procurement before enactment and proceed with award and implementation once the budget passed. The exchange established a proposed mechanism, not proof of execution.

03:22:58 · asr-002895-split-203:23:19 · asr-00289603:23:49 · asr-00289703:24:26 · asr-00289903:24:51 · asr-002903

Recto questioned optimistic revenue projections through 2030 while a pending tax bill could raise personal-income-tax thresholds, exempt minimum corporate income tax, and alter excise taxes. He asked whether unresolved rates and the roughly P85-billion revenue-loss context had been included in projections. DOF Secretary Frederick Go said the proposed revenue-enhancing and revenue-eroding measures were not included, and that collections would depend on how the legislature finalized the bill after deliberations.

Recto asked whether BSP's declining inflation targets were too optimistic given underspending, the unresolved Middle East crisis, global trade uncertainty, and volatile capital flows. BSP Deputy Governor Zeno Ronald R. Abenoja explained that 6.4% for 2026, 4.5% for 2027, and 3.1% for 2028 were forecasts from the prior Monetary Policy Meeting, while the 3% target plus or minus 1% remained unchanged; he identified oil prices, El Niño, and the lagged effects of monetary policy as risks and said the forecast would be updated at the next Monetary Board meeting.
