At a glance

  • Budget frame: The Chair opened with PHP 10.55 billion for DTI, its attached agencies, and corporations, PHP 1.36 billion below the FY 2026 GAA. Later discussion used other DTI budget figures; the session did not reconcile those framings program by program.
  • What DTI put forward: Secretary Roque presented foreign trade and investment, consumer protection and fair trade, and MSME development as DTI's core work, alongside Tatak Pinoy, investment facilitation, enterprise finance, product safety, and priority industries.
  • Central budget dispute: Members repeatedly asked what reductions would mean for market access, standards enforcement, industry development, automotive incentives, cooperative support, regional offices, creative industries, and economic-zone operations. DTI commonly described constrained fiscal space, DBM decisions, or a need for House support; no revised appropriation was decided in the briefing.
  • Industry and investment scrutiny: Pax Silica, New Clark City utilities and safeguards, local-vehicle participation, CARS and RACE, semiconductor and advanced-manufacturing plans, and prospective investments were examined. Several descriptions remained prospective, and members sought documents, consultation information, policy details, or project-specific follow-up.
  • Trade and consumer concerns: The hearing covered export promotion, halal certification, bamboo procurement, U.S. tariffs, product standards, substandard goods, and consumer enforcement. DTI described market-diversification and enforcement work but left requested data, compliance findings, and some legal or program outcomes for follow-up.
  • Attached bodies and regional delivery: CDA, APECO, SB Corporation, BOI, PEZA, and DTI's regional and bureau functions were questioned on staffing, financing, infrastructure, investment facilitation, and local-program delivery. Reported requests and plans were not equivalent to approved funding or completed projects.
  • Committee follow-up: At closing, Chair Suansing asked DTI to submit priority programs and projects, ideally that week, for BARC and the Committee on Appropriations to consider for potential restoration. The Chair then accepted a motion terminating the briefing; the record contains no delivered priority list, restoration amount, or appropriation decision.
