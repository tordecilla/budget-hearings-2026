At a glance

  • FY 2027 CSC request and NEP: CSC sought resources for service modernization and identified unfunded facilities, vehicles and backup-energy requirements. Members pressed on workforce protections, benefits and administrative-case resolution.
  • Contractual work and vacancies: Members pressed CSC on long-serving job-order and contract-of-service workers, unfilled plantilla posts and the division of responsibility with DBM. CSC described preferential rating, agency-level inventories and possible data-system improvements, but said it could not guarantee absorption into plantilla positions and that broader reforms would require action beyond CSC.
  • Labor representation and employee benefits: The discussion covered public-sector labor representation in the PSLMC, proposals for an independent labor-relations body, solo-parent leave for JO/COS workers, a possible increase in the P2,000 PERA, and emergency and wellness leave. CSC expressed support for some policy goals while distinguishing matters under its control from DBM funding and legislative decisions. 01:24:37
  • Digital safeguards and case handling: Members sought clearer cybersecurity funding, reliable online SALN identity checks and data-center procurement safeguards. CSC said it would later provide a cybersecurity breakdown, described identity verification and its DICT partnership, and said the initial data-center bidding failed over confidentiality safeguards. Separate exchanges pressed CSC for inspection and case-status information, faster case resolution and digital case-management tools.
  • Outstanding requests and hearing outcome: CSC undertook to furnish several reports or follow-ups, including CODI inspection information, status information on leave policies, cybersecurity funding details, and checks on specified pending cases. The committee terminated the CSC budget deliberations and suspended the House Bill No. 10858 hearing at Noglares Hall until September 2, 2026, at 9 a.m.

Civil Service Commission presentation

Workforce scale and CSC staffing: FY 2027 CSC proposal to DBM: PHP 4.8 billion; DBM-approved NEP ceiling: PHP 4.04 billion for the same commission. CSC Chairperson Marilyn B. Yap framed the proposed 2027 budget around CSC's national mandate. She reported a civil-service workforce of more than 2.25 million as of June 2026—2.02 million career and more than 232,770 non-career professionals—and more than one million job-order and contract-of-service workers, for a government workforce above 3.29 million. She said 73.6% of career professionals were in national government agencies, while 60.6% of non-career professionals and most JO/CO workers were in local government units. Against that scope, she reported CSC's 1,690-position allocation, 1,541 filled positions, 149 vacancies, and one CSC second-level-and-up employee for every 3,199 government workers.

Seven-day solo-parent leave for JO and COS workers: Nazal asked why the seven-day solo-parent leave for qualified job-order (JO) and contract-of-service (COS) workers was not expressly included in the 2025 CSC-COA-DBM joint circular on ancillary benefits, citing Republic Act 11861 and its implementing rules. She said CSC had found a 2004 circular allowing seven days of solo-parent leave, had circulated it to departments, and was in dialogue with DSWD, which she identified as the Act's primary implementing agency, to clarify the entitlement. Nazal said departments remained reluctant to implement it because of audit or disallowance concerns. Yap said the joint circular already involved DBM and COA and addressed those concerns, but agreed that its relevant provision could be reiterated.

00:51:02 · asr-000465

Pathways from JO/COS work to vacant plantilla positions: Nazal asked for a government-wide plan, targets and timetable to match qualified JO/COS workers to unfilled plantilla positions, assist those without eligibility, and reduce reliance on contractual work; he said CSC's inventory counted 919,868 JO and COS workers. Yap said applicant and passer numbers had increased, but CSC did not have data to establish that the measure had caused JO/COS workers to be absorbed into plantilla posts.
