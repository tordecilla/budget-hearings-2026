Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG)

Rep. Sarah Jane I. Elago: Continuing her discussion of public-market fees and vendor protections from Part 1, Rep. Sarah Jane I. Elago said failed privatization or joint-venture arrangements put some public markets at risk of closure, while large retailers could displace small vendors and vendors faced insecure tenure and limited participation in market policy. She asked for regional or national consultations, or another mechanism to hear vendors. DILG sponsor Rep. Arnie B. Fuentebella replied that consultations already occurred and cited DILG Joint Memorandum Circular No. 2019-01, which guides local government units (LGUs) in setting just, reasonable, standardized regulatory fees and service charges.

Elago requested the latest DILG report and urged a vendor-focused initiative alongside Paleng QRPH, which helps public markets offer cashless payment options. She called for national consultation with LGUs and market vendors, citing struggling public markets and calls for national support. Fuentebella agreed, but gave no consultation schedule or specific follow-up commitment in this exchange.

Elago asked whether the cashless system under BJMP Memorandum Circular No. 110 would be amended to align differing rules across institutions. Citing reports of missing PHP 5 to 6 million at the Bureau of Corrections (BuCor) and Correctional Institution for Women (CIW), she urged Congress to guard against missing funds and corruption and ensure persons deprived of liberty (PDLs) could access their funds without added hardship. She said per-PDL overhead costs were already very low, without giving an amount.

Fuentebella said a technical working group (TWG) was being formed, with BJMP, BuCor, and provincial jails discussing a common framework. Asked when amendments and consultations would conclude, he said consultative meetings were still underway and that legislation might be needed to unify the system, with advice from the jail institutions. He gave no completion date. Elago said she would continue monitoring the review for transparency and accountability.
