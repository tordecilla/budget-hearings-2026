At a glance

  • Budget gap: DHSUD presented a combined P5.498 billion NEP allocation against roughly P60 billion requested for the housing system. Members broadly supported increases, while DBM cited corporate income, available prior allotments and incomplete project documentation in explaining allocations.
  • Housing people can use: Questions centered on access for low-income households, the gap between completed inventory and loan takeout, vacant or flood-prone relocation sites, missing amenities and livelihoods, and water-service arrears. Officials described expanded rental, transitional and community mortgage options alongside 4PH financing.
  • Disaster assistance and unresolved cases: Members challenged exhausted funds and delayed releases for fire and disaster victims. Officials described prospective releases, while requests on estate-management funding, legal help for private land disputes and local dispute resolution remained unanswered or lacked a mechanism.
  • Commitments to follow: DHSUD undertook to provide proclamation status updates and an employee housing survey, coordinate with evicted families, and use remaining interest-subsidy and ISSP funds before year-end. Suansing requested ranked budget amendments within the week, including options for partial funding.
  • Outcome: The briefing was terminated after Alvarez moved and Suansing seconded. The closing motions did not approve the budget or the requested increases; committee proceedings were then suspended for resumption on August 26 at 9 a.m.

Agency presentation

A broader route to meeting housing need: Secretary Jose Ramon P. Aliling said the PSA's current estimate is 3.7 million housing needs by 2028, rather than the previously cited 6.9 million. He said the estimate includes homes that need repair, multi-family households, and households expected to form, so new construction alone is not the answer. DHSUD's proposed response combines the Community Mortgage Program (CMP), expanded SHFC support for repairs, site development and utilities, Pag-IBIG home-improvement loans, 4PH, and private-sector production.

Licensing and homebuyer complaints: Aliling said DHSUD had cleared overdue regulatory applications for 10 consecutive weeks, acting on each within 15 days through approval, denial, or a notice of deficiencies. Applicants given deficiencies have 90 days to comply and may seek a temporary license to sell while doing so. He also said the department had addressed the 3,000-case homebuyer-complaint backlog it inherited and would continue mass conciliations as new complaints are filed.

2027 budget gap across the housing system: Senior Undersecretary Henry L. Yap described DHSUD as the government's planning, policy, regulatory, coordination, and monitoring body for housing, human settlements, and urban development, working with production, financing, and adjudication agencies. He said the combined 2027 NEP allocation for DHSUD and key shelter agencies was P5.498 billion, P579 million below the 2026 GAA, against submissions of about P60 billion—a stated P54 billion shortfall. Delivery target and expanded housing options: Yap said the current administration had delivered 588,757 direct and indirect housing units or forms of assistance, with 544,000 still to undertake toward a 1.133-million target by the end of the administration.
