Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev)

Rep. Sarah Jane I. Elago: Rep. Sarah Jane I. Elago questioned the proposed economy's reliance on household consumption and the effect of VAT on purchasing power. She cited projected FY 2027 VAT collections of PHP860 billion and asked whether the budget implied an increase of PHP97.6 billion, or 12.8 percent. Appropriations Committee sponsor Rep. Bella Vanessa B. Suansing said the Department of Finance (DOF) was better placed to answer the VAT revenue projection. Turning to production, Elago said agriculture, forestry, and fishing contributed 1.8 percent to GDP growth from 2023 to 2025, compared with 19.8 percent for industry, and argued that budget support for local agriculture, fuel assistance, and food self-sufficiency was inadequate. Suansing attributed the sector's weak performance to external shocks, including El Nino and typhoons, as well as persistent low productivity, declining farm sizes, and variable yields.

Elago asked what would help people remain above the poverty line after DEPDev reported that 8.8 million people had moved above it between 2021 and 2025. Suansing confirmed the figure and explained that it meant their incomes crossed the poverty threshold. Elago questioned whether that alone meant a stable or sufficient income and cited poverty thresholds of PHP16,842 for a five-person family in the National Capital Region (NCR) and PHP14,634 outside NCR, describing the NCR threshold as PHP488 per day. She said minimum wages in 12 regions were below DEPDev's threshold, and that many of the four million jobs generated since 2020 were underemployed. She argued that emergency assistance received much more funding than livelihood and micro, small, and medium enterprise programs, and pressed for decent wages, stable jobs, and added household income so families would not fall back into poverty.

On the NAIA public-private partnership (PPP), Elago asked whether affected stakeholders had been consulted before approval, how many consultations took place, and whether recommendations were included in the concession agreement. Suansing said consultation occurs during the feasibility study; DEPDev was gathering the project details and agreed to submit them. Elago raised concerns about large retailers' role in public markets and the effect on small vendors; Suansing said market administration was within local government jurisdiction. On PPP monitoring, fee changes, and grievances, Suansing said oversight depends on each project and contract, but grievances could be reported to the PPP Center. She confirmed that the Center was handling complaints about privatization projects and promised to furnish the case information. Elago called for the House to review the PPP Center's work.

Department of Budget and Management (DBM)

Rep. Iris Marie Demesa Montes: 4K Rep. Iris Marie Demesa Montes questioned the Department of Budget and Management (DBM) over the proposed 2027 health allocation and what she saw as a threat to universal health care (UHC) services. Budget sponsor Rep. Bella Vanessa B. Suansing attributed the health-budget decline to Health Facilities Enhancement Program utilization and implementation delays, a return of that program and medical-assistance funding to National Expenditure Program levels, and a one-time PHP60 billion return to the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth) that could not recur. Suansing answered that the current-year figure included the one-time return: excluding it gives PHP69 billion, making the proposed allocation PHP5 billion higher on that basis. Montes contrasted PhilHealth's arrears figure with the smaller DBM figure; Suansing specified that DBM's amount covered PAGCOR and PCSO, leaving the broader figures unreconciled. At Montes's request, the sponsor committed to submit DBM's official itemized arrears and remittance accounting, including legacy premium-subsidy obligations. On Montes's question about unremitted earmarked collections, Suansing said UHC revenue is earmarked under law but lacks a cash-tracking mechanism, and relayed DBM's position that the variance remains in the general fund. Montes argued that government must honor those earmarks and timely finance PhilHealth rather than leave contributors bearing the gap.

Montes also asked where proceeds of an ADB-JICA UHC policy loan went, how its first three subprograms were financed and repaid, and whether their health-system targets had been met. Suansing explained that a policy loan enters the general fund and is attributed to UHC programs across the Department of Health (DOH), including facilities and medical assistance, rather than exclusively to PhilHealth. Montes requested loan amounts, terms, implementing agencies, fiscal projections and output assessments before deliberations ended. Suansing said DBM would comply with line-by-line details and projections; no assessment or loan accounting was delivered on the floor.
