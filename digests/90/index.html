At a glance

  • Hearing and agencies: Subcommittee chair Senator Erwin Tulfo led deliberations with Secretary of Tourism Dita Angara-Mathay. The hearing covered the Civil Service Commission (CSC), Career Executive Service Board (CESB), Department of Tourism (DOT), Tourism Promotions Board (TPB), Duty Free Philippines Corporation (DFPC), Nayong Pilipino Foundation (NPF), Tourism Infrastructure and Enterprise Zone Authority (TIEZA), and Philippine Retirement Authority (PRA).
  • Civil-service capability: Members examined unfilled positions, regional service capacity, recruitment and promotion authority, employee records, digital systems and the Career Executive Service Board's screening and development work. CSC said agency heads retain appointment authority, while modernization would connect employee records. CESB sought support for operations after its fee fund closes and Tulfo offered help with replacement vehicles.
  • Tourism delivery: The discussion linked tourism promotion to market access, routes, affordability, local infrastructure, safety, accreditation and service quality. Officials described current programs and proposed improvements, while members pressed for stronger accountability and follow-through. DOT agreed to send a regional tourism-road wish list; officials described accreditation verification and closer work with local governments.
  • Public corporations and funding: TPB explained that treasury balances still require spending authority. TIEZA requested a travel-tax transition while replacement funding was unavailable. Tulfo asked DFPC to study its future, including a possible sale requiring legislation; PRA described tighter retiree screening.
  • Committee action: Tulfo asked for timely replies to prepare the budgets for plenary defense and sought Department of Budget and Management (DBM) participation after its representative cancelled. CSC, CESB and the tourism proposals were deemed submitted for plenary consideration; this did not approve appropriations.

Civil Service Commission (CSC) presentation and questioning

Government staffing and unfilled plantilla positions: Chairperson Marilyn B. Yap described the government workforce under CSC's jurisdiction as more than 3.29 million, including career and non-career professionals and job-order and contract-of-service workers. In response to Senator Erwin Tulfo's questions about vacancies, she said agency heads hold appointment authority and decide whether to fill funded plantilla posts. She said vacancies commonly persist because candidates do not match position-specific qualifications and because recruitment proceeds through publication, screening, interviews and final processing; agencies can hire contract-of-service workers more quickly.

Yap said there were more than 57,000 Department of Education vacancies and that most were teachers. Tulfo asked why agencies did not promote qualified staff into vacancies. Yap said CSC issues anticipatory succession-planning advisories but can only remind agency heads; final decisions and whether to consider external applicants remain with the agencies. CSC was studying ways to focus qualifications on competencies, and Yap hoped to make a competency-based approach an official CSC policy in 2027. She also described a generational shift and retirements outpacing slow recruitment.

Modernization, employee records and public assistance: Yap described the PCSMP and related modernization work as a shift from document-based HR management toward integrated digital systems, including a human-resource management information and payroll system (HRMIS-PS) and a civil-service data-management system. She said records were still largely paper-based, limiting access to employee information and planning; Tulfo raised concern that disconnected records could conceal employees working in multiple government posts, and Yap said she could not discount that possibility. CSC had begun electronic personal-data-sheet submission to a database. Tulfo supported the modernization effort.

Yap said the intended PCSMP pilot was for 40 departments/agencies, with 20 additional agencies available to replace those unable to transition, maintaining a target of at least 40 in the first rollout. She said CSC aimed to complete the transition and begin go-live testing of HRMIS-PS, and to digitize exam registration on a continuing basis, examination and results, with results and authenticated eligibility documents available within one day as a 2027 target. CSC also planned to digitize legal and adjudication services to reduce case-processing delays, which Yap said could last three to four years.

Tulfo asked about the long delay in modernization. Yap said she joined CSC late in 2024, had requested modernization since then, and that the loan terms of reference were approved in the second quarter of 2025; she attributed earlier limits partly to a view that computers alone amounted to modernization rather than unified systems.

On promotion complaints, Yap said agency heads retain appointment discretion and CSC checks qualifications when cases come to it. She described plans to certify HR management officers and overhaul qualification standards toward competency-based requirements. CSC has no specific case category for failure to promote, she said, but its Public Assistance Program, Contact Center ng Bayan and help desk receive complaints.
