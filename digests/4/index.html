At a glance

  • PCSO presentation and officials: PCSO Chairperson Judge Felix P. Reyes and Corporate Planning Department Manager Atty. Anna Liza P. Inciong presented the agency's mandate, medical-assistance and transport programs, revenue and allocation figures, UHC and National Treasury remittances, and 2026-2027 estimates. General Manager Melquiades Robles and PCSO's Accounting and Budget Department representative Joana later answered members' questions.
  • Revenue and statutory-payment trend: PCSO described sales and major-game revenue as rising, while Chair Suansing noted that taxes, dividends, and other remittances did not move in parallel. PCSO attributed variation to net income, tax on winnings, sales-linked special-law shares, and forfeited prizes, while describing the UHC earmark as comparatively constant under law.
  • Accountability, records, and unresolved liabilities: Montes requested PCSO's official Bureau of the Treasury remittance record, which Reyes committed to submit and Garcia directed the agency to provide. Dy pressed PCSO on Philippine Sports Commission remittances under Guiao v. PAGCOR and PCSO; the exchange produced several figures but no securely established remaining liability or settlement schedule.
  • Medical transport and service delivery: Members tested how PCSO would deliver landing craft vessels, ambulances, equipment, and outpatient assistance. PCSO said it planned to begin with around 10 landing craft vessels, require the winning bidder to maintain them for three years and train operators, and make a standby ambulance available for Mandaluyong requests; no new vehicle or delivery schedule was committed specifically to Ynares's office.
  • Support and committee disposition: Several members manifested support for PCSO's proposed budget, often citing medical assistance and patient-transport services. The committee later carried a motion terminating the PCSO briefing and a separate motion suspending proceedings for resumption on August 25, 2026 at 9 a.m.; the motion makers and brief seconding voices remain role-identified rather than personally named.

PCSO presentation

Mandate, operating model, and 2027 priorities: Chairperson Felix P. Reyes framed the proposed budget under PCSO's mandate in Republic Act No. 1169 to generate and provide funds for health programs, medical assistance and services, and charitable activities of national character. Atty. Anna Liza P. Inciong described sweepstakes, lotteries, and similar activities as the revenue base for that mandate, then set out PCSO's stated 2027 priorities: landing craft vessels for coastal communities, medical-equipment donations, participation in a radiation-oncology center, new games and betting platforms to sustain charity funding, digitalized public services, and the transfer of operations to the PCSO Corporate Center in San Marcelino, Manila.

Medical assistance and community health delivery: PCSO reported that its Medical Assistance Program released ₱8.13 billion to 681,999 beneficiaries from 2024 through the first semester of 2026. It said assistance rose slightly to ₱3.25 billion in 2025 even as the beneficiary count declined, and described the expansion of eligible support to professional fees. The presentation also reported growth in institutional partnerships, patient-transport-vehicle distribution, medical-equipment donations, and outpatient and multi-specialty clinic reach.

00:10:49 · asr-000094

Revenue growth and financial performance: The presentation reported major-game revenue rising from ₱62.35 billion in 2024 to ₱64.86 billion in 2025, with ₱32.68 billion generated in the first semester of 2026; it attributed growth mainly to STL and the National Instant Sweepstakes Program. PCSO also reported that 2025 income and expenses rose while profit after tax and net income declined, alongside increased financial assistance, subsidies, and contributions. For the first semester of 2026, it reported lower income and expenses but higher profit after tax and net income than in the comparable 2025 period.

Revenue allocation and 2026-2027 estimates: PCSO presented actual retail receipts and the prescribed prize-, charity-, and operating-fund allocations for 2023 through 2025, followed by estimates for 2026 and 2027. For 2027 it reported estimated retail receipts of ₱68.55 billion and net receipts after printing costs of ₱67.18 billion, then stated ₱39.95 billion for the 55% prize fund, ₱20.15 billion for the 30% charity fund, and ₱10.08 billion for the 15% operating fund. Those are presenter-reported figures; the stated prize-fund amount and the three stated allocations do not arithmetically reconcile with the reported net receipts, so no silent correction is made.

Targets, vehicles, equipment, and operating standards: Against a reported ₱68.64 billion gross-revenue target for 2025, PCSO said it achieved ₱64.86 billion, or 94.5%, and attributed the shortfall largely to calamities and weather disruptions. It reported distributing 1,090 patient transport vehicles against a target of 500, releasing ₱3.245 billion in medical assistance against a ₱3.302 billion target, and establishing 38 institutional partners against a target of 30. It also reported attaining World Lottery Association Level 3 Responsible Gaming Certification and pursuing Security Control Standard certification. For 2025 and the first semester of 2026, it cited full patient-transport coverage, ₱176 million in medical-equipment donations, and ₱348.6 million in mobility aids, rescue devices, and evacuation kits under its corporate social responsibility program.

Universal health care and National Treasury remittances: PCSO reported approximately ₱2.57 billion in Universal Health Care remittances for 2025 and ₱651.47 million and ₱690.67 million for the first two quarters of 2026. UHC remittances, transfer timing and the requested Treasury record: Montes asked when PCSO began remitting its Universal Health Care share to the Bureau of the Treasury and read out presentation figures of ₱2.71 billion for 2022, ₱2 billion for 2023, ₱2.13 billion for 2024, ₱2.87 billion for 2025 and ₱1.94 billion for the first half of 2026. PCSO Chairperson Judge Felix P. Reyes confirmed the figures, said remittances began in 2022, were made quarterly and on time, and that PCSO remembered no delayed instance; he added that the general manager personally delivered the latest remittance to the Treasury. Montes then cited a ₱1.935 billion July 16 turnover covering mandatory contributions from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the first two quarters of 2026 and questioned its timing. She distinguished PCSO's payments to the Treasury from the onward transfer to PhilHealth, which she said she would verify with DBM and DOF. She requested PCSO's official Treasury remittance record for the 40% net-charity-fund share referenced in RA 11223. Reyes committed to submit it, and Senior Vice Chair Albert S. Garcia directed PCSO to comply so the committee could furnish Montes a copy. No submission appears within the round.

Rep. Faustino Michael Carlos T. Dy III

Sports Commission remittances and the Guiao ruling: Speaking as chair of the Youth and Sports Committee, Dy cited Guiao v. PAGCOR and PCSO, G.R. No. 223845, and the ruling concerning PCSO remittances to the Philippine Sports Commission: 30% of the charity-fund proceeds from six sweepstakes or lottery draws per year beginning in 2006. He contrasted that obligation with a reported ₱1.5 million remittance for 2025 and asked whether PCSO had fully complied, the exact accumulated amount due since 2006, how much had been remitted, and when any balance would be settled; he said he had heard an estimate of about ₱30 billion. Reyes said the dispute involved computation based on the old sweepstakes races and PCSO's interpretation of the Supreme Court ruling. His answer referred to a ₱45 million amount, said the total remitted for 2006-2025 was more or less ₱73 million, and later cited a figure rendered in the exchange as approximately ₱122.419 million after a billion-versus-million correction. Reyes also distinguished PCSO's percentage tied to six races or draws from PAGCOR's 5% obligation under Republic Act No. 6847. The answer did not reconcile the ₱45 million, ₱73 million, and approximately ₱122.419 million figures into one accounting basis, provide the exact accumulated unpaid balance, or give a settlement schedule.

04

Rep. Jan "Atty. JP" Padiernos

Landing craft vessels for island medical transport: Padiernos asked about the procurement and feasibility of PCSO's planned landing craft vessels, including PCSO's mandate, allocation, and the risk of burdening recipient local governments with maintenance costs. General Manager Melquiades Robles said the vessels were intended to make patient transport between island municipalities more effective by allowing an ambulance and patient to continue the trip without an extra vehicle transfer. He said recipient LGUs would secure MARINA licenses; PCSO would begin with around 10 vessels and proceed in later years. The vessels would go to provincial governments, which would schedule their use among municipalities. Robles added that the winning procurement bidder would maintain them for the first three years and train operating personnel.

Priority rules for patient transport vehicles and AI-capable X-rays: Padiernos cited PCSO's reported 100% distribution of 1,675 patient transport vehicles worth about ₱3.73 billion and ₱197 million in medical equipment, including AI-capable portable X-rays. He asked how provinces and hospitals were selected and whether geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas would be prioritized over urbanized LGUs able to buy their own ambulances. Robles said selection combined demonstrated need, the recipient's ability to administer the service and train personnel to handle the equipment, and the order in which requests were filed. He noted that specialized equipment could require personnel such as radiologists. The answer did not announce a categorical GIDA-first rule.

Rep. Mikaela Angela B. Suansing

Rising sales versus taxes, dividends, and remittances: Suansing compared PCSO's reported and projected sales—about ₱60.4 billion in 2023, ₱61.3 billion in 2024, ₱63.8 billion in 2025, ₱65.9 billion in 2026, and ₱67.2 billion in 2027—with total taxes, dividends, and other remittances that she said were about ₱22.99 billion in 2023, ₱22.26 billion in 2024, ₱21.85 billion in 2025, and ₱11 billion for the first half of 2026. She asked why the remittance trajectory did not rise with sales. PCSO's Accounting and Budget representative said corporate income tax and dividends move with net income, while final income tax on winnings also varies; she described the applicable rate as 20% for winnings above ₱10,000. She added that the UHC computation follows its applicable statutory basis. The 2026 and 2027 sales figures were presented as projections, and the 2026 remittance figure covered only the first half of the year.

01:41:47 · asr-00062201:45:07 · asr-000660

Special-law contributions and factors outside PCSO's control: Suansing also questioned the uneven contributions under Republic Acts and executive orders, citing about ₱802 million in 2023, ₱680 million in 2024, and ₱344 million in 2025 before an increase in 2026. PCSO said the Girl Scouts and Boy Scouts shares depend on sales, while the dangerous-drugs share depends on forfeited prizes, so those amounts can vary. Suansing summarized that taxes, dividends, and special-law contributions were contingent on changing factors, while the UHC earmark was comparatively constant under law. She said she now understood the mismatch and commended PCSO's reported contribution of roughly ₱112 billion from 2021 through 2026 to priority government programs and projects.

The record establishes the motion and ruling but does not safely identify the mover or seconder.
