At a glance

  • Wage orders and the court injunction: Members disputed whether the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) could implement Wage Order No. 27 while the Pasig court injunction remained in effect and whether Wage Order No. 28 displaced its scheduled increase. DOLE Secretary Francis N. Tolentino said DOLE could not set aside a court order on its own and would act when directed by the court; members pressed for worker protections and a clear legal position. The hearing did not resolve the court matter, and a motion to defer DOLE's budget was rejected.
  • Labor inspection and access to justice: Members connected inspector shortages to workplace enforcement and to the evidence needed in labor cases. DOLE described expanded inspection procedures and training needs; the discussion also covered legal aid for workers, labor-justice institutions, delayed enforcement of awards, and requests for inspection records and case updates.
  • TESDA training and employment: The Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA) described industry-linked training, scholarships, digital learning and job-matching efforts, and reported graduate-employment results. Members asked how training reaches local employers and underserved learners, how scholarship allocations are used, and how graduates move into jobs; workforce facilities and regional access were recurring concerns.
  • Worker protection and social-program accountability: The hearing covered Tulong Panghanapbuhay sa Ating Disadvantaged/Displaced Workers (TUPAD), livelihood sustainability, sugar-worker benefits, child labor, platform work, women workers, market vendors, construction safety and labor compliance in economic zones. Officials described monitoring and support programs, while members sought stronger checks on repeat beneficiaries, clearer outcomes and reports that would let the committee assess whether services reached workers.
  • Requests, deadlines and the close: Members asked DOLE and TESDA to submit budget priorities, program reports and institutional amendments for committee review, with agency heads agreeing to provide several requested materials. Committee Chair Rep. Mikaela Angela B. Suansing set 12 noon the following day for DOLE's consolidated letter and TESDA's updated institutional amendment. The committee then approved a motion to terminate and adjourn the hearing series; those procedural votes did not approve the pending budget amendments.
