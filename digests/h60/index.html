At a glance

  • Budget proposal and committee disposition: Department of Agrarian Reform (DAR) Secretary Conrado Estrella III presented the department's FY 2027 proposal. Subcommittee Chair Senator Francis Kiko Pangilinan asked how funding would translate into resilient, productive farms; Senate Majority Leader Senator Juan Miguel "Migz" F. Zubiri expressed support, while Senate Committee on Finance Chair Senator Joseph Victor "JV" G. Ejercito noted that the committee would prioritize programs within limited fiscal space. Estrella also asked the committee to consider transferring slow-moving or non-performing funds to DAR, without specifying an amount; no disposition is recorded in his presentation. Ejercito moved to advance the budget, and Pangilinan seconded subject to DAR submitting requested information and documents.
  • Debt condonation and land distribution: Estrella said DAR met the Agrarian Emancipation Act deadline to complete debt condonation, with remaining certificate distribution in the Bangsamoro area after processing and validation. He reported progress in land-title distribution and described remaining work on tenure security, digitized records and beneficiary services.
  • SPLIT titles and field delivery: Pangilinan asked about the Support to Parcelization of Lands for Individual Titling (SPLIT) project's extended schedule and counterpart funding. DAR Undersecretary for Foreign Assisted and Special Projects Jesry T. Palmares said the project was in progress and on schedule, that DAR had requested no additional budget or loan, and that completion and catch-up plans extended into the following years. Estrella attributed delays in distributing processed electronic titles to successive typhoons, field access and the focus on meeting the certificate deadline; he said distributions would resume.
  • Farm support, roads and market access: Estrella described irrigation projects funded through the Comprehensive Agrarian Reform Program and implemented by the National Irrigation Administration (NIA), as well as farm-to-market roads. Senators also discussed direct public purchasing under the Sagip Saka Law, farmer access to markets and the need to measure whether assistance raises farm incomes. Zubiri described reduced transport costs in Bukidnon after roads made truck access possible. Estrella described DAR's market-linkage and Partnership Against Hunger and Poverty initiatives; Pangilinan requested success stories and income data. He also described equipment, training and credit assistance, while senators stressed cooperative capacity and extension services.
  • Agrarian justice and land-service policy: Pangilinan asked how Department of Justice Opinion No. 16 affected farmers' access to DAR services. DAR Undersecretary for Legal Affairs Atty. Napoleon U. Galit explained the department's interpretation concerning private agricultural land coverage and said consultations would continue before the Secretary decided whether to issue an administrative order. The exchange distinguished the affected private-land coverage from DAR's continuing acquisition of government-owned public land.
  • Bridge financing and project locations: Ejercito asked about an agrarian-reform bridge program involving the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) and local governments. Palmares described loan and government-counterpart financing and said the proposed sites had been narrowed through National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) review. He promised a location list but requested discretion because of attempted negotiations in project areas; Estrella said the National Bureau of Investigation (NBI) had been alerted.
  • Farmer organization and food supply: Pangilinan argued that clustering and stronger extension support can help small farms produce at scale, and asked DAR to provide data on Agrarian Reform Communities (ARCs). Palmares described the IPARC research and Value Chain Innovation for Sustainable Transformation in Agrarian Reform Communities (VISTA) initiative. Members also discussed public food purchasing through the Sagip Saka Law; Estrella identified the Bureau of Jail Management and Penology (BJMP), the Philippine Army and the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) as direct purchasers from agrarian reform beneficiaries.

DAR presentation

Land distribution, legal services, and agrarian justice: DAR reported rising land distribution from 21,876 titles covering about 25,159 hectares for 24,804 beneficiaries in 2022, to 69,897 titles (84,044 hectares; 73,399 beneficiaries) in 2023, a 396% increase over baseline; 102,536 titles (120,772 hectares; 88,310 beneficiaries) in 2024, a 581% increase; and 199,838 titles (302,540 hectares; 176,202 beneficiaries) in 2025, a 1,133% increase. Estrella also described declining agrarian-law case backlogs after hiring roughly 62-63 lawyers, local dispute-resolution efforts and Agrarian Reform Justice on Wheels. The Department of Agrarian Reform Adjudication Board (DARAB) resolved 7,396 of 9,874 cases (74.9%) from January to June 2026.
