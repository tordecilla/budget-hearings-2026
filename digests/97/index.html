At a glance

  • Department and sponsor presentation: Rep. Bella Vanessa B. Suansing presented the Department of National Defense (DND) and attached agencies, describing external and internal defense, Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP) modernization, veterans’ benefits and disaster response. Her opening presentation gave budget categories but no aggregate department amount.
  • Maritime security, drones and exercises: Rep. Presley C. De Jesus asked about West Philippine Sea surveillance, fisher support, Balikatan and other exercises, drone integration and cost-sharing. Suansing described coordination and modernization priorities but did not establish a 50–50 exercise-cost arrangement.
  • Infrastructure, gender programs and military-access costs: Rep. Sarah Jane I. Elago questioned Tatag ng Infrastruktura para sa Kapayapaan at Seguridad (TIKAS) project spending and completion records, gender and development programming, Women, Peace and Security activities, Enhanced Defense Cooperation Agreement (EDCA) costs and effects on fisherfolk. The project figures and status categories were not fully reconciled, and no cumulative EDCA total was supplied.
  • Drone policy, partnerships and campus rights: Rep. Renee Louise M. Co pursued the delivery of the Toboso post-operations report, then asked about a disputed drone video, school partnerships, scholarships, military presence on campus and alleged harassment of University of Santo Tomas (UST) student Raven Rosales. DND later delivered the requested post-operations report during the round; its investigation of the drone allegation remained separate.
  • Modernization and pensions: Rep. Antonio L. Tinio questioned AFP modernization funding, public reporting and the military and uniformed personnel pension burden. Suansing said the modernization proposal fell below the department’s stated need, agreed pension reform required study and supported further evaluation.
  • Foreign influence and defense posture: Tinio challenged foreign-influence narratives concerning Chinese-Filipino schools and argued that U.S. state-linked voices also required scrutiny. Citing conflict risks to military and economic sites, he advocated greater self-reliance and regional cooperation. Suansing took note of his concerns and urged support for modernization and archipelagic defense.
  • Army readiness and personnel concerns: Rep. Sergio C. Dagooc challenged Army readiness results and described personnel concerns. Suansing attributed the gaps to new standards and evolving threats; Dagooc urged review and supported modernization.

Agency budget proposals

Department of National Defense and attached agencies: AFP modernization. Suansing described a ₱50 billion FY 2027 National Expenditure Program (NEP) proposal for the AFP Modernization Program and said it fell short of DND’s stated ₱126.96 billion need. She identified ₱35 billion for Horizon 2 and 3 projects and additional multi-year contract obligations in FY 2027, plus ₱21.2 billion in existing multi-year contracts due under the FY 2026 General Appropriations Act (GAA). She summarized these servicing obligations as ₱56 billion. Tinio gave two unreconciled historical modernization totals: ₱469.393 billion for 2013 through the proposed FY 2027 period, then ₱489.5 billion after adding FY 2027. The aggregate FY 2027 DND and attached-agencies NEP allocation and original agency-submission total were not stated; the modernization need is distinct from that missing submission total.

Budget categories. Suansing described the DND proposal as covering personnel and operating needs, AFP modernization capital outlay, veteran pensions and benefits, and civil defense. She appealed for immediate passage.

Military and uniformed personnel (MUP) pensions. Tinio cited cross-government pension totals of ₱145 billion for FY 2026 and ₱154 billion for proposed FY 2027, and cumulative funding of ₱1.295 trillion from 2014 through proposed FY 2027. These figures cover MUP services across government, not DND or AFP alone. Tinio said the national budget bears all pension costs. Suansing agreed the burden was unsustainable but said reform required further study; she wanted AFP personnel distinguished from civilian uniformed services in any burden-sharing proposal and said DND was open to reform subject to assessment. No timeline or adopted reform was stated.

Convergence program and construction projects. Elago cited ₱38.03 billion in combined DND–Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) Tatag ng Infrastruktura para sa Kapayapaan at Seguridad (TIKAS) program funding for 2019–2025. A DND submission listed Philippine Army TIKAS projects valued at ₱14.496 billion for 2021–2026 and Philippine Air Force projects valued at ₱4.806 billion for 2019–2025. Elago said the spending exceeded the Department of Science and Technology’s (DOST) entire budget for FY 2027. Elago said the project lines appeared in DPWH’s Basic Infrastructure Program (BIP) after having been listed under TIKAS. She separately cited a San Antonio, Zambales barracks and boot-camp project with a ₱56.91 million contract, ₱60 million in the 2024 GAA and a ₱6.5 million FY 2027 NEP line; The contract ran from September 2024 to July 2025; DPWH showed 66.81% accomplishment. For a married-quarters rehabilitation project, she cited a ₱28.67 million contract, ₱30 million in the 2025 GAA and a ₱25 million FY 2027 NEP line; DPWH showed 0% accomplishment. Suansing said DND submitted no FY 2027 TIKAS projects and had no TIKAS project list. Elago requested a project-by-project accounting; Suansing asked her to furnish the details for review and said DND would raise the awards concern with DPWH. The project amounts and the multi-year TIKAS totals have different scopes and are not summed into a DND-wide NEP figure.

Gender and development (GAD). Elago cited a Philippine Commission on Women (PCW) 2024 assessment that 80.6% of DND’s Women, Peace and Security (WPS) allocation went directly to related activities, programs and expenditures. She said the Philippine Army ranked fifth in GAD allocation at 15.37%, against a general minimum of at least 5%. The discussion also covered WPS programming and service-member protections; these utilization and allocation percentages are prior-year policy evidence, not proposed FY 2027 appropriations.

Other requested amounts. Elago asked for cumulative government spending under the Enhanced Defense Cooperation Agreement (EDCA), but Suansing gave no cumulative figure. She said the FY 2027 proposal covered regular camp expenses without an EDCA-specific line item, and Elago requested a breakdown. De Jesus asked for a drone appropriation; Suansing gave no amount, saying governance systems would be funded first and agreeing that analytical personnel, communications and maintenance could yield more benefit than equipment alone. No amount for either question was established.

Members expressed support for the DND budget and pressed for stronger funding and oversight. The joint majority–minority motion to terminate interpellation was carried without objection; no increase or restoration was adopted in this record.

Department of National Defense (DND) and attached agencies

DND mandate and operating context: Suansing described the Department of National Defense (DND) as responsible for guarding against external and internal threats to national peace and security. She said the mandate is carried out by the Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP) across maritime, air, land and cyber domains, through the Office of Civil Defense (OCD) and administration of the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Systems, and through the Philippine Veterans Affairs Office (PVAO) to fulfill obligations to veterans who have completed service.

Suansing said uniformed services are ordinarily the first national instrumentality to reach affected communities after a calamity, and sometimes the only one present in the initial period. She also described the armed forces as increasingly called upon to be the first relief to reach stricken communities, while maritime areas remain contested and threats extend into domains that fleets or garrisons cannot hold alone.

Rep. Presley C. De Jesus: De Jesus asked what the Department of National Defense (DND) had established about reports of a possible new structure at Bajo de Masinloc, which DND Secretary Gilberto C. Teodoro Jr. had said remained unverified, and what the 2027 surveillance and verification requirements would address. Sponsor Suansing answered that there was no permanent structure. Asked what impeded prompt verification without disclosing sensitive coverage or deployment details, she cited the accuracy of information. She said the proposed budget was a stepping stone toward gradually increasing operations and surveillance capability.

Suansing described real-time information coordination with the Philippine Coast Guard (PCG) and other agencies through DND fusion centers. Asked what fishers and civilian maritime personnel could expect during emergencies, she cited the Coast Guard’s fleet increase and coordination on fleet capacity; when De Jesus asked about fishing guidance and where fishers could operate, she answered that the Coast Guard and Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (BFAR) also provide fuel assistance.

De Jesus asked what defense objectives the expanded exercises with the United States, Japan and France—including Balikatan 2026 activities in Itbayat, Batanes, near Taiwan—were intended to achieve. Suansing cited 360-degree terrain awareness, acclimatizing troops to local terrain and introducing them to new types of defense, weaponry and machinery. Asked whether China could view Balikatan as aggression, she said the department’s position was that the scale gave no reason to treat it as aggression, while acknowledging that joint activities could nevertheless be construed as provocative given their objectives.

Asked what measurable capability gains prior exercises produced, she said experience from the exercises enabled the Philippines to lead a “40-fighter strike” in Australia, which she presented as evidence of improved troop capability. On preventing miscalculation and preserving independent defense decisions, Suansing cited strict adherence to the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS) and international law.

De Jesus asked how the proposed 2027 budget would pair drone acquisitions with the systems and trained personnel needed to use them effectively, emphasizing operational integration, secure communications and support networks. Suansing said integration systems were being introduced under C4ISTAR—command, control, communications, computers, intelligence, surveillance, target acquisition and reconnaissance—which she identified as a core pillar of the comprehensive archipelagic-defense concept.

Asked whether DND uses artificial-intelligence models to process imagery, identify unusual activity or prioritize information for human analysts, she said the department could not disclose that information.

De Jesus noted the March 2026 Philippines–France visiting forces agreement and a proposed rise in bilateral and multilateral engagement targets from 159 in 2026 to 242 in 2027, then asked what capability gains justified the increase. Suansing said the department was observing classified changes in capability and defense posture.

Asked how exercise costs were divided, she explained that each country generally bears expenses for its own troops and equipment, while partner countries bear their own. De Jesus characterized this as “kanya-kanya” and floated a likely 50–50 proposition; Suansing affirmed the separate-country cost principle, but the exchange did not establish a 50–50 arrangement. Suansing said every exercise is scored against indicators to assess proficiency or interoperability. Asked which completed initiative showed the clearest improvement relative to cost, she identified Exercise Pitch Black in Australia. De Jesus closed by supporting the DND budget and thanking its personnel.

His account relied on reports and statements he presented during the exchange; the sponsor did not offer a site-by-site threat assessment.
