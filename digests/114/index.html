At a glance

  • Climate, water and food resilience: The hearing opened with El Niño risks to crop, livestock and fishery production. Members urged coordinated water storage, irrigation, drought response and timely assistance, and questioned whether existing contingency plans and resources would reach affected producers.
  • Budget execution and public accountability: Questions tracked delayed releases, low utilization, procurement and documentation for irrigation, cold storage, farm-to-market roads, post-harvest facilities and other programs. Officials described implementation progress and agreed to provide additional lists, status reports and supporting records.
  • Trade, prices and enforcement: Members examined the tension between farmgate returns and affordable food prices, import timing and permits, tariffs, smuggling and safe disposal of seized products. Officials described possible actions and enforcement limits; proposed import restrictions remained tentative, and the hearing did not establish a general fertilizer-import ban.
  • Fishers, smallholders and civil-society input: The Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (BFAR) faced questions about commercial vessels in municipal waters, pending litigation and overdue case and production data.
  • Civil society and project transparency: People's Budget Coalition (PBC) Co-convenor Kenneth Isaiah Ibasco Abante raised project-coordinate, cost and disclosure concerns and offered citizen and scientific monitoring. CODE-NGO Project Coordinator Timothy Salomon, speaking for PBC and Task Force Mapalad National Peasant Federation, urged local pest controls, farmer organization, family-farming support and access to land-use information.
  • Producer livelihoods and reliable data: Members asked agencies to measure whether programs improved farmer and fisher income, and to reconcile production data used for import decisions. The discussion also covered farmer registration, cooperative access to services, livestock restructuring, sugar-pest assistance and milk-feeding delivery.
  • Plenary status: The chair deemed the DA Office of the Secretary budget and attached agencies and corporations submitted for plenary consideration subject to requested documents. Outstanding submissions and proposed funding changes remained pending; the committee action was not an appropriation approval.

Citizen monitoring and scientific participation: Abante commended the DA's Farm-to-Market Road Watch website, which he said listed 3,455 completed projects out of 6,428, worth PHP 110 billion and spanning nearly 2,700 kilometers; he noted that the e-reports portal then showed only 24 reports from citizen inspectors. He offered PBC and the Bisto Proyecto network, active in 12 regions, to help monitor DA road and irrigation projects with farmer and fisher groups. He said more than 200 volunteers had monitored 251 DPWH projects worth PHP 14 billion across 12 regions, identified 92 unsatisfactory projects and resolved nine, and were improving the feedback process with DPWH. Committee chair Senator Francis Kiko Pangilinan placed on the record that Agriculture Secretary Francisco P. Tiu Laurel Jr. gave a thumbs-up to Abante's request to work with the DA; Abante welcomed the signal.

Abante also urged the committee to involve scientists, hydrologists and disaster-resilience specialists, and agriculturists and ecologists from the University of the Philippines Los Baños (UPLB), in reviewing the budget, project coordinates and El Niño needs. He proposed institutionalizing the Budget Monitoring Group and Budget Monitoring Network and supporting third-party research institutions to conduct detailed project analysis, and asked that farmers' and fishers' groups be heard. He thanked the committee for inviting civil society, which he said was the first such invitation in at least ten years, and asked Pangilinan to recognize Timothy Salomon, who was carrying input from farmer and fisher groups.

CODE-NGO Project Coordinator Timothy Salomon

Civil-society proposals for sugar pest control and farm inputs: CODE-NGO project coordinator Timothy Salomon, representing the People’s Budget Coalition and Task Force Mapalad National Peasant Federation, supported the Department of Agriculture’s response to RSSI but urged stronger pest control. He contrasted synthetic pesticide costs of ₱10,000 per hectare with a proposed biocontrol method costing ₱2,600 per hectare, which he said could be multiplied in small laboratories, including by agricultural students. He described RSSI as related to cocolisap and urged the Department to apply lessons from its coconut response to sugar.

Salomon also urged less reliance on imported synthetic fertilizers and pesticides and greater development of locally produced, nature-based inputs. He said reports identified seven fertilizers still used in banana production that had been banned in the European Union and United States, and promised to submit the product names; he framed this as a report requiring follow-up, not a verified list in this exchange. He argued that local production could retain value with farmers and protect soil.

Farmer organization, extension services and land conversion: Salomon raised gaps in farmer registration and access to agricultural services. Citing reported figures, he said 40% of the farming and fishing population remained unregistered in the DA farmer registry, while an Agricultural Training Institute (ATI) report indicated that 80% of farmers had access to organizational registration. He argued that registration requirements could leave smallholders outside support and urged follow-through on a prior Department commitment to community organizing and cooperatives.

He supported the proposed National Land Use Act and said that, over a decade, land conversion had outpaced irrigation, with irrigation reaching only about 30% of the area converted each year. Although local-government reclassification and land-conversion processes included public consultation, he said farmers lacked access to irrigation data needed to participate meaningfully and asked the Department to make the information available.

Senator Francis Kiko Pangilinan said the Senate had passed on third reading twin measures on extension services and reestablishing the Bureau of Agriculture and Fisheries Cooperatives. Under the proposed arrangement, the Department of Agriculture would organize and develop agricultural cooperatives while the Cooperative Development Authority would regulate them. Pangilinan said the Department could meanwhile augment its REACH program and consider a transition provision; Laurel said he would push the measures.
