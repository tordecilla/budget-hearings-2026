Rep. Presley C. De Jesus: Representative Presley C. De Jesus cited the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD)’s fiscal year 2027 target of 3.5 million active Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program (4Ps) households, including 493,000 new households identified through the Community-Based Monitoring System (CBMS). Sponsor Representative JC Rahman A. Nava, M.D., said registration and validation of listed beneficiaries were expected to finish by October, and confirmed that the newly identified households were included in the 3.5 million target.

De Jesus asked how many CBMS households remained untraceable, ineligible or unregistered; Nava said about half of those identified through CBMS could not be traced. He said a new digital system for real-time tracking of 4Ps beneficiaries was being installed and would be functional by year-end.

De Jesus also asked whether graduates who later fall back into poverty are monitored. Nava said there is no regular monitoring after graduation; some graduates receive sustainable livelihoods through other programs such as the Sustainable Livelihood Program (SLP), while others are fully separated from DSWD’s list. He said DSWD coordinates with local governments and endorses graduates to them.

De Jesus compared the Assistance to Individuals in Crisis Situations (AICS) allocation of ₱63.90 billion in the fiscal year 2026 GAA with ₱33.29 billion in the fiscal year 2027 NEP, a reduction of ₱30.61 billion, and asked how many crisis clients the proposed amount could serve. Nava said the initial target had been 6–8 million, but estimated that about 4 million beneficiaries would not be able to avail of the program under the reduced allocation. He also said about 8 million were served in the current year.

De Jesus cited a Commission on Audit (COA) finding of ₱397.45 million in duplicate and overlapping AICS–Ayuda sa Kapos ang Kita Program (AKAP) payments in 2025 across the central office and nine regional offices. Nava said DSWD and COA were flagging beneficiaries with multiple payments and had an embedded cross-matching system before accepting clients. He said the number considered actual duplication was about 14,000 beneficiaries, far below the roughly 400,000 duplications referenced from the COA finding. DSWD had flagged those beneficiaries and barred them temporarily from further assistance; Nava said repeat incidents could lead to appropriate charges. He did not say that the money had been recovered.

Asked how former AKAP recipients would be handled after AKAP lost funding for the next fiscal year, Nava said assistance was now under AICS and that AICS had also been used for other crisis responses. He described AICS as a department-initiated program for temporary relief to individuals or families in crisis, while AKAP was Congress-initiated and aimed at low-income people on a more regular basis. He confirmed eligible applicants may walk into a DSWD office without a barangay endorsement, presenting documents such as a medical certificate to establish their need.

De Jesus cited an expected expansion of the Walang Gutom Program from 300,000 to 750,000 households through the 3.8 billion Asian Development Bank (ADB) Refuel Project (currency unspecified in this exchange), while the program’s locally funded fiscal year 2027 allocation remained ₱1.89 billion. He asked about the loan’s effectivity and disbursement and the counterpart funding and implementation requirements for expansion. Nava referred to a current “700” figure without clearly stating its unit, described Refuel as an expansion of food stamps, and said the national government was putting more funds into Walang Gutom using CBMS to identify the food-poor population. He said the efforts had been merged into the Walang Gutom Program.

De Jesus asked about the program’s ₱3,000 monthly food credit on electronic benefit transfer cards. Nava said about 95% of beneficiaries usually attend the monthly Kalusugan, Kasanayan, at Kabuhayan sessions, and those who repeatedly miss them are eventually dropped. He said the sessions aim to encourage behavioral changes and build skills.

Nava reported 266,057 active CBMS-identified beneficiaries under Walang Gutom. He said the program had 1,214 retail partners, including 221 farmers’ cooperative associations and 788 women-led or women-owned groups, and that prices were within standard retail prices. The payment system runs through merchants’ phones using point-of-sale capability; beneficiaries may use their credit in one transaction, which Nava said can reduce repeat trips. The sponsor said he knew of no cases of beneficiaries reselling their purchases. Asked whether unused credit carries forward, he said the department’s experience was that the monthly credit was fully used.

De Jesus said the fiscal year 2027 NEP provides ₱51.64 billion for 4.224 million social pension beneficiaries, while CBMS data reportedly identifies about 550,000 additional indigent seniors who remain unserved. Nava said no additional beneficiaries were expected under the proposed allocation; DSWD would most likely use the same list next year, and the budget would suffice only for that number.

De Jesus asked how many eligible claims under the Expanded Centenarians Act were pending and what was causing delays. Nava replied that releases were generally quarterly. He said applicants could apply directly and that the responsible commission intended to digitize the system; applicants with bank accounts could receive payment within about a week, compared with about three months for those without accounts. The answer did not give the backlog size or explain the causes of the delays. De Jesus urged DSWD to prevent delays and said he supported the department’s budget.

Rep. JC M. Abalos: Representative JC M. Abalos opened by crediting the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), citing 1.77 million Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program (4Ps) households that had graduated to self-sufficiency since 2022 and 7.24 million children from beneficiary households who had completed basic education. He then asked whether the proposed 2027 budget accounted for emerging poverty risks: the 2025 poverty incidence of 9.7% did not reflect the Middle East-linked oil-price shock that the Philippine Institute for Development Studies (PIDS) projected could add 1.34 million people to poverty, while the country’s new upper-middle-income classification could raise the international comparison threshold from $4.20 to $8.30 per person per day and potentially triple measured incidence.

The DSWD sponsor, Representative JC Rahman A. Nava, M.D., said the budget basis was the 2023 poverty figure of 15%, rather than the latest figure below 10%. He said Assistance to Individuals in Crisis Situations (AICS) had been tapped through Uplift to address the current crisis and augment 4Ps benefits. Asked what the department could do if inflation or other risks worsened conditions beyond the 2023 baseline, Nava said it could repeat crisis interventions such as Uplift. Abalos later asked for continued communication with the Department of Budget and Management (DBM) and the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DepDev) if conditions on the ground worsened; Nava agreed and said AICS and temporary programs could provide relief and be scaled up if needed.

Abalos raised overpayments associated with delays in removing households that had become self-sufficient, noting heavy social-worker caseloads and delays in Community-Based Monitoring System (CBMS) data. Nava said DSWD was developing a digitalization program with almost real-time tracking to address these concerns. Abalos welcomed a real-time tagging system and proposed empowering 4Ps parent leaders to help verify households ready to graduate. Nava accepted the proposal but said DSWD could not compensate the leaders because there was no additional budget; Abalos welcomed the department’s continued support for parent leaders’ role in the program.

Abalos asked about Set 12 households awaiting 4Ps inclusion after the Listahanan-to-CBMS transition. He cited 434,069 wait-listed households, of whom the Social Registry Office had endorsed 166,251 for inclusion in the 4Ps CBMS. Nava said 59,462 of the 434,069 remained unidentified; the rest were already included and would be among the 3.5 million beneficiaries next year. He said validation began early in the year, the remaining households were spread across the country, and households had been advised to approach their local government units for identification. The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) had committed to validate the remaining group but was behind target; Nava said it had told DSWD it expected to finish by the end of September.

Abalos asked why the proposed 2027 coverage target was 3.5 million households, following the reduction from the customary 4.4 million to 3.5 million beginning in 2026. Nava attributed the lower target to declining poverty incidence, while saying DSWD still intended to cover 10% of the near-poor group. He confirmed the target was based on the prevailing poverty incidence and identified the 15% figure as the basis. Nava said PSA uses the Family Income and Expenditure Survey, conducted every three years. Abalos warned that a three-year survey cycle may miss intervening crises and asked DSWD and its sponsor to keep DBM and DepDev informed if emerging conditions materially worsened. Nava agreed; he said AICS and temporary programs such as Uplift could provide relief and be duplicated or scaled up if necessary.

Abalos said the House had passed the priority Four-Ps Act measure, which would institutionalize the first 1,000 days and a rice subsidy, and asked whether those programs remained intact despite a slight reduction in 4Ps funding. Nava said the allocation remained sufficient because the target beneficiary count was smaller.

He said the proposed one-third funding reduction could reverse the 180-day feeding cycle achieved in 2026 and asked whether the expected 120-day cycle for 1.9 million children would affect outcomes. Nava said restoring the 2026 level for 2027 would be better for the department; under the reduced allocation, its estimate was a 120-day cycle.

Abalos asked whether DSWD’s reported Walang Gutom program discrepancies covered the full beneficiary list or a regional sample: 238 households had unidentified or disputed roster members, and local government units had confirmed that 128 households no longer lived in the surveyed area. Nava said the figures covered the whole program. He said DSWD continued field validation to resolve discrepancies and confirm or finally accept beneficiaries.

Abalos asked when the DBM-approved 5,627-item enhanced staffing structure would appear in the staffing summary and be funded, noting that DSWD had over 17,000 contract-of-service and job-order personnel—more than three times its authorized permanent positions—and that many 4Ps links had served for over a decade. Nava said about 5,000 new items were available that year and were being filled, with another 5,000 expected the following year. Abalos welcomed the expansion as a path to security of tenure for long-serving social workers.

Abalos asked how the DSWD Special Committee on LGBTQIA+ Affairs, established by Executive Order No. 51 (2023), could fund its programs after he found no specific line item for its activities in the General Appropriations Act. Nava said DSWD needed to ask DBM for a specific item, that no such DBM item existed at the time, and that DSWD was absorbing the related costs. Abalos said he understood the department had written to DBM seeking a small sum, then asked whether Nava would support a future request for a separate committee budget. Nava said he would support the initiative.
