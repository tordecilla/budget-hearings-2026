At a glance

  • Funding gap: CHED Chairperson Shirley Castañeda Agrupis presented a 70.2-billion-peso request against 35.43 billion in the NEP. Members supported additional funding, but the committee chair warned that limited fiscal space required prioritization rather than assuming the full request could be restored.
  • Who gets assistance: Members challenged late payments, inadequate grants and the role of congressional allocation lists in Tulong Dunong. CHED cited regional validation and direct Land Bank payments, while warning that newer scholarship cohorts needed continuing appropriations. Questions about equitable access and some submitted beneficiary lists remained disputed.
  • Capacity and quality: Discussion covered SUC reimbursement rates, permanent faculty positions and pay, classrooms and dormitories, student safety and expression, medical training and LUC compliance. CHED and LEB also sought support for faculty development, legal-education programs, research and digital systems.
  • Delivery commitments: CHED reported 97.9% utilization of 2025 continuing appropriations and 51% for 2026. Agrupis committed to settle the earlier SUC deficiency package by the third week of September, subject to institutions completing requirements. Improved utilization did not mean every payment had already reached its recipient.
  • Work still required: The committee requested CHED–DSWD reconciliation to prevent double counting of 4Ps beneficiaries, updated enrollment-capacity simulations, and a prioritized funding letter in the first half of the following week. New SUC deficiencies and the final amount of any budget restoration remained unsettled. The CHED hearing ended without approving its budget.

Agency presentation

A budget proposal nearly twice the NEP provision: Agrupis presented CHED's ₱70.2 billion request against a ₱35.43 billion provision in the 2027 National Expenditure Program, warning that roughly half of its identified requirements remained unfunded. She said about 98% of the allocation was classified as maintenance and operating expenses because it financed scholarships, subsidies, and institutional assistance, rather than simply administration, while personnel and capital funding remained limited.

Free tuition, subsidies, and scholarship continuity: CHED sought ₱16.24 billion for free higher education against ₱6.97 billion in the NEP and warned that 137,000 beneficiaries could be left behind. Agrupis also contrasted a ₱35.5 billion TES proposal with ₱17.09 billion provided, and a ₱5.13 billion Tulong Dunong request with ₱3.71 billion provided; she put the latter's exposed beneficiaries at 93,000.
