Senator Joel Villanueva

Flight cancellations, airport weather information, and advance notice: Using an account from friends in Bulacan that he said he could not independently verify, Villanueva described two consecutive days when they traveled to Ninoy Aquino International Airport (NAIA) Terminal 1 for a Manila–Isabela flight and learned about two hours before departure that it was canceled, reportedly because of heavy rain and inadequate instruments or information. He asked whether PAGASA had supplied the Civil Aviation Authority of the Philippines (CAAP) with timely data needed for flight decisions, saying he would raise the matter with CAAP and the Department of Transportation (DOTr). Solidum said PAGASA issues a color-coded 24-hour public rainfall forecast and a three-hour heavy-rain forecast relevant to possible cancellations, and that airport-area forecasters also provide aviation forecasts. Asked whether information covered both departure and destination airports, he said this depended on the route. Servando described coordination with airport operations, meteorological services and monitoring equipment at major airports; he said PAGASA did not have a station at the Isabela destination discussed, contrasting it with major airports. Asked how much advance warning airlines could receive, Servando explained that sudden, short-lived thunderstorms during an active habagat period could affect airport operations.

Villanueva clarified that he wanted to know how many hours ahead PAGASA could provide a reliable warning, including information that might help determine whether a flight should proceed. Servando said rainfall warnings were issued every three hours, while forecasts could be valid for 24 hours, extend up to three days, and be updated every six hours. Solidum said the useful heavy-rain forecast horizon was then three hours; he described a research-stage target of updating forecasts every 15 minutes using artificial intelligence, which would require substantial data inputs and funding. Villanueva asked whether that lead time could support an airline-cancellation decision. Senator Paolo Benigno “Bam” Aquino IV reframed the issue around rainfall and flood prediction, asking whether Philippine capability was comparable internationally and whether better technology could improve it. Solidum and Servando distinguished stronger tropical-cyclone tracking from rainfall forecasting: Servando said rainfall remained difficult to predict precisely by amount, timing and affected area even with advanced technology, though Doppler radar supported forecasts; he described the country’s cyclone-tracking accuracy as comparable and its expertise as strong. Aquino suggested that airlines’ cost incentives could favor last-minute cancellations and raised a possible DOTr policy requiring earlier cancellation decisions. Villanueva agreed that his anecdotal question might be unfair but said the agencies’ explanation helped him understand their capabilities and the airline decision.
