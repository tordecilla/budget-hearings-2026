At a glance

  • Flood mitigation beyond isolated projects: Members pressed for coordinated basin and urban flood plans, functioning pumps, regular dredging and completion of unfinished defenses. DPWH described planning deadlines and interventions, while questions about project effectiveness, local coordination and funding remained.
  • Procurement records and disputed allocations: Members demanded project documents, contractor accountability and explanations for uneven district allocations. Nazal challenged the treatment of unimplemented appropriations; Tinio questioned project insertions and pork barrel. These exchanges recorded contested claims and proposed safeguards, without establishing that every disputed allocation was unlawful.
  • Finishing roads and bridges: Right-of-way problems, funding gaps and incomplete approaches continued to delay usable infrastructure. Dizon supported prioritizing unfinished works and multiyear continuity; members also sought repairs and progress on foreign-assisted projects. Requests and referral steps were not final funding or construction approvals.
  • Safety, accessibility and workers: Questions covered geotechnical studies, earthquake readiness, accessible crossings and facilities, construction-site safety and workers’ rights. DPWH acknowledged implementation weaknesses and offered follow-up checks, enforcement and project-specific action.
  • Classroom schedules and project readiness: Tinio and Dizon discussed different classroom funding cohorts: the 2027 proposal and construction using the 2026 allocation. Dizon proposed earlier validation and school-specific project lists to reduce delays; the exchange did not establish an adopted amendment or guarantee completion of the entire backlog.
  • Briefing concluded: The committee terminated the DPWH briefing and suspended until August 27, 2026 at 9 a.m. Termination of the briefing was not passage of the department’s budget.

01

Rep. Iris Marie Demesa Montes

Project transparency, progress payments and quarterly reporting: Montes asked DPWH to make its transparency portal cover the full project life cycle, including bidding, physical progress, costs and disbursements, contractor performance and public feedback. Dizon said the portal already carried documents for completed and ongoing projects from the past ten years and would also carry supporting records for more than 11,000 proposed 2027 projects. He described archived bidding livestreams, bidding documents, accomplishment updates and satellite imagery obtained with the Philippine Space Agency, approximately quarterly. He acknowledged that the portal showed fully paid projects but lacked progress billings, and agreed these should be added. When Montes sought a report at the next budget hearing on the portal's contribution to accountability, Dizon committed to regular quarterly reports instead of waiting until then.
