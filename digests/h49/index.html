At a glance

  • Fiscal autonomy and budget priorities: Members examined the new fiscal-autonomy framework, questioned the gap between DBM's recommendation and the Judiciary's proposal, and asked officials to identify priorities if not all requests could be funded. The Judiciary agreed to provide priorities, particularly for infrastructure, and sought a copy of DBM's comments.
  • Backlogs, staffing, and court performance: The exchanges covered uneven caseloads, case-resolution targets, vacancies, hiring, digital courts, and a requested infrastructure catch-up plan. Officials emphasized the complexity of individual cases and described regional court management and digital systems as ways to support court operations.
  • Court facilities and proposed interventions: Members pressed for progress on the Manila Hall of Justice, the two-building Cagayan de Oro judicial complex, and Sandiganbayan facilities. Design, procurement, safety, and staffing steps remain central to those projects; members expressed support or offered to pursue amendments, with requests kept distinct from approved appropriations.
  • Accountability and appellate review: Members raised public concerns about corruption cases and judicial decisions. Judiciary officials said allegations of misconduct should be substantiated and brought to the proper forum; the Court of Appeals presented statistics on administrative appeals. The exchange kept those appeals separate from criminal bail decisions.
  • Agriculture cases and committee action: The Court of Tax Appeals described its early work on cases under the Anti-Agriculture Economic Sabotage Act and new search-and-seizure procedures. At the close, the committee approved submission of the Judiciary budget to plenary with an unspecified allowance for increases; the motion did not quantify an appropriation change.
