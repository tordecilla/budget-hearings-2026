At a glance

  • DA and NIA funding priorities: DA presented a restoration and addition list for fertilizer, post-harvest systems, farm-to-market bridges, laboratories, solar irrigation, fisheries, livestock, coconut planting, sugar, ube and food hubs. NIA said its FY 2027 NEP allocation was far below its request and warned that reduced funding could delay ongoing irrigation work; the agencies asked the committee to consider prioritized items rather than reporting new appropriations.
  • Irrigation flexibility with safeguards: NIA asked to return to a program-based budget format and to relax a 2026 special provision that it said made it difficult to shift from stalled projects to ready work. Members recognized implementation constraints but insisted that any new rule retain validation, transparency and accountability safeguards. The committee requested NIA’s proposed language and prioritized list for later budget-amendment review.
  • Food security, rice buying and market logistics: Members examined palay buying under EO 100, NFA storage, Sagipsaka implementation, farm-to-market roads, food hubs, KADIWA and the role of public markets. DA described available rice-purchase funds, expanded warehouse capacity, food-hub plans and KADIWA sites, while several requested reports, cost details, supplier inquiries and a broader food-logistics plan remained pending.
  • Climate resilience, fisheries and local production: The discussion linked drought and disaster recovery to crop insurance, replanting inputs, soil testing, solar irrigation, fish ports, cold storage, seaweed, aquaculture and coastal market access. Agency officials described ongoing work, proposals and expected future facilities in Eastern Samar, Palawan and other areas, but many district-specific projects, funding levels and delivery dates remained conditional or unfunded.
  • Animal health, commodity support and oversight: Members pressed DA on ASF-vaccine evidence, backyard-hog recovery, coconut replanting, sugarcane pest control, ube planting material and fishery exports. The agencies described clinical documentation to be shared, prospective vaccine and recovery support, coconut-fund legislation, chemical and biocontrol responses to RSSI, and planting-material expansion. The record preserves material differences in figures and leaves clinical, funding, market-access and implementation outcomes open.
  • Closing action: After the final member remarks, Senior Deputy Speaker Ferdinand L. Hernandez moved to terminate the DA and NIA budget hearing. The chair’s closing procedure then suspended proceedings until September 2, 2026 at 9 a.m.; it did not convert agency requests or member recommendations into approved FY 2027 appropriations.

Deputy Speaker Ferjenel G. Biron, M.D.

Dumangas rice-processing facility and equipment rollout: Deputy Speaker Ferjenel Biron said a rice-processing facility promised for Dumangas three years earlier had still not been implemented, despite the local farmers' organization and cooperatives completing the required feasibility study and audited financial statements. Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel Jr. acknowledged the delay and said a rice-processing system had been allocated. PhilMech Director Dionisio Alvindia said the planned warehouse would have a 1,200-square-meter floor area, its bidding had been posted that month, and it would include 12-ton dryers; after the warehouse, a Rice Processing System 2 with a 3-ton-per-hour input capacity would be installed. He said contractors had 150 days for the warehouse, and Tiu Laurel stated a June 2027 commitment for the RPS. Alvindia confirmed the requested tractors, combined harvesters, dryers, forklifts, and other equipment, but said hauling trucks still lacked DBM clearance. He said bidding for tractors and combined harvesters was complete and distribution was scheduled for October through December that year. Tiu Laurel said those machines would come first, while the RPS was committed for June 2027.

00:22:58 · asr-000102

ASF vaccine safety and efficacy documentation: Biron asked whether the DA had completed a conclusive clinical trial demonstrating the safety and efficacy of its ASF vaccine. DA Undersecretary Constante Palabrica said 500,000 doses had been distributed, reported 85% to 90% efficacy, and said vaccinated pigs had responded well. Biron said the clinical-trial documentation was necessary, citing what he described as an earlier non-recommendation by two FDA veterinary experts and emphasizing the stakes for the pork industry. Palabrica said the Bureau of Animal Industry had capable veterinarians, maintained the vaccine was sound, and agreed to send Biron's office all documents intended to show the vaccine's effectiveness.

Rep. Carl Nicolas C. Cari

Climate adaptation and limits on government response: Rep. Carl Nicolas C. Cari asked whether DA programmes and resources were keeping pace with the risks that climate change and global warming posed to farmers. DA Secretary Francisco P. Tiu Laurel Jr. said climate change was already being felt but admitted that government could not react quickly enough because lessons from current events had to be funded for implementation in the following year. He said DA was applying lessons from an earlier El Niño through mega and modular cold storage, NIA and DA solar irrigation, and solar pumps replacing engine-driven pumps, while cautioning that catching up remained difficult.

00:33:57 · asr-000209

Farm recovery after floods and other disasters: Cari asked what immediate financial assistance was available after floods, landslides or other severe-weather damage, and how quickly it reached family farmers before the next planting season. Laurel cited Philippine Crop Insurance Corporation coverage, for which farmers must apply each cropping season, and said indemnity checks normally issue within 21 days of a farm being hit, although verification can cause delays. He said regional centres pre-position seeds and fertilizer for replanting, including in flood-affected Region III, and cited SURE low-interest loans up to PHP 35,000, loan restructuring and, in worst cases, presidential assistance for farmers and fisherfolk.

00:37:06 · asr-000240

Soil testing and post-flood technical support: Cari asked whether farmers could obtain free or subsidized soil testing and technical help to decide when land was ready to plant after severe flooding. Laurel said DA had 17 mobile soil laboratories in addition to fixed laboratories, up from about five previously, and that testing was free at the time of the hearing. He said capacity was still insufficient, but farmers could access the service. He added that land could normally be replanted once floodwater had receded, while soil testing could better identify nutrient needs and reduce costs.

00:39:04 · asr-000262

Palawan fish landing, cold storage and seaweed support: Cari asked what FY 2027 production and livelihood support would reach Palawan fishing communities dependent on West Philippine Sea grounds, including fuel, ice, gear, insurance and market support. Laurel described a PFDA port near Puerto Princesa, said to cover about 15 hectares and include an ice plant, cold storage and blast freezers, connected to Ulugan Bay so fish could be landed, moved by road, processed and sent to markets; he said completion was expected the following year. He also described a planned seaweed bioreactor for propagation material in Palawan. Cari raised the immediate lack of cold storage on the west coast, where fishers in barangays named in the source could lose catch to buyers before reaching the mainland. Laurel said the 2027 budget included solar-powered ice plants of five to 10 tons per day, with 10–15 units for North and Southern Palawan, plus cold storage and freezers near Puerto Princesa as part of a plan to move fresh seafood to processing and tourist markets.

00:40:24 · asr-000280

Backyard-hog biosecurity and swine recovery: Rep. Carl Nicolas Cari asked what 2027 funding would expand community pig-raising facilities, speed financial payouts for ASF-affected farmers, and teach low-cost farm protection. Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel Jr. said the DA had requested an additional P1 billion for vaccines that could be used for ASF or avian influenza during an outbreak. Cari cited biosecurity guidance from Visayas State University and P4.9 million in indemnification aid distributed to neighboring hog raisers in Southern Leyte, and asked the DA to coordinate with the university, ATI, and local governments in Leyte's 5th District on low-cost training and a potential swine-recovery facility. Tiu Laurel agreed to coordinate on training and said the DA could include the district in its Inspire 600 program, which he described as biosecure designs for backyard-farmer or hog clusters at P12.5 million or P13.5 million per setup.

01:01:34 · asr-000516

Drought response for affected farms: Cari asked what direct financial and equipment support would be available for farmers affected by a prolonged El Niño dry spell. Tiu Laurel said that where soil moisture was completely exhausted and the area met an agricultural-drought condition, the DA had little it could do directly; DSWD and DOLE would provide interim help, including work preparing land for the next cropping season. For areas that could still be planted but not with usual crops, he said the DA would support alternatives such as corn for drier areas and malunggay, with other seeds and fertilizer for crops more resistant to drought and heat.

01:04:34 · asr-000540

Coconut replanting and access to Coco Levy funds: Cari asked how the PCA and DA would use the 2027 budget and Coconut Farmers and Industry Trust Fund to reduce bottlenecks and deliver fertilizer and planting materials on time amid volatile copra prices. Tiu Laurel said the PCA's 2027 budget was mostly for replanting under a 100-million-tree mission, with about 40 million trees expected to be planted by year-end. He said the more important step was to unlock Coco Levy funds through a new bill being advanced in Congress and the Senate, so they could finance massive replanting, facilities, value adding, scholarships, and education for coconut farmers.

01:06:11 · asr-000548

NIA repair requests after typhoon damage: Cari asked what 2027 NIA funding and quick-release mechanism would let regional offices repair typhoon-damaged canals, intake gates, and water delivery before a planting season was lost. Administrator Eduardo Eddie Guillen said NIA had requested P2.8 billion for the district but only P66 million had been approved, citing a repair project whose name is unclear in the frozen source. He said NIA had also submitted requests to the Office of Civil Defense for repairs not funded in its allocation, including typhoon damage, and that several projects in Cari's district had been included in the OCD request.

00:46:25 · asr-000338

Blue swimming crab export access and dolphin-interaction safeguards: Guintu raised the U.S. denial of comparability findings for two blue swimming crab fisheries, identified in the exchange as fisheries 2129 and 2130, and asked what government was doing to protect exports and crab fishers. DA Undersecretary Drusila Esther E. Bayate said the issue arose under the U.S. Marine Mammal Protection Act because crab gill nets, pots, and traps could interact with Irrawaddy dolphins. She said the blue crab meat export fishery was concentrated in the Visayan Sea, Guimaras Strait, and Malampaya Sound, with artisanal fishers under LGU jurisdiction. Bayate said the Philippines had issued a fisheries administrative order limiting gill-net length and was helping LGUs make local ordinances to reduce interactions; DA planned to report an update to the United States in the last quarter of the year. Guintu said Western Visayas had 6,500 crab raisers, including 2,600 in his district, and that the affected U.S. market represented 90% of their market, making restored access urgent.

EO 100 palay prices, buying funds and storage capacity: Rep. Sarah Jane I. Elago asked how Executive Order No. 100 would establish a palay floor price and protect farmers. DA Secretary Francisco P. Tiu Laurel Jr. said its government buying-price application could not be imposed on the private sector. 01:27:24 · asr-000699

EO 101, Sagipsaka implementation and supplier concerns: Elago connected EO 100 to EO 101 and full implementation of the Sagipsaka Act, asking what DA would spend to require government entities and LGUs to buy food and agricultural products directly from farmer and fisherfolk cooperatives and to establish Sagipsaka desks. 01:35:15 · asr-000827

Farm roads, public markets and food-logistics access: Elago asked whether farm-to-market-road selection ensured that roads reached markets, warning that public markets were declining while large retailers expanded. Laurel said the master plan is intended to connect to markets, but a PHP 16 billion allocation would only cover about 1,000 kilometres a year against a 55,000-kilometre gap. He cited road influence area, poverty incidence, beneficiaries, network distance, road condition, climate adaptation and traffic among the criteria, alongside routes opening production areas or serving isolated barangays. Elago argued that public markets support jobs, local agriculture and affordable food and asked for a stronger national role in their upkeep. Laurel welcomed the idea but said DA was then concentrating on mega food hubs that would link municipal trading areas and markets; Elago asked for the food-logistics plan and noted its three-year horizon and funding shortfall.

01:38:51 · asr-000873

Food hubs and KADIWA links for producers and buyers: Elago asked how a national logistics chain would connect farms and fisheries to markets and households beyond the Clark food hub, including Visayas and Mindanao. Laurel described 2026 food hubs in Clark, a location rendered as “Kawayan,” Isabela and Pili, Camarines Sur, and listed further 2027 locations and amounts in the source, including a nearly PHP 1 billion Manolo Fortich, Bukidnon hub. He said the hubs would converge farmer, fisher and livestock products with laboratories, cold storage and other storage, but these were an initial network rather than a completed nationwide system. On market-day access for small producers, Elago asked about DA coordination with DILG and LGUs. Laurel said KADIWA worked with LGUs to provide sites, had grown from fewer than about 20 sites when he entered office to around 800 largely fixed sites, and linked local cooperatives and farmers directly to buyers.

Elago pressed DA to take a clear position and to pursue compensation for lost daily income, not food packs alone.

Immediate chemical response to RSSI infestation: Rep. Julienne 'Jam' Baronda asked how many sugarcane farms were affected by red-striped soft-scale insect (RSSI), whether all affected farms could receive pesticides, and what funding was needed. She said a pesticide container could cost as much as P10,000 per hectare and urged cheaper, nature-based control. Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel Jr. reported 134,000 hectares affected as of August 14, with 67,000 hectares validated, and said the DA was providing funds to local governments for chemical control because biocontrol production was too slow for the current infestation. SRA Administrator Pablo Luis Azcona said chemical pesticide averaged about P2,000 per liter but cost about P1,000 per hectare per application, disputing the P10,000 figure. He said the DA and SRA had provided P209 million, including P23 million for chemical pesticides and the remainder for fertilizer for recovering fields, with a further P10 million from the DA for pesticides. He said chemicals sufficient for about 35,000 hectares had been distributed and estimated that another P120 million was needed for an initial application, which would require roughly two sprayings. Azcona later gave 134,000 reported hectares and 67,443 verified hectares.

01:56:39 · asr-001274

Biocontrol laboratories and regionally targeted quick response: Asked about lower-cost and nature-based options, Azcona said the SRA and DA had produced an RSSI-killing fungus since 2025. He described it as costing about P150 per hectare and then said it amounted to P1,500 per hectare when applied, while stressing that production was very slow because SRA laboratory capacity was limited. He said the Secretary had instructed DA and other government laboratories and state universities to help produce the fungus; Region 10 had done so successfully, and discussions with Negros local governments would lead to proposals for LGU-based laboratories. Azcona said the DA had earmarked P300 million to establish laboratories producing the agents. For immediate action before the 2027 budget, he said P35 million would be released directly to local governments for chemical control, distributed by verified affected area and limited to Negros Island Region; additional resources would be needed for Luzon and Bukidnon. He gave about P120 million as the initial first-response need across regions, with further quick-response funding from the DA and SRA as needed.
