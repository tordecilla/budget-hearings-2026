At a glance

  • The FY 2027 case: DOE sought PHP 5.58 billion, up 25.74% from its FY 2026 GAA, citing a national resource survey, mobile energy systems and digitalization. ERC presented a PHP 1.7-billion proposal against a PHP 944.065-million NEP allocation and argued that the gap would affect rate resets, case resolution and consumer protection.
  • Energy security and affordability: Officials described fuel-supply measures during the Middle East crisis, plans for a PNOC strategic petroleum reserve, emergency energy systems for islands and disasters, renewable and indigenous-resource development, and proposed action on generator accountability and system loss. Members repeatedly questioned whether these measures would produce lower bills and more reliable service for consumers.
  • Electric cooperatives and last-mile service: Members focused on franchise renewals, CBA rules, debt and performance of cooperatives, rate and capacity constraints, and the gap in funding for sitio and household electrification. Officials described prioritizing Mindanao and considering solar-and-battery alternatives where line extension would be costly or increase technical losses.
  • Regulatory and market accountability: The ERC was questioned on consumer complaints, Meralco refunds, WESM line rentals, ancillary charges, rate resets and distribution-utility oversight. Its officials committed to submissions, audits, case work and consultation, while members sought faster decisions and the resources to complete long-delayed rate resets.
  • Projects that remain contingent: Discussion covered offshore wind ports and auctions, transmission upgrades, data-center supply, nuclear readiness and foreign-linked technical-assistance and storage proposals. Several matters remained studies, draft guidelines, conditional investments or requests for written clarification rather than approved projects or completed outcomes.
  • Budget follow-up: DOE, NEA and ERC sought restoration or augmentation of selected funding and special provisions, including ERC consultancy support and NEA technical staffing. The committee requested prioritized submissions and proposed wording for review before plenary budget deliberations.

DOE and Energy Regulatory Commission presentation

DOE energy-security and electrification results: Undersecretary Giovanni Carlo J. Bacordo said DOE's response to the Middle East energy emergency combined fuel-supply measures, staggered price adjustments, subsidies, anti-hoarding enforcement, efficiency measures and support for transport and agriculture; he reported an average 50-day fuel supply. It said the Green Energy Auction Program had awarded 20.7 GW of renewable capacity, with another 25 GW planned for auction from 2027 to 2035; 187 renewable projects endorsed through the Green Lane program since 2023 were said to amount to PHP 5.46 trillion.

Forward-looking rate setting and capital expenditure recovery: Nazal asked ERC why consumers were charged rates based on projected capital investments before projects were complete. ERC Chairperson and CEO Francis Saturnino C. Juan said Section 43 of EPIRA allows internationally accepted performance-based rate-setting methodologies. He described performance-based regulation as forward-looking, in contrast with the historical-cost RORB approach: revenue requirements take account of efficient operating expenses, the regulatory asset base, and capital expenditure over the regulatory period, normally four years and five years for NGCP. 01:56:30 · asr-001825

Rate-reset accountability for delayed or unbuilt projects: Nazal asked what happens when a project used in rate-setting is delayed or not done. Juan said the rate reset at the end of each four- or five-year regulatory period checks whether the utility completed its CAPEX; delayed or uncompleted CAPEX is flowed back by reducing the next revenue requirement, with penalties and interest. He said this accountability mechanism had not operated for more than a decade because ERC had not reset tariffs for private distribution utilities, but that ERC had resumed the exercise that year. Juan said private utilities had meanwhile made individual CAPEX filings and, where projects were approved and implemented, had spent without a rate adjustment; the current resets were intended to correct the accumulated inaction. On Nazal's final question, Juan said a flowback would be deducted from the following period's revenue requirement.
