At a glance

  • Funding gap: The agency presentation put the 2027 NEP at ₱11.616 billion: ₱7.267 billion for DMW's Office of the Secretary and ₱4.349 billion for OWWA. Each sought a further ₱2 billion. DBM emphasized increases in broader welfare-program totals; the agencies stressed reduced emergency line items and needs created by the conflict.
  • Revised figures requested: Mika Suansing asked for needs-based 2027 projections and remaining UPLIFT balances, due by the end of the week or the first half of the next week for budget-amendment review. No final augmentation amount was approved.
  • Crisis assistance: DMW sought to raise AKSYON from ₱2 billion to ₱3 billion. OWWA challenged the roughly ₱900 million emergency-repatriation provision after much higher crisis spending. Cacdac committed to covering pending AKSYON claims by early in the fourth quarter.
  • Returnees and families: Members pressed for measurable reintegration outcomes, advance profiling, stronger livelihood support and services for children left behind. Officials described existing training and family programs, a proposed interagency aftercare framework, and continued medical and legal help even after a reintegration business fails.
  • Hospital and maritime training: The OFW Hospital sought ₱200 million more for services and expansion. NMP defended free regular courses despite a capital-led budget reduction and sought funds for Tacloban capacity; proposed Cavite and Davao centers remained in preparation.
  • Protection and access: Questions covered trafficking and scam hubs, seafarer compensation, mental-health care, verification fees and database security. DMW committed to occupying a Catanduanes office; third-doctor guidelines with DOH and stronger mental-health staffing remained work in progress.

Agency presentation

Protection and service delivery: DMW Secretary Hans Leo J. Cacdac described DMW as a home for OFWs and their families, emphasizing practical help as well as case totals. He reported roughly 80,000 financial-assistance recipients, 14,000 legal cases and 5,000 medical-assistance cases; DMW and OWWA had brought home about 12,000 people from conflict-affected countries, while around 20,000 others received US$200 assistance after income losses. He also cited about 150,000 OFW Hospital service recipients over three years, nearly 50,000 reintegration beneficiaries and 1.5 million OFW passes. Digital services were still being improved. Examples of counseling and help for workers’ children illustrated support extending to families.

2027 budget and operating priorities: DMW Undersecretary Dominique Rubia-Tutay presented a ₱11.616 billion NEP allocation: ₱7.267 billion for the Office of the Secretary and ₱4.349 billion for OWWA, against a departmental request of ₱37.674 billion. She reported operations at ₱9.77 billion. Priorities included the AKSYON Fund, overseas offices, reintegration, the OFW Hospital, employment facilitation, regulation and anti-trafficking work.
