At a glance

  • Budget reductions and plenary referral: The Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) faced reductions while senators sought a prioritized funding wish list and better utilization. Presiding Senator Joel Villanueva and Finance Committee Chairperson Senator Joseph Victor “JV” G. Ejercito referred the proposals to plenary, subject to requested documents and the hearing’s discussion.
  • Jobs, training and enforcement: DOLE Secretary Francis N. Tolentino described pathways from temporary assistance into skills, jobs and livelihood. Senators pressed for lasting employment outcomes, coordination with the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), and stronger inspection capacity. Officials promised utilization details, program guidelines and staffing information.
  • Wage relief and unresolved litigation: Senator Christopher Lawrence T. Go questioned wage delays and regional coverage. Tolentino said DOLE continued defending Wage Order 27 while implementing separate relief under Order 28. The additional tranche remained subject to court decisions. Villanueva’s question about a regularly updated household-income benchmark received a process-based answer.
  • Professional regulation and labor justice: Senator Raffy T. Tulfo challenged the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC) over director tenure, examination security and malpractice complaints. PRC Chairperson Charito A. Zamora promised reshuffling; Tolentino committed to fact-finding and a clinic inspection. The unfinished National Labor Relations Commission (NLRC) headquarters remained without requested project funding.

DOLE presentation

Employment priorities and labor-market conditions: Tolentino described a shift from temporary assistance toward routes into skills, livelihood, enterprise and continuing employment, alongside monthly job fairs, stronger job matching through public employment service offices (PESOs), and closer employer partnerships. DOLE Planning Service Director Patrick P. Patriwirawan Jr.’s presentation set eight priorities spanning employment generation and sustainable livelihoods, decent work and workers' rights, labor justice, social protection, social dialogue, digital transformation and a stronger, accountable Department.

Its labor-market snapshot showed the labor force growing while participation declined; unemployment and underemployment rose, employment growth lagged labor-force growth, and the number and rate of young people not in employment, education or training increased. Wage-and-salary employment grew, but the presentation characterized job quality, hours and income adequacy as continuing concerns.

SkillPass credential and referrals: SkillPass was presented as a proposed credential connecting authorized TUPAD work or Government Internship Program (GIP) experience with competency development and, where applicable, Technical Education and Skills Development Authority training, assessment and certification. Sagip Trabaho job preservation: Sagip Trabaho was described as time-bound job-preservation assistance for qualified distressed micro, small and medium enterprises (MSMEs) facing temporary shocks that could cause worker displacement or business closure. Employers must apply with a job-preservation and business-continuity plan; DOLE would validate the enterprise, worker eligibility, employer compliance and counterpart, and prospects for recovery before deciding whether assistance could prevent displacement or closure. Temporary wage support may cover up to 35 qualified workers per employer for up to 90 days.

A tripartite undertaking would specify covered workers, activities, support, monitoring and retention commitments. The intervention does not replace employers' statutory obligations. DOLE said it would monitor actual work, labor-standard compliance and recovery, assessing whether jobs were preserved and workers remained employed after assistance ended.

Tulong-Aral foundational learning: Tulong-Aral would engage qualified education graduates, licensure passers and academic mentors to provide supervised small-group reading, writing and mathematics support for Grades 1–3.
