At a glance

  • Public-safety equipment and procurement: Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) and its attached agencies faced questions about fire-truck and station gaps, unfinished infrastructure, police equipment, emergency response and procurement delays. Officials described reviews and proposed procurement steps, while some project records remained for follow-up.
  • Staffing and service quality: The discussion covered vacancies, recruitment standards, deployment, training, and part-year salary provisions for new fire officers. Members pressed agencies to reconcile staffing and funding figures and sustain service quality.
  • Local governance and community programs: Members raised barangay and Sangguniang Kabataan (SK) terms, planning and accountability, local-government implementation of early-childhood programs, and how local plans connect with national funding priorities.
  • Legislative coordination and plenary referral: DILG agreed to designate an official for legislative matters. The subcommittee requested its draft firearms proposal and referred the budget to plenary for further discussion and debate.

Senator Vicente C. Sotto III

Barangay terms, planning and accountability: Senator Vicente C. Sotto III opposed the Senate's second-reading approval of a bill extending barangay and SK officials' terms to five years and postponing the November 2026 elections, citing reported misconduct and arguing that a longer holdover would magnify officials' power. He cited the Ombudsman's dismissal of 12 barangay officials in Iloilo over an alleged scheme, alleged misuse of a ₱130 million annual budget in Pasig, and Makati Mayor Nancy Binay's report that SK officials demanded 25% for projects. He also cited a January 2026 suspension over delayed Clean and Green Program payouts, a DILG complaint against alleged 'epal' officials, and other administrative or criminal cases. These were Sotto's examples and allegations; Remulla later said they were few relative to the country's 42,000 barangays. Sotto said he had proposed an amendment to prevent barangay and SK officials with pending cases from exercising active duties, and asked Remulla for comment. Remulla described problems with SK officials leaving their posts during a term and attributed them partly to the raised age limit and to fiscal autonomy without barangay supervision. Sotto said his amendment had been carried, though it could still be revised before third reading and would need to carry through bicameral consideration and enactment. Asked how many officials had been dismissed or suspended and how many complaints came from ordinary citizens, Remulla said he did not have the list and undertook to bring it to a later deliberation. He agreed that officials with pending cases should not hold over, in response to Sotto's follow-up. Sotto contrasted the proposed five-year barangay term with the department's three-year barangay development plan and asked whether DILG would revise planning and budgeting guidance. Remulla said the term-limit deliberations had not been set when the budget was prepared and that the department would adjust to the law as passed.
