At a glance

  • The 2027 fiscal outlook: BSP, DEPDev, DOF, and DBM set out the macroeconomic and budget framework, while senators tested its assumptions against inflation, exchange-rate exposure, a slower growth path, collection performance, the deficit, and debt service. The hearing repeatedly returned to whether fiscal consolidation can proceed without cutting the investments needed for growth.
  • Food, health, and human capital: Members linked rice prices, imports, agricultural productivity, El Niño preparation, nutrition, classrooms, health financing, and shortages in health workers. Questions focused on whether appropriations, releases, and program design would deliver reliable food supply, PhilHealth obligations, local health access, and education capacity.
  • Revenue and spending quality: The discussion covered BIR and BOC target performance, tax-policy proposals, asset sales and GOCC dividends, operating-cost pressures, cash management, and the scrutiny of unused, overlapping, or low-value appropriations. Officials described collection reforms, target adjustments, and DBM reviews of agency MOOE and other requirements; senators sought reports on results and accountability.
  • Infrastructure, local government, and productive investment: Senators asked about stalled public construction, transport and railway projects, PPPs, classroom and health facilities, LGU transfers and LGSF outcomes. The economic managers described releases, planned devolution, and efforts to preserve capital outlay, while members asked for proof that spending was implemented and producing development gains.
  • Resilience and policy reform: The hearing connected energy costs, logistics and ports, digital and industrial policy, anti-scam enforcement, transparency, land use, and disaster readiness to the 2027 plan. Several members called for a more proactive approach to shocks and for legislative or administrative reforms that could make investment, service delivery, and oversight more effective.

BSP presentation

Inflation path, policy response, and outlook: Governor Eli M. Remolona Jr. said BSP's primary mandate is price stability and described global supply shocks—especially higher oil and food prices linked in the briefing to the conflict in Iran—as the immediate inflation challenge. He said BSP cannot control oil prices directly and is focused on limiting second-round effects. Deputy Governor Zeno Ronald R. Abenoja said inflation rose from 2.4% in February 2026 to 7.2% in April before easing to 6.2% in July. BSP raised its target policy rate by 25 basis points in April and again in June, from 4.25% to 4.75%, to anchor expectations and contain spillover effects while complementing fiscal measures. Abenoja recalled BSP's June forecast of average inflation at 6.4% for 2026, 4.5% for 2027, and 3.1% for 2028, while noting that the outlook would be updated after that day's Monetary Board meeting. He cited volatile oil prices and weather disturbances as risks, and said BSP remained prepared to act if needed.

DEPDev economic outlook and 2027 budget priorities

Economic conditions, jobs, and inflation: Balisacan said the proposed 2027 budget must protect earlier gains, address implementation and structural bottlenecks, and restore faster, durable, inclusive growth. He reported average real GDP growth of 5.1% a year from 2010 to 2025, versus 4.2% in the preceding 15 years, and 5.8% from 2022 to 2025. But momentum weakened in the second half of 2025 and growth slowed to 2.6% in the first half of 2026. He said the country had reached upper-middle-income status in 2025, with GNI per capita of USD4,850 above the World Bank's USD4,636 threshold, but said that status must translate into better jobs, incomes, and living standards. He attributed the slowdown largely to weak domestic demand as governance and corruption concerns and the Middle East conflict weighed on confidence: domestic demand grew 1.5% in the first half of 2026, versus 5.7% a year earlier; investment fell 6.5%, household-consumption growth eased to 2.9%, and exports grew 10%. Services remained the main source of growth while industry contracted and agriculture contributed modestly. Balisacan said the post-pandemic expansion created 4 million net jobs between the first half of 2022 and the first half of 2026, but employment then fell by 166,000 over the past year and unemployment rose from 3.1% to 4.2% in June 2026. He reported that official 2025 estimates put poverty incidence at 9.7%, down from 15.5% in 2023, with 6.5 million Filipinos lifted out of poverty through recovering incomes and employment and sustained social-protection programs. He cautioned that these gains remain vulnerable to high inflation. He said inflation had surged from March because the Middle East conflict affected global oil and commodity prices; headline inflation was 6.2% in July, down from 6.4% in June but still above the government's target range. High food prices, he said, weigh disproportionately on poor and low-income households and could reverse recent poverty-reduction gains.

Growth constraints, outlook, and 2027 investment response: Balisacan identified heavy reliance on household consumption and services as a structural constraint, with fixed investment, net exports, agriculture, and industry contributing less. He said growth must be broadened by strengthening investment and exports and revitalizing agriculture and industry; the Philippines still trails several ASEAN peers in fixed investment and exports. He argued that greater investment and export competitiveness require reliable, high-quality infrastructure. Although productive-infrastructure spending has risen, he said the spending had not fully yielded stronger infrastructure competitiveness, so project selection, appraisal, procurement, implementation, and maintenance must improve. He also said improved innovation rankings had not translated into stronger productivity relative to regional peers, calling for stronger links among research, skills development, technology adoption, commercialization, and production. Balisacan said the Philippine Development Plan remains the guide and that the administration's final two years should focus on implementation, bottlenecks, and concrete results. DEPDev projects growth of 3.5% to 4.5% in 2026 and, with faster public-program and infrastructure execution, better domestic supply conditions, and restored confidence, 5% to 6% over the following two years. He said inflation is expected to stay elevated in 2026, ease to 4% to 5% in 2027, and return to the 2% to 4% target range by 2028. He identified risks from delayed public investment and underspending, continuing governance concerns, severe weather affecting food production, the Middle East conflict, and geopolitical and trade tensions. His near-term priorities were targeted, timely help and stronger food supply for vulnerable households; catch-up plans for high-impact infrastructure; and transparency, accountability, monitoring, and efficient resource use. He said limited resources should go to implementation-ready investments in learning poverty and classrooms, future-ready skills, primary health care, farm productivity, and transport, logistics, energy, and digital infrastructure, while safeguarding fiscal sustainability through coordinated planning, budgeting, execution, spending efficiency, and tax and customs modernization.

DOF presentation

Fiscal framework, revenue performance, and non-tax revenue: DOF Secretary Frederick D. Go said the administration inherited P12.79 trillion in debt in 2022 and adopted a medium-term fiscal framework to reduce deficit and debt while supporting growth. He said the fiscal-consolidation target was to lower the deficit-to-GDP ratio from 5.4% in 2026 to 3.5% by 2030. He also said GOCCs had been asked to remit 75% of annual net earnings as dividends to the national government.

Deficit, debt management, and 2027 reforms: Go reported PHP3.175 trillion in first-half 2026 disbursements, or 49% of the full-year program, and said constrained resources should go to higher-quality expenditures with greater multiplier effects rather than simply lower spending. DBM 2027 National Expenditure Program presentation

2027 framework, programmed spending, and fiscal discipline: Acting DBM Secretary Kim Robert C. De Leon presented a proposed PHP7.2 trillion fiscal-year 2027 national budget, equal to 21.7% of GDP and described as 6% above the 2026 GAA. He said much of the increase reflects mandatory expenditures and existing commitments, including higher national tax allotments for LGUs, the fourth salary-adjustment tranche for civilian employees, and corresponding military and uniformed-personnel pay increases. De Leon stressed that the overall increase is not uniform across agencies: limited fiscal space requires choices for implementation-ready programs with the greatest impact and value. He said agencies submitted more than PHP11 trillion in requests, and DBM used actual budget performance and implementation capacity, as well as funding needs, in determining allocations. De Leon reported that, as of June 30, 2026, PHP7.54 trillion in appropriations was available, PHP6.63 trillion had been released as allotments, PHP3.29 trillion had been obligated, and 81.9% of those obligations had been disbursed. The 2027 proposal consists of PHP4.494 trillion in programmed appropriations and PHP2.706 trillion in automatic appropriations. He listed major automatic items including PHP1.32 trillion for the LGU national tax allotment and PHP1.114 trillion for interest payments. He said unprogrammed appropriations would be PHP112 billion, the lowest proposed level since 2019, and are standby authority rather than automatic spending; they are mainly limited to restoring a PDIC fund balance and foreign-assisted projects still under review. By expense class, he reported PHP2.944 trillion in MOOE, PHP1.982 trillion in personnel services, PHP1.16 trillion in capital outlays, and PHP1.114 trillion in financial expenses, while describing a gradual fiscal-consolidation path that does not reduce the deficit at the expense of growth.
