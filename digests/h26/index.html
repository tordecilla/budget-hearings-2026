At a glance

  • FY 2027 proposal: DBM recommended slightly more than the OVP had proposed for FY 2027, though the agency-specific NEP was below the FY 2026 appropriation. The presentation also identified a separate automatic retirement-premium appropriation.
  • Program delivery: The presentation described nine core initiatives, including assistance for learners, microenterprises, transport, disaster response, food, mobility aids and burial support; the OVP reported serving 1,395,867 individuals and groups in FY 2025 and 773,858 in the first half of 2026.

Office of the Vice President

Mandate, operating model and stated priorities: The Office of the Vice President (OVP) presented itself as a direct bridge to underserved and marginalized communities, working with nine agencies to extend social protection, social security, government presence and emergency relief. It said its mandate is direct access to basic social services, delivered nationally through the central office, 10 satellite offices and a Tondo extension office. The presentation described nine core initiatives for 1.4 million target beneficiaries: microenterprise grants; school supplies, rainwear and dental kits for last-mile learners; youth leadership exposure; disaster relief; family sustenance; hot meals; free transport; wheelchairs; and burial support for Islamic rites. It framed these as targeted help with household shocks, education and commuting costs, hunger, disability and bereavement.

00:04:23 · asr-000083

FY 2027 proposal and allocation structure: Atty. Kelvin Jerome Tenido said the FY 2027 agency-specific NEP recommendation was net of RLIP and 11.7% below the FY 2026 appropriation. The presentation put FY 2027 personnel services at PHP 235.776 million including RLIP (29.4% of the total including automatic appropriations), PHP 561.651 million for maintenance and other operating expenses (70.1%), and PHP 3.3 million for capital outlay (0.41%), chiefly for ICT and equipment under the OVP information-systems plan. It also identified PHP 193.103 million, or 24.6% of the FY 2027 NEP agency-specific budget, for goods and grants under socioeconomic programs, excluding logistics and other operational costs. The office attributed a PHP 102.147 million proposed-budget decrease from FY 2026 primarily to use of the 2025 budget level and cost adjustments, including mandated salary increases. In later questioning, Rep. Sarah Jane I. Elago cited PHP 220.232 million for personnel services, and Atty. Rosalynne L. Sanchez described the amount as about PHP 220 million. Those figures arose in questioning; Tenido's PHP 235.776 million presentation figure included RLIP.

Program rules and the You Can Be VP allocation: Rep. Antonio L. Tinio questioned whether the OVP's directly implemented discretionary programs had clear, publicly accessible guidelines. Undersecretary Sulaika T. Lopez said all nine core programs were covered by operations manuals, regularly updated in consultation with the Commission on Audit; she added that some procedures were in the citizens' charter. Tinio noted that You Can Be VP lacked detail online and questioned its proposed PHP 1.1 million allocation for two target beneficiaries, which appeared to imply more than PHP 500,000 each. Lopez acknowledged that the program was not in the citizens' charter and referred the details to Director Norman Valora. Valora said the two learners were targets for local or international engagements with the Vice President, but the program also included a Junior Leadership Program; he said five participants had recently received skills development aimed at helping them mentor other young learners. He said the apparent PHP 500,000 was not a payment to an individual recipient but would support other program activities, and that the two-person count was an eligibility target rather than the whole scope of mentoring and skills activities.

Passenger counts and transport costs: Pressed on how nine buses could produce the 1.2 million beneficiary figure, Valora said the OVP counted boardings rather than unique individuals. Magnegosyo grants, outcomes and oversight records: Elago argued that parliamentary courtesy should be removed from budget deliberations involving the highest offices, regardless of a proposal's size, so that every programme, activity and peso receives meaningful scrutiny. She then asked how Magnegosyo assistance was distributed among nearly 10,000 reported beneficiaries since 2023 and whether recipients received uniform amounts or proposal-based assistance. Director Norman Valora said individual grantees receive P15,000 each, while civil-society organizations receive about P150,000 based on their feasibility studies; he said CSOs undergo OVP accreditation and groups have a minimum 20 to 25 clients or members. Asked for measurable evidence that enterprises grew or were sustained, Valora said the OVP monitors individual grants after 90 days, acknowledged that not all recipients succeed, and said it sometimes refers them to appropriate agencies. He committed to provide the committee a list of clients and beneficiaries. Elago also requested the programme manual of operations and success indicators, citing the need to avoid duplication with similar assistance and to show that subsidies reach their intended purpose.
