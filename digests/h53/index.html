At a glance

  • Budget scope and follow-up: The FY2027 National Expenditure Program (NEP) hearing covered the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT), Cybercrime Investigation and Coordinating Center (CICC), National Privacy Commission (NPC), and National Telecommunications Commission (NTC). The hearing compared FY2026 General Appropriations Act (GAA) funding with new proposals. Their presentations gave program funding, revenues and additional requests, but omitted whole-agency NEP and original-request totals. Subcommittee Chair Senator Pia S. Cayetano sought funding priorities and full connectivity and data-center cost plans. She was ready to submit the DICT budget to plenary; additional requests remained for consideration.
  • Connectivity and emergency access: DICT described the National Broadband Program and Philippine Digital Infrastructure Project as building backbone and middle-mile links, alongside free public internet, Bayanihan SIM access, and emergency communications for communities and public-service sites. Presentations covered schools, government facilities, disaster-prone areas, and remote communities, with gaps and future implementation still under discussion.
  • Digital government and workforce: The hearing covered the e-government app, digital identity, local-government services, and government data-center infrastructure, as well as digital-skills training and pathways into information and communications technology (ICT) work. Members raised practical access to computers, long-term platform security, local technical capacity, and staffing needed to sustain government systems.
  • Cybersecurity, complaints, and privacy: DICT outlined cybersecurity monitoring, incident response, prevention, and institutional readiness. CICC described a no-wrong-door approach to cybercrime complaints, case development, referrals, and proactive monitoring of scams, child exploitation, and illegal recruitment. NPC presented public education, breach response, registration improvements, and a planned complaints chatbot. Members urged responsible artificial intelligence (AI) use, information sharing, and human involvement in sensitive cases; proposed AI tools and capacity improvements remain subject to resources and implementation.
  • Telecommunications regulation and consumer service: NTC described rulemaking for connectivity and spectrum, site blocking, regional mobile licensing, public information work, and handling of scam, telecommunications, and lost-device complaints. The presentation also distinguished collections remitted to the National Treasury from Spectrum User Fees (SUF) earmarked for specified digital programs.
