Dangerous Drugs Board (DDB)

Dangerous Drugs Board (DDB): Teodoro framed the Dangerous Drugs Board (DDB) as coordinating prevention, treatment, rehabilitation and reintegration alongside enforcement, rather than operating the whole treatment system or acting primarily as an arresting agency. He said families may seek treatment and recovery support, and argued that a national strategy should connect separate government programs.

Teodoro cited the 2023 National Household Survey, saying current drug-use prevalence fell from 2.05% in 2019 to 1.71% in 2023, representing approximately 1.4 million people. He cautioned that needs differ: some people may require residential treatment, others outpatient or community-based rehabilitation, and others continuing support. He said data should help government identify what help is needed, where it is needed, and how people can reach it.

Teodoro said the Department of Health was best positioned to report national treatment capacity, including beds, occupancy, waiting lists, outpatient services, personnel, equipment and facility needs, while DDB should bring information together under a national strategy. He described enforcement, health and treatment, local community programs, social support and reintegration as work distributed across agencies and local governments. He also said NEP-recognized rehabilitation funding extends beyond DDB's regular appropriation through statutory funding sources involving Philippine Charity Sweepstakes Office (PCSO) and Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corporation (PAGCOR) revenues. He described the intended result as accessible treatment, support after rehabilitation and effective community programs.

Teodoro argued that anti-drug policy should distinguish people who profit from illegal drugs from people seeking to recover from dependence, pairing enforcement with help and a path back to the community. He concluded by urging the House to approve DDB's proposed FY2027 budget.

Philippine Drug Enforcement Agency (PDEA)

Philippine Drug Enforcement Agency (PDEA): Teodoro framed the budget around public safety and said drug syndicates had moved beyond street-level distribution to cross-border operations using cargo planes, airports, international parcels, ports, coastlines and open water. He described PDEA's statutory role as conducting anti-drug operations and intelligence work, identifying high-value people and organizations, coordinating interdiction, and making it harder for illegal drugs to enter and circulate in communities. He cited airports, seaports, coastlines and mail and parcel systems as major entry points, and described a June 2025 interdiction off Subic involving approximately 1,484.65 kilograms of shabu. Teodoro reported that in 2025, 84 percent of PDEA-initiated arrests involved high-value targets, against a 60 percent target; the agency conducted 696 high-impact operations against a target of 500; and 73.88 percent of drug-related information and reports acted on led to actual operations. He said the next step was to reach organizations that finance, import and distribute drugs, identify trafficking routes earlier, and strengthen interdiction before shipments reached communities. He emphasized weakening syndicates and preventing drug entry, rather than merely increasing arrests, and said this would sustain disruption of organized networks.

Teodoro said PDEA's work was strongest when integrated with agencies responsible for borders and national security, with immediate operational coordination across the country's long coastline and many ports of entry. He named the Bureau of Customs, Philippine Coast Guard, Philippine Navy, military intelligence, airport authorities, local law enforcement and international partners, and said information should move quickly between agencies. He argued that continued budget support should produce disciplined intelligence work, stronger interdiction, closer coordination and measurable results against major drug organizations, with the practical goals of making drug entry and syndicate operations harder and communities safer.
