## Department of Finance budget

### FY2027 department-wide allocation and appropriation structure

For FY2027, the Department of Finance (DOF) proposal comprised PHP28.6 billion in new general appropriations and PHP3.4 billion in automatic appropriations, including retirement and life-insurance premiums and special accounts. Finance Secretary Frederick D. Go said the department-wide allocation was 3.49% below the FY2026 General Appropriations Act (GAA), a decrease of PHP1.159 billion. 

Go described the appropriation mix as about 52% for personnel services, 35% for maintenance and other operating expenses (MOOE), about 13% for capital outlays, and less than 1% for financial expenses. Digitalization and modernization were priorities across agencies, with PHP3.8 billion described for transforming processes and integrating systems.

At year-end 2025, DOF-wide obligations stood at 87% and 90% of obligations had been disbursed. As of July 31, 2026, 49% of available FY2026 GAA allotments had been obligated, and 75% of those obligations disbursed. Go said the Office of the Secretary had earned an unmodified Commission on Audit (COA) opinion for five consecutive years, and emphasized timely implementation and prudent execution.

### National debt outlook and debt management

Ejercito cited national debt of PHP19.39 trillion as of July 2026, debt-to-GDP at 66%, a projection above PHP21.48 trillion by end-2027, and PHP3.3 trillion in expected borrowing for the coming fiscal year. ### BIR audit safeguards and letters of authority

Senator Go raised complaints that letters of authority (LOAs) had been used repeatedly or abusively against taxpayers and asked what safeguards had changed. BIR Commissioner Charlito Martin R. Mendoza said the BIR had suspended audits for two months after complaints, reviewed procedures with business stakeholders, and issued new audit guidance and a revised audit manual. The reforms included a single audit per taxable year across tax types, ending separate VAT audits; priority audit selection shifted from regional directors to a system-assisted, risk-based process. He stressed that an LOA requests documents after initial red flags and is not a finding of liability; an audit can end without a deficiency.

Mendoza also described anonymized case assignment within regions, with taxpayer identity disclosed once an examiner and group supervisor were assigned. He acknowledged deployment delays as the new process was introduced. He said discrepancy discussions with taxpayers are recorded in meeting minutes before an assessment notice, and described a website LOA verifier where taxpayers can enter their tax identification number to check whether an LOA is legitimate. He acknowledged that abuse continued and was not fully eliminated, while saying complaints should be reported directly for action. Asked whether personnel had been investigated, Mendoza reported ongoing investigations, preliminary evaluations, formal charges, and administrative investigations.

DOF Secretary Frederick D. Go said the number of complaints reaching him had fallen under Mendoza's leadership, but confirmed that complaints about harassment or corruption still came in. He supported continuing reforms to make procedures simpler and faster.
