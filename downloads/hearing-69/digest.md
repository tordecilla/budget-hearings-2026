### Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC)

For FY2027, the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) National Expenditure Program (NEP) allocation was about ₱944 million; its original FY2027 request was about ₱1.7 billion, with the sponsor confirming that NEP figure was ₱762 million (44.7%) below the request. Representative Presley C. De Jesus and sponsor Representative Arnie B. Fuentebella also compared total available appropriations of about ₱1.83 billion for FY2026 and ₱944.1 million for FY2027, describing the reduction as 12.8%; that percentage does not reconcile with the stated totals and is retained as said. The sponsor linked the reduction mainly to maintenance and operating expenses and capital outlay, including rate-reset consultancies, information-technology modernization and case-resolution capacity, and agreed that cuts could slow rate resets. The sponsor separately sought ₱200 million for rate-reset consultancies, said ERC requested ₱700 million and would accept ₱250 million if provided; De Jesus expressed support for the ERC budget to speed rate resets and capital-expenditure review. The sponsor reported FY2025 collections of ₱2.354 billion against a ₱1.067 billion collection target. De Jesus asked for a special provision allowing ERC to use collections for regulation and recalled a possible ₱700 million retention cap; the bill remained pending in the Senate, so this mechanism was not yet in effect. Asked by Representative Sarah Jane I. Elago about staffing, sponsor Fuentebella said private firms recruit trained technical staff with better offers and appealed to strengthen ERC’s staffing and expertise, contrasting the trillion-peso industry with about ₱900 million for the regulator. This rounded figure was the sponsor’s comparison, not another agency principal total. ERC’s opening presentation stated no overall request or NEP allocation; the original request and NEP comparison above came in questioning.

### Department of Transportation (DOTr)

No overall FY2027 Department of Transportation (DOTr) original agency-request total was stated. Representative Antonio L. Tinio cited ₱251.59 billion as the General Appropriations Bill (GAB) total and ₱232.23 billion for 14 foreign-assisted projects, without specifying whether the larger total included attached bodies. He compared the FY2024 NEP foreign-assisted-project proposal of ₱161 billion with ₱3.5 billion in the GAA, alleging the cut created room for insertions and calling diversion of loan-tied funds unlawful; sponsor Representative Edwin Ongchuan described delays, unpaid contractors and commitment fees, and said a more representative amendment subcommittee might make cuts harder, without guaranteeing it. For the Japan International Cooperation Agency (JICA)-financed Metro Rail Transit Line 3 (MRT-3) rehabilitation, Tinio cited ₱1.942 billion; Ongchuan said it was limited to MRT-3, while a separate unprogrammed-appropriation issue remained before the Supreme Court.

For FY2027 projects, Representative Jesus “Bong” C. Suntay described ₱197.3 billion (about 82% of five principal operating programs) for rail and about ₱191.2 billion (75.9% of the Office of the Secretary (OSEC)) for the North-South Commuter Railway (NSCR) and Metro Manila Subway; he elsewhere gave ₱191.3 billion for the pair, without reconciling it. He cited ₱123.8 billion for NSCR, and compared rail figures of ₱77.9 billion (2025), ₱55.3 billion (2026) and ₱197.3 billion (FY2027), calling the change ₱141.96 billion or 256.5%; the bases were not established as identical. Suntay described the NSCR integrated cost as ₱440.881 billion initially, ₱777.55 billion in 2018 and ₱873.61 billion in 2022, a stated ₱432.73 billion or 98.2% rise. Suntay asked for total government exposure, interest, commitment fees and penalties on foreign-assisted loans, and total accomplishments; Ongchuan distinguished construction progress from right-of-way measures and different project segments. He reported overall construction at 57.11% in July 2025 and 62.33% in July 2026, and the Malolos–Valenzuela reach separately at 82.49% and 87.8%, respectively. He said payments followed progress billing and agreed to provide cost, financing and accomplishment data, but gave no total exposure or interest amount. Ongchuan said the planned NSCR length had expanded and DOTr sought additional funding to protect it from being defunded. On the subway figure, Suntay described the FY2027 allocation as a 230.8% increase. Ongchuan gave the 33-km Valenzuela-to-airport line a last-quarter 2031 operating target, and 2033 as the NSCR north section full-operation target. For the subway, Suntay stated one FY2027 NEP figure of ₱67.442 billion (also rounded to ₱67.44 billion), comprising ₱26.71 billion in loans and ₱40.73 billion in government counterpart funding; he requested milestone, billing and right-of-way (ROW) data, and Ongchuan undertook to submit it by Friday. Historical amounts cited were ₱68.4 billion (2024), ₱7.8 billion (2025) and ₱20.38 billion (2026). A separate DOTr distribution account gave 72.4% rail under OSEC, 6.7% maritime infrastructure and 6.1% land transport, plus ₱18,137 million for maritime infrastructure; those are the sponsor’s stated bases, not a reconciliation of Suntay’s shares.

Representative Sergio C. Dagooc’s question on operator loan repayment elicited that FY2026 included ₱1.5 billion amortization support, with ₱600 million for public utility vehicle (PUV) loans at ₱20,000 monthly for three months and about ₱450 million for fuel discounts at roughly ₱12 per liter; proposed FY2027 amortization was ₱1.7 billion. Ongchuan said the FY2027 NEP omitted DOTr’s proposed ₱5 billion fuel subsidy and separate ₱5 billion Service Contracting Program (SCP) (₱4 billion land, ₱1 billion maritime); FY2026 maritime service contracting had ₱200 million, while its FY2027 component was omitted. Dagooc reported that the FY2026 maritime service reached Dinagat and Siargao for ten days and reduced fares by ₱60. He sought restoration and Ongchuan supported seeking it, with no adoption. Representative Percival “Perci” Cendaña asked about absent FY2027 EDSA Carousel funding; Ongchuan said a ₱1.7 billion restoration request had been submitted. The FY2027 service-contracting and fuel-subsidy programs remained unfunded; Cendaña supported restoring them, with no amount stated in that exchange. Separate accounts described a possible ₱20,000 monthly PUV amortization subsidy, a current ₱450 million fuel-discount program at ₱12 per liter with Department of Energy (DOE) assistance, and a ₱20 million Muntinlupa road-safety park. Continuation of the fuel discount in 2027 depended on Congress funding the fuel-subsidy program. The Road Safety Action Plan had no allocated funds; Cendaña pressed for support and Ongchuan said he relied on Congress, while proposing school and student-permit road-safety instruction and the park as a model for replication in Cebu, Bohol and Iloilo. Cendaña’s right-of-way (ROW) question elicited ₱2.7 billion NEP for NSCR and a stated ₱30 billion 2027 requirement, but the answer did not clarify whether the latter covered NSCR alone or both NSCR and the subway. In a separate timeline exchange, Ongchuan said inadequate ROW funding would delay both projects by at least two years, estimating NSCR completion in 2033–2035 and the subway in 2031–2033; if DOTr received the additional funding it sought, the previous timelines might still be met. These were conditional estimates, not an adopted schedule change.

The Maritime Industry Authority (MARINA) FY2027 Maritime Industry Regulatory and Supervision Program was ₱904 million, including ₱61.268 million for ship inspection and enforcement. Representative Dadah Kiram Ismula’s questions prompted a promise to collect certification, suspension and violation data from MARINA regional offices. Representative Sarah Jane I. Elago questioned relocation funding and facilities. Sponsor Representative Reynante U. Arrogancia said projects would not proceed without clear relocation sites and that 27,333 planned housing units matched 27,333 affected households; Phase 2 funding was awaited for four of six South Long Haul projects. Elago urged more funding for the National Housing Authority (NHA), cited the reported 11.28% of housing awards, and asked for school, livelihood, utility and site details while advocating on-site relocation where possible. Arrogancia said the sites had needed facilities and child-development-center funds. Elago cited ₱10,000–₱15,000 assistance per affected family; Arrogancia identified the Right-of-Way Fund and government funds as sources. Her NHA funding appeal was unquantified; the FY2027 NEP fuel-subsidy entry was ₱0 and no restoration amount was stated. For active transport, Tinio identified ₱33.856 million FY2027 NEP against DOTr’s ₱1.2 billion request and restoration proposal, not an additional amount; the current allocation was estimated for about 5 km versus about 180 km under the request. Historical figures cited were ₱1.316 billion during Bayanihan II and a separate ₱1.09 billion/498-km calculation whose basis was not reconciled; the series for 2022–26 was ₱2 billion, ₱705 million, ₱1 billion, ₱60 million and ₱69 million. Ongchuan gave pathway estimates of ₱10 million/km (Class 1), ₱5.4 million/km (Class 2) and ₱2.5 million/km (Class 3); Tinio’s distinct calculation was about ₱2.1 million/km. No restoration outcome was recorded.

### Rep. Presley C. De Jesus

Rate-reset and capital-expenditure backlogs. De Jesus cited more than a decade of delay in distribution rate resets and a roughly 12-year lag for electric-cooperative capital-expenditure applications, with the oldest dating to 2013–2014. Fuentebella said ERC had made 170 capital-expenditure decisions in 2026. On a previously reported backlog of 107 applications involving 62 cooperatives, he cited 82 cooperative cases and an average of about 30 new cases each year, and offered to submit decision data.

Under the revised rules, ERC restarted at the first regulatory period. Fuentebella said ERC had issued a final decision on Cotabato Light’s first regulatory-period application and resolved the CEPALCO case. Proceedings involving Meralco and Dagupan Electric Corporation remained open, with completion targeted within 2026. He said the remaining private-utility groups were under review. Separately, he gave a first-quarter-2027 target for the electric-cooperative rate-reset schedule, while the ongoing private distribution-utility resets proceeded under the current 2026–2029 regulatory period. De Jesus asked when consumers would see revised distribution rates; Fuentebella said an ERC decision would take effect in the next billing cycle.

Transmission review and capital recovery. De Jesus asked about the National Grid Corporation of the Philippines (NGCP)’s fifth and sixth regulatory periods. Fuentebella said ERC issued its fifth-period decision on January 30, 2026, and addressed a motion for reconsideration on May 13. NGCP had filed for the sixth period, covering 2028–2032, on May 14, 2026; Fuentebella said ERC expected the next review to proceed on a timely schedule, with a first-quarter-next-year target.

De Jesus asked when consumers begin paying a return on approved transmission capital expenditures. Fuentebella said returns accrue as projects are implemented, in proportion to the capital introduced, rather than only after construction is complete. He said ERC examines whether consumers pay for infrastructure that has not delivered service during rate resets, underscoring the need for timely reviews.

Regional secondary price cap. De Jesus asked why ERC shifted the Secondary Price Cap (SPC) from a nationwide calculation to regional application in the Visayas and Mindanao, retroactive to the August 2026 billing period. Fuentebella explained that lower Luzon prices could suppress the nationwide rolling average and prevent the trigger from being reached despite severe regional price spikes. He said the regional safeguard was being monitored weekly in coordination with the market operator.

Fuentebella described the regional cap as temporary and condition-dependent, while saying ERC was working to make it permanent. He also said ERC would restore standard system-wide application once price divergence subsided and rolling averages returned to normal. De Jesus agreed that a cap could provide immediate consumer relief but should not indefinitely replace fixing generation and transmission constraints. Fuentebella agreed and pointed to delayed interconnection and transmission upgrades, along with faster baseload and renewable capacity, as longer-term measures.

Generator availability and compliance rules. De Jesus asked what authority ERC had over generating companies that repeatedly failed to remain available during forced outages. Fuentebella said ERC regulates generators under EPIRA through certificates of compliance and applicable technical and market requirements, and sets technology-specific allowable unplanned-outage days.

Asked why reliability standards were revised, Fuentebella said the prior benchmark needed to reflect actual operating experience and differences among technologies. He said the revised rules set technology-specific outage limits, require adherence to planned-maintenance schedules and post-maintenance reports, and provide compliance plans and administrative sanctions for persistent non-compliance.
