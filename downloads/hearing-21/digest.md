## Agency presentation

### Appearing in deference to the power of the purse

FY 2027 Office of the President NEP allocation: PHP 10.156 billion. Executive Secretary Ralph Recto said he appeared not merely because the law requires the Executive to defend its budget before Congress, which holds the constitutional power of the purse, but in compliance with the President's standing directive to honor Congress, respect its mandate, and treat it as a full partner in advancing the welfare of the people and protecting the national interest. He described the two branches as co-equal institutions united by a common duty to the Filipino people rather than competing centers of power.

### A ₱10.15-billion request, 64% below the previous year

The NEP proposal was 0.14% of the proposed PHP 7.2 trillion national budget and 64% below the previous year. He attributed the reduction to lower operational requirements among the Office's delivery units and to the completion of activities for the Philippines' hosting of the ASEAN Summit, for which no further funding would be needed. The Office was, in his words, asking for less while remaining fully prepared to do more, and he called the amount sufficient for the Presidency amid geopolitical tensions, disruptions in the Middle East, typhoons, El Niño and other calamities.

### What the budget sustains: 49 delivery units and Malacañang operations

The budget would sustain the Office of the President's 49 delivery units; support cabinet meetings, policy consultations, presidential engagements and official travel; and fund the daily operations of Malacañang as the government's principal center of decision-making and executive coordination. Recto said it would also bring timely assistance to vulnerable families and let government respond swiftly in emergencies. Activity alone, he said, is not the measure of performance: the test for every peso is whether it solves a problem, improves a life, or strengthens the nation, and said the Office ensures each policy, meeting, engagement and mission yields a public dividend in jobs, investment, national security, public services or opportunity.

### Audit record and fiscal discipline

Recto said public funds are not the government's money but the people's, earned through hard labor and entrusted to government in the expectation that it will uplift their lives — which is why, he said, fiscal discipline and accountability remain central to the Office's operations. Recto reported that the Commission on Audit issued the Office of the President an unmodified opinion for Fiscal Year 2025 — the highest opinion an agency can receive and the Office's third consecutive unmodified opinion since 2022 — meaning its financial statements were presented fairly in all material respects under public-sector accounting standards. He called it a badge of good housekeeping and proof that the people's money is accounted for and their trust respected. ### Full support from Cagayan de Oro: foreign policy and the West Philippine Sea

Rep. Rufus B. Rodriguez, for Cagayan de Oro City, manifested 100% support for the entire budget of the Office of the President, saying the President deserved support and admiration. His first reason was foreign policy: the President had made sure not an inch of national territory — the first substantive provision of the Constitution — was lost, had pivoted to like-minded countries such as the United States, Japan, the European Union, Australia and New Zealand as a matter of international law, and was protecting the West Philippine Sea and the 200-mile economic zone under the 1982 UN Convention on the Law of the Sea. The President, he said, stood on the right way to counter China, and Congress had to support his quest that no foreign power take the economic zone.

## Committee outcome

### All questions to be answered on or before plenary; CSO queries forwarded; proceedings suspended

Closing, Rep. Albert S. Garcia, the committee's Senior Vice Chairperson and presiding officer, said Tinio was the last member wishing to manifest, that he had watched the Office of the President's staff taking notes intently and Executive Secretary Recto's attention fixed on the members' questions, and that all of them would be answered on or before the plenary so the public could hear the answers. Continuing a practice he said the leadership and the committee chair had instituted with the previous year's budget, civil society organizations had submitted questions, clarifications and suggestions to the Committee on Appropriations through Chair Mikaela Suansing; these too would be given to the Office of the President for answers before plenary.
