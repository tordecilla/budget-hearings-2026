## DEPDev economic outlook and 2027 budget priorities

### Economic conditions, jobs, and inflation

Balisacan said the proposed 2027 budget must protect earlier gains, address implementation and structural bottlenecks, and restore faster, durable, inclusive growth. He reported average real GDP growth of 5.1% a year from 2010 to 2025, versus 4.2% in the preceding 15 years, and 5.8% from 2022 to 2025. But momentum weakened in the second half of 2025 and growth slowed to 2.6% in the first half of 2026. He said the country had reached upper-middle-income status in 2025, with GNI per capita of USD4,850 above the World Bank's USD4,636 threshold, but said that status must translate into better jobs, incomes, and living standards. He attributed the slowdown largely to weak domestic demand as governance and corruption concerns and the Middle East conflict weighed on confidence: domestic demand grew 1.5% in the first half of 2026, versus 5.7% a year earlier; investment fell 6.5%, household-consumption growth eased to 2.9%, and exports grew 10%. Services remained the main source of growth while industry contracted and agriculture contributed modestly. Balisacan said the post-pandemic expansion created 4 million net jobs between the first half of 2022 and the first half of 2026, but employment then fell by 166,000 over the past year and unemployment rose from 3.1% to 4.2% in June 2026. He reported that official 2025 estimates put poverty incidence at 9.7%, down from 15.5% in 2023, with 6.5 million Filipinos lifted out of poverty through recovering incomes and employment and sustained social-protection programs. He cautioned that these gains remain vulnerable to high inflation. He said inflation had surged from March because the Middle East conflict affected global oil and commodity prices; headline inflation was 6.2% in July, down from 6.4% in June but still above the government's target range. High food prices, he said, weigh disproportionately on poor and low-income households and could reverse recent poverty-reduction gains.

## DBM 2027 National Expenditure Program presentation

### 2027 framework, programmed spending, and fiscal discipline

Acting DBM Secretary Kim Robert C. De Leon presented a proposed PHP7.2 trillion fiscal-year 2027 national budget, equal to 21.7% of GDP and described as 6% above the 2026 GAA. He said much of the increase reflects mandatory expenditures and existing commitments, including higher national tax allotments for LGUs, the fourth salary-adjustment tranche for civilian employees, and corresponding military and uniformed-personnel pay increases. De Leon stressed that the overall increase is not uniform across agencies: limited fiscal space requires choices for implementation-ready programs with the greatest impact and value. He said agencies submitted more than PHP11 trillion in requests, and DBM used actual budget performance and implementation capacity, as well as funding needs, in determining allocations. De Leon reported that, as of June 30, 2026, PHP7.54 trillion in appropriations was available, PHP6.63 trillion had been released as allotments, PHP3.29 trillion had been obligated, and 81.9% of those obligations had been disbursed. The 2027 proposal consists of PHP4.494 trillion in programmed appropriations and PHP2.706 trillion in automatic appropriations. He listed major automatic items including PHP1.32 trillion for the LGU national tax allotment and PHP1.114 trillion for interest payments. He said unprogrammed appropriations would be PHP112 billion, the lowest proposed level since 2019, and are standby authority rather than automatic spending; they are mainly limited to restoring a PDIC fund balance and foreign-assisted projects still under review. By expense class, he reported PHP2.944 trillion in MOOE, PHP1.982 trillion in personnel services, PHP1.16 trillion in capital outlays, and PHP1.114 trillion in financial expenses, while describing a gradual fiscal-consolidation path that does not reduce the deficit at the expense of growth.
