### Expired medicines, reported losses, and distribution failures

Senator Raffy T. Tulfo challenged the Department of Health (DOH) over medicines he said were repeatedly left to expire or buried while poor patients and barangay health centers lacked supplies. Undersecretary Dr. Gloria J. Balboa described a systems problem involving program forecasting, delayed procurement and failed bids, delivery, and inventory visibility; she said medicine should arrive with 18 months of shelf life, while in some cases the DOH accepted 15 months with a supplier guarantee to replace it when it neared six months. Tulfo demanded records and contracts to support earlier claims that expired drugs were returned to suppliers. Balboa said she knew of replacement requirements for near-expiry stock in the terms of reference, but was not aware of an arrangement returning already-expired medicines. Asked about 2026, Secretary Edwin M. Mercado instead reported 2025 figures: expired stocks valued at about PHP 217 million and near-expiry stocks at PHP 477 million. Tulfo separately alleged that 11 billion pesos worth of medicines had been buried in 2023. He also alleged that medicine procurement favored suppliers offering near-expiry drugs and kickbacks, and that expired mental-health medicines intended for the Mandaluyong Mental Health Center were disposed of while some products were sold after expiry dates were removed. These were Tulfo's allegations. He said he would raise the matter in a privilege speech for possible Senate Blue Ribbon Committee investigation.
