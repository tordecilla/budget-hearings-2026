## At a glance

- **Competition enforcement and project accountability:** The Philippine Competition Commission (PCC) discussion covered market monitoring, enforcement priorities, foreign-assisted project utilization, performance targets and requests for case and spending data.

- **Regional and freeport authorities:** The Metropolitan Baguio City, La Trinidad, Itogon, Sablan, Tuba and Tublay Development Authority (MBLISDA), Authority of the Freeport Area of Bataan (AFAB), Cagayan Economic Zone Authority (CEZA), and Zamboanga City Special Economic Zone Authority (ZamboEcozone) were sponsored; the interpellation periods ended without substantive question-and-answer exchanges.

- **Development and corporate oversight:** The Southern Philippines Development Authority (SPDA) sponsorship was followed by closure. Governance Commission for Government-Owned or -Controlled Corporations (GCG) discussions addressed subsidy-based institutional reviews, liquidation timelines and costs, and corporate governance and employee benefits.

- **Mindanao development and Marawi compensation:** The Mindanao Development Authority (MinDA) discussion addressed regional development coordination and energy projects. Marawi Compensation Board (MCB) exchanges examined claimant processing, funding sufficiency, transferred claims, the statutory sunset, reporting and support services; requests for additional funding and mandate extension were not adopted.

- **Conversion authority and Subic:** Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA) exchanges covered the Clark Advanced Manufacturing Park proposal, land and labor safeguards, affected communities and consultation. The House approved a suspension of BCDA budget deliberations. Subic Bay Metropolitan Authority (SBMA) exchanges addressed waste-trade disputes, infrastructure funding, port activity and risks associated with military-linked facilities.

- **Science and technology:** Department of Science and Technology (DOST) questions covered grants, research support, artificial intelligence (AI) capacity and domestic computing infrastructure. DOST interpellations were terminated; consideration of the General Appropriations Bill was later suspended, not passed.

## Philippine Competition Commission (PCC)

### PCC budget and competition enforcement record

Rep. Dante S. Garcia, Vice Chair of the House Committee on Appropriations and sponsor for the Philippine Competition Commission (PCC), said the FY2027 request was calibrated to complex challenges. He said additional resources would strengthen surveillance, investigations, enforcement, analytics and interagency coordination.

Garcia said PCC referred initial findings of suspected bid-rigging in Bulacan flood-control projects to the Department of Justice (DOJ). He also described a dawn search with the National Bureau of Investigation (NBI) at premises associated with SunWest Incorporated and Eco Leisure and Hospitality Holdings Company, where investigators suspected relevant records for an ongoing inquiry were located.

He said PCC fined the Boracay Business Administration of Scuba Shops and 40 member dive shops a total of 2.17 million for price-fixing on Boracay Island, Malay, Aklan, and ordered them to stop enforcing the multi-year agreement. The currency of the fine is not stated in this passage. Garcia also said PCC reviewed 21 mergers and acquisitions with an approximate transaction value of PHP 515.6 billion for competition concerns in FY2026 to date; this is transaction value, not a PCC budget amount.

Garcia described the PCC's work against anticompetitive agreements and abuses of dominant position, alongside merger review. Garcia said the PCC, Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT), and National Telecommunications Commission (NTC) finalized implementing rules for the Konektadong Pinoy Act to promote competition among data-transmission participants and internet service providers. He also described advocacy and capacity-building work, including pledges by four business organizations to uphold the Philippine Competition Act: the Philippine Chamber of Commerce, Philippine Retailers Association, Pharmaceutical and Healthcare Association of the Philippines, and Financial Executive Institute of the Philippines.

### Rep. Presley C. De Jesus

PHILRECA Party-list Representative Presley C. De Jesus asked the Philippine Competition Commission (PCC) sponsor, Representative Dante S. Garcia, Vice Chair of the House Committee on Appropriations, whether the PCC had assessed the Green Energy Auction Program (GEAP) with the Department of Energy (DOE) and the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC). Garcia said the PCC completed a study in 2025 and discussed its findings and policy options with the DOE and ERC in January and March 2026. He described potential concerns involving location-specific or per-grid auction design, consistency and transparency in setting Green Energy Auction Reserve (GEAR) prices, disclosure of auction information, and pre-bid conferences. The PCC assessed competition effects within the existing framework and recommended pro-competitive reforms to the agencies; the policy dialogue also considered possible application to future programs.

De Jesus asked whether the PCC had moved from monitoring fuel markets to a formal investigation or motu proprio inquiry into oil-company conduct during the period of fuel-price pressure. Garcia said the PCC was conducting a fuel study on asymmetric price adjustments and price dispersion, including whether prices rose quickly with international costs but fell slowly, or remained persistently above local prices. He said the study was still under way: the PCC had requested information from fuel companies and government agencies, some had responded, and the PCC was analyzing the data for possible anti-competitive conduct. Garcia said requested government data had arrived on September 18, 2026. He reported no completed findings from the study.

De Jesus cited the PCC market study on social media and search platforms, which he said found a highly concentrated Philippine digital-platform market and identified accumulated user data as a potential source of market power. Asked what the PCC would do beyond publication, Garcia said it had recommended giving data privacy greater weight in competition assessments, studying data portability and interoperability, coordinating more closely with privacy regulators, and considering legislative updates to the competition framework for data-driven markets.

De Jesus asked the sponsor to confirm that only 34% of the continuing appropriation for the foreign-assisted capacity-building project had been obligated. Garcia confirmed the figure and said the continuing appropriation was for a project released in 2023. He said the PCC requested a Special Allotment Release Order (SARO) for PHP 104 million, equivalent to loan proceeds downloaded by the Asian Development Bank (ADB), but the Department of Budget and Management (DBM) issued PHP 280 million instead, which he said resulted in excess resources.

De Jesus cited ADB project data as of June 23, 2026: cumulative contract awards were 45%, cumulative loan disbursements were 41%, and the loan had been extended to June 30, 2028. He distinguished these from Commission on Audit (COA) FY2024 utilization ratios and said the figures showed substantial project financing remained undisbursed. He asked for quarterly FY2027 obligation and disbursement targets and the activities that would use the funds. Garcia pointed to the loan extension, revised procurement and programming, additional project proposals, and stronger monitoring as measures intended to accelerate implementation, without providing quarterly targets or an itemized funding plan.

Asked how many active antitrust cases were pending or under review, Garcia said seven were ongoing. When De Jesus asked whether the cases could be disclosed, Garcia committed to submit the complete data to him for review. De Jesus closed by emphasizing the PCC's role in merger review and preventing collusion, particularly in fuel and energy markets.

### Rep. Roberto Nazal

Rep. Roberto Nazal asked whether the Philippine Competition Commission (PCC) had studied a major retailer acting both as gatekeeper and as a competitor to suppliers in its stores. PCC sponsor Rep. Dante S. Garcia said yes. Nazal said private-label products were not inherently illegal, but asked when a retailer's control over shelf and counter placement, inventory and promotions, while selling a similar product, became predatory. Garcia said the PCC was monitoring the market and would investigate.

Asked about complaints and interventions, Garcia said the PCC had received minor complaints, given them attention and informed manufacturers. He said small businesses could complain through the PCC website and social media, including Facebook, and that filing was easy and would prompt an investigation.

Nazal described a retailer prominently displaying its own lower-priced product while making a supplier's competing product harder to access. Garcia identified the practice as self-preferencing and said the PCC could bring a case. When Nazal raised the risk that a powerful retailer might retaliate by removing a complainant from its shelves, Garcia said the PCC was mandated to act against even large companies and affirmed that it could protect smaller players.

Nazal asked whether the PCC had publicly studied how major retailers treat their own products versus competing suppliers across placement, inventory, promotions, sales data, pricing, fees and payment terms. Garcia said there was no public study, but the PCC had compelled a large retailer to be fair. Asked whether Congress should strengthen the PCC's authority, Garcia said a bill was pending in the economic-affairs committee, he thought, and the PCC was working with it; Nazal offered to co-author the bill.

Nazal then asked whether the PCC was examining online marketplaces that also sell or promote competing products, and what safeguards it considered necessary. Garcia said it was looking into the issue and monitoring the market, and that the PCC was bound to file a complaint if it found a violation.

Nazal said online platforms might charge up to 60%. As an example, he said that on a PHP 100 sale, a platform could take PHP 60, sometimes PHP 70, leaving PHP 30 before the seller's costs. He asked the PCC to examine these charges. Garcia said the PCC had studied digital platforms but not that specific issue and would now look into it.

Nazal asked whether a conglomerate's ownership of both a bank and financing-dependent businesses, such as real estate, could reinforce market power through preferential access to financing even when related-party lending complied with banking rules. Garcia said the PCC had studied cross-sector effects. He said it had not yet found such an unfair advantage in an industry, but would act if one emerged.

Nazal suggested using artificial intelligence (AI) to examine possible effects.

Nazal asked what sector reviews and enforcement milestones it would deliver, particularly for retail, digital platforms and concentrated markets. He asked whether the PCC would investigate proactively rather than wait for small businesses to complain. Garcia said the PCC actively monitored social media and would act promptly on complaints.

Nazal requested a spending plan, safeguards, sector studies, measurable outputs and a way for small businesses and suppliers to share market information safely. Garcia promised to provide a copy. Nazal closed by urging the PCC to develop proactive protections for small businesses.

Nazal then moved to terminate sponsorship and debate on the PCC budget. Rep. Arnan C. Panaligan joined for the majority; Deputy Speaker Ferjenel G. Biron, M.D. approved the joint motion and declared PCC interpellations terminated. This closed debate; it did not approve the appropriation.

## Governance Commission for Government-Owned or -Controlled Corporations (GCG)

### GCG budget sponsorship

Rep. Bella Vanessa B. Suansing, Vice Chairperson of the Committee on Appropriations and sponsor of the Governance Commission for Government-Owned or -Controlled Corporations (GCG) budget, said she was ready to answer questions about the proposed budget.

### Rep. Presley C. De Jesus

Rep. Presley C. De Jesus cited Department of Budget and Management (DBM) Corporate Budget Memorandum No. 48 for the FY2027 Corporate Budget Call. He also described a rule that a government-owned or -controlled corporation (GOCC) covered by Republic Act No. 10149 and receiving 60% or more of its funding from national government subsidies for 10 consecutive years is automatically flagged for mandatory institutional review by the Governance Commission for Government-Owned or -Controlled Corporations (GCG). Sponsor Rep. Bella Vanessa B. Suansing confirmed the rule. Asked how many GOCCs met the threshold, she said the study was ongoing, GCG was checking its records and would submit the qualifying list; she gave no count.

De Jesus asked whether reviews had begun and what timeline applied. Suansing described three monitoring tools: GOCC performance evaluations, director performance evaluations and corporate governance scorecards. She said finalization of FY2020 reports depended on Commission on Audit (COA) financial statements, so reviews remained ongoing, with completion expected later in the year. Asked how many directors were under review, she estimated about 500.

De Jesus requested information on 10 years of national government support, GOCC performance and scorecard results, and the status of GCG reviews. Suansing said GCG was checking the support figures and observed that government support had generally declined as some previously supported GOCCs improved their performance.

De Jesus cited GCG's June 30, 2026 classification of 21 GOCCs under abolition and the target of completing liquidation for at least five during 2026. Suansing confirmed that 21 were abolished and under liquidation and 11 had completed liquidation. She initially said 11 terminal reports were due to the Office of the President that year, then corrected herself: five GOCCs were in the 2026 terminal-report pipeline, leaving 16 under abolition. She said some cases dated back to 2013, roughly 13 years, because settling liabilities and compromise agreements took time. GCG had established a Disposition and Asset Management Office to expedite work on the 16 remaining GOCCs.

De Jesus asked for the annual personnel, administrative, liability, asset-maintenance and legal costs of keeping corporations in the process. Suansing said a GOCC recommended for abolition stopped receiving national government subsidy and that pending cases incurred no additional expense for the national government, but she gave no annual cost estimate.

Asked about employees, Suansing said those not absorbed by a government agency received separation incentive pay; employees needed to safeguard assets and process the abolition remained maintained by the corporation.

Asked why some GOCCs were being abolished, Suansing listed six grounds for recommendation: a function no longer relevant to state or national development; duplication or unnecessary overlap with other agencies; failure to produce desired outcomes or meet objectives; dormancy or non-operation; an activity considered better carried out by a private firm; or operations requiring consolidation under a holding company.

When De Jesus asked whether the private-sector criterion meant an entity would be sold or given away, Suansing clarified that GCG could recommend abolition, merger or privatization. For abolition, she said, operations, employees and assets were generally absorbed by a related government agency. This distinguished the private-sector criterion from the usual transfer of an abolished GOCC's functions and assets to a government agency.

De Jesus asked whether objective performance thresholds determined when a GOCC should be wound up. Suansing said GCG used the Corporate Governance Scorecard and Performance Evaluation Scorecard; consistent failure on key indicators could trigger review for abolition.

Asked whether poor performance had consequences for a GOCC board, Suansing said a director who scored below 85% on the director performance scorecard would not qualify for the shortlist for a succeeding term and could not be reappointed. De Jesus said he hoped GCG's commitments would produce measurable results to revisit in the next budget cycle and expressed support for the GCG budget.

### Rep. Antonio L. Tinio

Rep. Antonio L. Tinio asked which Clark area was under discussion. Sponsor Rep. Maria Angela S. Garcia distinguished the 4,400-hectare Clark Freeport Zone, where Clark International Airport is located, from Clark Advanced Manufacturing Park (CAMP), inside the Special Economic Zone in New Clark City, Tarlac. Garcia described the Pax Silica declaration as involving a broader global chain of manufacturing, logistics, roads, and the airport, but said the specific BCDA proposal was in New Clark City. Tinio cited U.S. statements describing 4,000 acres; Garcia gave the local area as 1,620 hectares. The exchange also left open how the coordination-office proposal related to the manufacturing park.

Tinio asked about a reported BCDA-Subic Bay Metropolitan Authority (SBMA) partnership to position the Port of Subic Bay as a maritime gateway for Pax Silica. Garcia confirmed there was a memorandum of understanding (MOU) and promised to provide a copy for the House and public.

Tinio cited U.S. public releases describing an "Economic Security Zone" and joint governance, including reports of a U.S. demand for diplomatic immunity. Garcia said BCDA could not confirm the U.S. statements and that she knew of no Philippine law establishing an Economic Security Zone. Garcia confirmed that the reported bilateral governance framework had not been signed, was still being studied, and was not yet available for release; Tinio sought a copy for public disclosure once signed.

Tinio read the BCDA letter proposing a Pax Silica Coordination Office in an area described as approximately 4,000 acres and questioned how that office related to the later description of CAMP. Garcia said the proposal was preliminary and would be discussed; she did not identify an amended proposal. Tinio quoted the U.S. response as accepting a proposed two-year rent-free lease subject to agreement on precise boundaries and a formal lease. Garcia described a lease-like arrangement with lease payments, while Tinio said the accepted proposal itself was not a lease agreement. The rate after the rent-free period and the full lease term remained undetermined; Garcia identified 99 years as the legal maximum, not an agreed term.

Tinio read the Special Economic Zone Act's immigration provisions, including permanent resident status for an investor with an initial investment of at least US$150,000 and eligible family members; Garcia confirmed the investment threshold when asked.

Tinio read a BCDA letter inviting Barangay Santa Lucia residents to discuss processes and possible government assistance for the New Clark City master development plan; he said residents understood it as relocation assistance. Garcia said the letters went to residential addresses outside New Clark City, while some people inside the city could be affected by master-plan development. She distinguished that letter from the 1,600-hectare CAMP area, which she said it did not identify. Tinio pressed for the barangays covered by the specific project. Garcia eventually identified Aranguren and O'Donnell; she also acknowledged that residents inside New Clark City could be affected by development.

Tinio said residents he visited in September included Aeta communities and asked whether free, prior, and informed consent (FPIC) had been obtained. Garcia said the area was not ancestral domain and that, in BCDA's view, FPIC was not required; she said BCDA was coordinating with the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP). Tinio said the Aeta Hungay had a pending ancestral-domain application, originally filed in 1999 after an initial spoken reference to 1991, and refiled in 2021, which he said NCIP had confirmed, and argued that consultation was still needed while the application remained pending. The speakers' positions on the land and consent requirements remained in disagreement.

Tinio moved to suspend deliberations on the proposed BCDA budget so the House could consider the remaining scheduled agencies. Majority Leader Rep. Antonino B. Roman III said the Majority joined the Minority in the motion. Deputy Speaker Janette L. Garin put the joint motion, heard no objection, approved it, and declared consideration of the BCDA budget suspended.
