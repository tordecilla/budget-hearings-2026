### Proposed BFP fire-truck procurement estimates and conditions

These are estimates and a proposed procurement, not a completed contract or appropriation outcome.

### Rep. Renee Louise M. Co

Rep. Renee Louise M. Co asked for a report locating the 50,431 evacuation centers cited by Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) sponsor Rep. Arnie B. Fuentebella, and for the cities and municipalities that still lacked one. Fuentebella said the agency had the report and would submit it that day. Co connected the request to implementation of the Ligtas Pinoy Centers Act (Republic Act No. 12076) and the Student Loan Moratorium During Disasters Act (Republic Act No. 12077), for which she said Kabataan Party-list was principal author.

Co asked how many Sangguniang Kabataan (SK) councils had implemented comprehensive barangay youth development plans, what obstacles explained nonimplementation, and whether incomplete councils and quorum problems affected activity. Fuentebella cited 2025 SK web-portal activity counts by area: governance 22,729; active citizenship 23,962; environment 11,846; global mobility 1,483; health 19,105; education 16,856; economic empowerment 3,472; social inclusion and equity 8,413; peace building and security 5,774; and agriculture 1,831. He offered to submit the data. Co also requested a list of councils with no activities for about a year, and Fuentebella agreed to submit it. The exchange gives activity counts, not a count of councils that completed their plans.

Co said gaps in SK councils and vacant positions needed urgent attention and urged that barangay and SK elections proceed in November 2026 rather than be postponed to 2028. No response or decision on election timing is recorded in this round.

Co asked whether a memorandum or other document established the proposed Philippine National Police (PNP) Counterterrorism Center and what duties it would have. Fuentebella described it as a planned data-banking and operational facility that would also cover cybersecurity and support data-driven PNP planning. He said there was no establishing memorandum, but a building design existed, and offered the design and concept report. He later described an Integrated Facility inside Camp Crame for coordination, information sharing, operational readiness, technical capability, human rights and rule-of-law policy, including cybersecurity, information and communications technology, peace-process development, and human-rights offices. Fuentebella said the report and concept could be provided that day.

Co asked whether police deployments at mobilizations were covered by body-worn-camera rules and how many officers could be equipped. Fuentebella said the PNP had 2,600 cameras and that the inventory was far too small. He said camera prices had fallen from "60" to "20 to 30," but did not specify a currency or unit; he attributed the limited inventory to budget constraints. Asked whether cameras were used at the September 21 mobilization, he answered yes, while saying he could not otherwise be specific about their operational use.

Co noted that DILG had submitted Memorandum Circular No. 2024-58 on technical specifications and asked whether it remained current as the PNP considered AI-capable cameras. Fuentebella said the specifications were updated as needed, but confirmed that the PNP was still following the May 2024 circular while a technical working group studied possible revisions. Co requested the procurement plan, number of units, vendors and cost. Fuentebella agreed to submit information, said procurement was handled by the PNP, and confirmed that procurement continued, without giving a quantity, vendor or total cost. Co said the purpose was public and legislative accountability for PNP operations and possible violations, not institutional modernization alone.

Co quoted a PNP report submitted to her office on preparations for the State of the Nation Address mobilization, which described police-community-relations activities and community engagements "to discourage" groups and individuals from joining anti-administration protests and rallies. She asked what the phrase meant and challenged the report's characterization of the practice as advanced. Fuentebella first said police did not discourage rallies and should protect everyone and let them be heard. When Co said the wording was verbatim from the submitted report, he asked to see the complete document and could not explain the passage from the pages then available. He said police should prevent disorder, protect public safety and follow the law, and offered a written response after reviewing the document. Co said she would obtain the remaining pages and reiterated her concern that the reported practice could deter criticism and undermine democratic rights. No substantive explanation of the quoted wording or written follow-up is recorded in this round.

Co showed a photograph she attributed to the 89th Infantry Battalion's Facebook page, describing an educational tour for 150 children under six from a child development center in Barangay North Poblacion, Maramag, Bukidnon, where children were shown holding long firearms. She asked whether this was acceptable and whether DILG and the Early Childhood Care and Development Council (ECCD) would prevent similar tours. Fuentebella said he could not yet answer the specific case, agreed the image looked concerning, and said he would bring it to the agency's attention. After Co explained her concern about early gun exposure, Fuentebella committed to investigate and ensure it would not happen again, asking her to provide the date.

Co then argued that responses to school violence should center children's mental health and rights. She proposed better psychological guidance for mentors and parents to address bullying, digital-literacy lessons, and stricter gun policies and monitoring for government employees with firearms. Fuentebella's specific commitment concerned investigation of the child-center tour; the exchange records no response on these broader policy proposals.

Co asked what assessment led DILG Memorandum Circular No. 2022-118 to make anti-illegal drugs a mandatory peace-and-order and public-safety focus in every local government unit (LGU), and whether the circular would be reviewed in light of the Dangerous Drugs Board's (DDB) stated shift toward health and rights. Fuentebella said the question also involved DDB, described police anti-drug work as continuing, and said DILG had no direct mandate over the DDB/PDEA-led issue; police operations required Philippine Drug Enforcement Agency (PDEA) deputation. He acknowledged more was needed to protect young people. Co said LGU plans still appeared centered on criminalization and intelligence gathering. Fuentebella described improvement as ongoing, dependent on circumstances, and said DILG worked through its officers in LGUs. ### Rep. Percival "Perci" Cendaña

Rep. Percival "Perci" Cendaña asked how the Department of Tourism (DOT) would maximize limited tourism-promotion resources amid competition with larger Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) budgets. Sponsor Rep. Marie Bernadette G. Escudero said DOT would focus on priority source markets and tourism products with stronger potential for arrivals, bookings and visitor spending, naming South Korea, Japan, the United States, Canada and Australia, with targeted campaigns and airline, tour-operator and travel-platform partnerships. She said Discover More to Love had offered more than 3,000 travel deals, generating 800 additional room nights and 203,000 seats. She also cited 20 new international routes in 2026, 13 more scheduled, and an estimated 472,420 additional seats. Escudero added halal and Muslim-friendly tourism, 108 Michelin-recognized establishments, 70 halal or Muslim-friendly establishments, and 28 dive clusters with more than 900 dive sites as products to promote. Cendaña urged the department to make domestic travel more affordable and accessible as well as maximize the impact of limited resources.

Cendaña asked how DOT would strengthen domestic tourism and encourage Filipinos to visit destinations within the country. Escudero said Filipinos made 140 million domestic trips in 2025, nearly 5% more than the previous year, supporting hotels, restaurants, transport workers, small businesses and dependent families. She described the Tourism Industry Training Program as serving 64,000 direct beneficiaries through courses in training, digitalization and technology, certification and ladderization. Cendaña welcomed continued planning to make local travel goals more accessible and affordable.

Cendaña asked about dive tourism's economic contribution and the resources needed by the Philippine Commission on Sports Scuba Diving (PCSSD). Escudero estimated the industry's annual economic value at 73 billion (currency not stated), equal to 2.2% of gross internal tourism expenditure and 0.3% of national gross domestic product (GDP). She said PCSSD's budget was PHP 44.9 million in FY2026 and PHP 49.5 million in FY2027, and described a separate PHP 82.49 million augmentation request for standards, research, stewardship, safety networks and operations. Cendaña supported increasing PCSSD resources to develop scuba diving and protect marine life and dive sites. The transcript's question and answer identify the requested augmentation as PHP 82.49 million; no disposition is recorded here.

Asked which interventions would produce the greatest measurable gains in jobs, visitor spending and regional economic activity, Escudero identified three priorities: PHP 82.49 million for PCSSD; PHP 41.56 million for DOT's Office of the Secretary (OSEC) for national tourism planning, operations and the Tourism Development Fund; and PHP 50.92 million for the Intramuros Administration for tourism promotion and heritage conservation, including its United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO) World Heritage bid. She said DOT could submit its longer list to Cendaña's office. She then added the Tourism Road Infrastructure Program (TRIP), describing it as a 6 billion request for tourist destinations and the Department's top request; currency is not stated in this exchange. Cendaña said he supported the top three and the fourth item, and expressed support for additional DOT funding for campaigns, infrastructure and scuba diving. These were statements of support and requests, not an appropriation vote or recorded disposition.

### Rep. Presley C. De Jesus

Rep. Presley C. De Jesus asked the Department of Tourism (DOT) what it funded for dive tourism and how the programs would grow the industry. DOT sponsor Rep. Marie Bernadette G. Escudero said the 2026 initiative focused on trade development and support, including international dive expos. The DOT invested PHP 3.2 million and generated PHP 39.56 million in stakeholder sales leads. Escudero also cited the 2026 Salon de Plongee sous-marine, which generated 80,150,000 in sales leads (the currency was not stated). Other activities included the Puerto Galera Underwater Shootout and Philippine International Dive Expo 2027, training dive spotters, and familiarization for key media and opinion leaders.

De Jesus then asked whether the proposed 49 million or 50 million budget for the Philippine Commission on Sports Scuba Diving (PCSSD) could serve its mandate across the 956 dive sites. Escudero said it could not and called the amount a survival budget. She reiterated the previously discussed augmentation request for standards, research, safety networks and operations. Escudero agreed that divers typically stay longer and spend more than average tourists. De Jesus said trips and food in Vietnam could be cheaper than Siargao or Boracay and urged promotion of less familiar Philippine dive destinations to European and North American travelers; Escudero partly agreed that Vietnam could sometimes be cheaper. No approval or disposition of the additional PCSSD request was stated.

De Jesus asked how much the Tourism Development Fund (TDF) held and who controlled it. Escudero described it as a special fund under Section 16 of Republic Act No. 9593. For 2027, she said the DOT needed PHP 13 million for priority tourism activities, while the Department of Budget and Management (DBM) provided PHP 4.578 million. She said the TDF was held in a Special Account in the General Fund (SAGF).

Asked for the amount on hand as of March 2026, Escudero first said 25.451 billion. De Jesus checked whether she meant billion or million; Escudero corrected the unit to million and repeated 25.451 million. The final clarified balance was PHP 25.451 million, not billion.

De Jesus asked about the travel-tax charge for a Filipino economy-class traveler; Escudero confirmed the stated 1,620. She said collections were allocated 40% to the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), 10% to the National Commission for Culture and the Arts (NCCA), and 50% to the Tourism Infrastructure and Enterprise Zone Authority (TIEZA), an attached agency of the DOT.

For 2025, Escudero reported total collections of PHP 8.87 billion, with 4.4 billion going to TIEZA, 3.5 billion to CHED and 887,000 to NCCA (she did not restate a unit for the NCCA amount). For January through July 2026, she reported PHP 4.7 billion collected, with 2.3 billion to TIEZA, 1.8 billion to CHED and 474,000 to NCCA (again, no unit was stated for the last amount). De Jesus estimated that collections could be about PHP 8 billion a year and asked whether the money returned to the Treasury or was spent by the agencies. Escudero said collections go to the National Treasury; TIEZA submits a request and the DOT seeks a special accounts budget.

On the possible abolition of the travel tax, Escudero said the abolition bill had passed the House on third and final reading but had not yet passed the Senate. She said the government would fund through the General Appropriations Act (GAA) the lost travel-tax funding of TIEZA, CHED and NCCA. De Jesus argued this could spare travelers the separate charge, which he said should instead be incorporated into ticket prices; he supported replacing the tax through the GAA. No enactment of the bill was reported. The stated NCCA collection shares do not reconcile with the 10% distribution rule; the exchange did not explain the discrepancy.

De Jesus said there were no more minority members seeking to interpellate and moved to terminate sponsorship and debate on the DOT budget and attached agencies. Rep. De Carlo L. Uy joined the motion for the majority. Presiding Deputy Speaker Yasser Alonto Balindong put the joint motion to the House; hearing no objection, he approved termination of the interpellations. This ended debate and did not approve the DOT or attached-agency appropriations.

## Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA)

### Resumed BCDA deliberation and termination

After the Deputy Speaker declared the Department of Tourism interpellation terminated and the session was suspended, the House resumed. Rep. Daphne A. Lagon moved to resume interpellation on the proposed budget of the Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA). Presiding Deputy Speaker Yasser Alonto Balindong heard no objection and declared the BCDA sponsorship and interpellation approved. This was approval to proceed with the budget deliberation, not approval of an appropriation.

Rep. Sergio C. Dagooc said BCDA's budget had already been presented in the previous plenary session and that this was a continuation. With no other minority member seeking recognition, he moved to terminate interpellation and debate on the BCDA budget for fiscal year 2027. Lagon joined the motion for the majority. Balindong stated the joint motion, heard no objection and declared BCDA interpellation terminated. This unit contains no renewed sponsorship, substantive budget questions or answers, or appropriation amount.
