### Project readiness, geotagging and signed accountability

Dizon said DPWH had dropped the previous parametric allocation formula in favor of completing unfinished projects, maintaining critical infrastructure and addressing immediate community needs. Proposed projects now required approved programs of work, on-site geotagged photographs, DBM compliance forms and signed certificates of implementability. Implementing offices had to certify right-of-way readiness, specific locations and absence of duplicate funding; continuing projects had to be identified as such. Dizon contrasted this with past submissions containing only titles and costs, and promised to publish supporting documents so Congress and the public could scrutinize them. Projects also needed a planning basis and coordination with development councils or, at minimum, LGU certificates of no objection.

### Fewer projects and revised regional allocations

Dizon described consolidating local projects under the Basic Infrastructure Program and removing separate convergence programs. Tourism, trade and other departments would hold their own priority infrastructure funds, with transfers and agreements if DPWH implemented the work. DPWH also sought to eliminate duplicates and overlapping projects, focus on larger projects and bring workloads closer to implementing offices' capacity; DBM had directed removal of Basic Infrastructure Program projects below PHP 5 million, subject to Congress's consideration. Dizon put the proposed geographic shares at roughly 52% for Luzon, 20% for Visayas and 28% for Mindanao, compared with ten-year averages of 56%, 17% and 27%. He highlighted higher allocations for poorer regions but said the distribution remained imperfect and invited congressional adjustment.

### Flood mitigation grounded in plans and completed systems

Dizon emphasized flood mitigation rather than a promise to control all flooding. He acknowledged that many river-basin and drainage master plans were absent, unfinished or outdated, and sought more funding for studies and design. Priorities included closing gaps in unfinished defenses, approved designs, integrated water-resource management, coordination with local governments and actual on-site geotagging. Detention basins and dams could also store water for irrigation and supply. Investigations and COA clearance requirements complicated repairs to implicated projects, for which he sought congressional help. He cited alignment with plans in Quezon City, Metro Cebu and Iligan and continuing foreign-assisted projects, including Pasig-Marikina, ADB-supported river-basin projects and the JICA-supported Cavite project. Claims of reduced flooding in particular areas were his reported observations, not a claim that national flood risk had been eliminated.

### Delayed procurement and the September award target

Dizon acknowledged serious 2026 procurement delays. He attributed them to the absence of early procurement after the budget overhaul, roughly PHP 460 billion in capital outlay being placed for later release, allotment releases arriving in late May and June, and stricter requirements for complete plans before bidding. As of August 23, 15,032 of 17,764 projects had been advertised, representing about PHP 349.5 billion or 76% of the capital-outlay value. Awards covered 7,467 projects worth PHP 146.1 billion, about 32% by value. He aimed to have at least 80% awarded by the end of September for starts in the fourth quarter, offered updated figures, and acknowledged that disbursements would remain low despite catch-up efforts.
