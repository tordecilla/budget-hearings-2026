## Department of Trade and Industry (DTI)

### Senator Pia S. Cayetano

Vape-liquid testing laboratories, standards and expertise: Senator Pia S. Cayetano questioned the use of Shenzhen Element Testing Company Limited and whether its laboratory could be treated as a gold-standard tester. Sponsor Senator Paolo Benigno "Bam" Aquino IV relayed that the Department of Health (DOH) had identified the Tobacco Laboratory Network (TobLabNet) standard, and said the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) accredits laboratories able to test against it. Aquino said products had initially been tested in Shenzhen, while local facilities in Cavite and Quezon City now test most products. Cayetano noted that only one local facility, La Primera Testing Center in Quezon City, tested liquid rather than devices, and said she would verify the standard with DOH. She questioned DTI's ability to assess health safety without medical expertise; Aquino agreed DTI officials were not health experts and said they relied on TobLabNet's tests and equipment criteria. Cayetano recounted her understanding of Supreme Court rulings placing tobacco under the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and argued that inhaled vape liquid likewise required health-regulator scrutiny. She said she would check the standards with DOH and FDA; Aquino said DTI had been assigned the task and had chemical engineers rather than medical or addiction specialists.

Flavor-test results, youth use and seller enforcement: Asked how many submissions had failed testing, Aquino estimated that about 20 flavors had been rejected from more than 200 submissions, mainly for high formaldehyde levels; he said the 20 rejected flavors came from one company. He said the official lacked per-brand or per-company detail and committed to provide the data later. Aquino identified 17 registered brands. Cayetano said she would submit names of companies she found operating and obtain samples to check whether they were registered. Cayetano cited reported vaping among ages 10 to 19 rising from 3% in 2015 to 39% in 2023. Aquino said DTI could close unregistered shops and had issued notices of violation and formal charges in some cases.

Evidence for adult cessation benefits and youth appeal of flavors: Cayetano cited the U.S. FDA's rejection of 55,000 flavors and a later total of about one million, arguing that manufacturers should have to show that flavors help adult smokers quit enough to outweigh the risk of attracting youth. Aquino said DTI could reject flavors marketed to or attractive to minors under the existing law.
