## Office of the Vice President

### Mandate, operating model and stated priorities

The Office of the Vice President (OVP) presented itself as a direct bridge to underserved and marginalized communities, working with nine agencies to extend social protection, social security, government presence and emergency relief. It said its mandate is direct access to basic social services, delivered nationally through the central office, 10 satellite offices and a Tondo extension office. The presentation described nine core initiatives for 1.4 million target beneficiaries: microenterprise grants; school supplies, rainwear and dental kits for last-mile learners; youth leadership exposure; disaster relief; family sustenance; hot meals; free transport; wheelchairs; and burial support for Islamic rites. It framed these as targeted help with household shocks, education and commuting costs, hunger, disability and bereavement.

### FY 2027 proposal and allocation structure

Atty. Kelvin Jerome Tenido said the FY 2027 agency-specific NEP recommendation was net of RLIP and 11.7% below the FY 2026 appropriation. The presentation put FY 2027 personnel services at PHP 235.776 million including RLIP (29.4% of the total including automatic appropriations), PHP 561.651 million for maintenance and other operating expenses (70.1%), and PHP 3.3 million for capital outlay (0.41%), chiefly for ICT and equipment under the OVP information-systems plan. In later questioning, Rep. Sarah Jane I. Elago cited PHP 220.232 million for personnel services, and Atty. Rosalynne L. Sanchez described the amount as about PHP 220 million. ### Passenger counts and transport costs

Pressed on how nine buses could produce the 1.2 million beneficiary figure, Valora said the OVP counted boardings rather than unique individuals. Rep. Ma. Isabel L. Sagarbarria moved to terminate the committee's hearing on the proposed FY 2027 OVP budget under House Bill No. 10858. Rep. Roberto Nazal seconded, and Deputy Speaker Jose C. Alvarez recognized the motion. Alvarez then asked Nazal to change his earlier ‘static’ motion to a ‘dynamic’ one so the briefing could be terminated together; Nazal separately moved to terminate the budget briefing. The chair later thanked the OVP for answering the questions and ultimately declared the briefing terminated under Sagarbarria's motion.

Before the session ended, Sagarbarria separately moved to suspend the committee hearing on House Bill No. 10858 at Dandaya Hall until September 3 at 9 a.m. Alvarez treated this as another motion and, stating there was no objection, ruled that the hearing was suspended. He set the continuation for September 3, 2026, at 9 a.m.; the procedural outcome was a suspension of the hearing alongside termination of the OVP briefing.
