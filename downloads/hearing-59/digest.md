## At a glance

- **Unused funds and funding requests:** Secretary Ma. Theresa P. Lazaro of the Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA) and department officials linked unused appropriations and continuing requests for additions to concrete causes: delays to headquarters and overseas-property projects, posts opening late, lower passport demand after validity was extended, and reduced assistance and legal-fund use after the Department of Migrant Workers (DMW) assumed most overseas-worker cases. They also explained that some e-passport funds needed special release and could carry forward. Senator Erwin Tulfo questioned why additions were sought alongside unused funds; officials said some wish-list items had been omitted from the National Expenditure Program (NEP).

- **Passport fees and E-Gate failures:** Senate Committee on Finance Chair Senator Joseph Victor "JV" G. Ejercito raised the risk that APO Production might stop making passports if the fee stayed unchanged, and asked how the proposed increase would affect overseas Filipino workers and other applicants. Lazaro said the DFA had asked APO to postpone a change until the planned new-generation passport in mid-2028, citing the three-year restriction under the Philippine Passport Act. On reports of passports not reading at E-Gates, Assistant Secretary Anne Jalando-on Louis said the reader might be at fault; an Australian Border Force issue was reported resolved, while the DFA continued its investigation with the Bureau of Immigration (BI). Louis and Lazaro said the DFA had not been invited to test the E-Gates before installation; BI equipment and passport samples were to be tested through the technical working group.

- **Assistance and limits on overseas cases:** DFA officials said embassies and consulates assist Filipinos regardless of residency status and rely on community organizations and local contacts to find people needing help; overseas estimates are not exact counts. For detained Filipinos, the DFA described seeking consular access through its Warsaw embassy for a reported detainee in Ukraine and said representations to Russia and Ukraine had not received responses. Lazaro said European Union privacy rules prevented France from sharing asylum details, while the Philippine Embassy in Vienna had no information on whether Austria had denied Harry Roque asylum. The DFA also said the lack of extradition, transfer-of-sentenced-persons, and mutual-legal-assistance agreements made cooperation with the countries discussed more difficult.

- **Attached-agency oversight and next steps:** The Foreign Service Institute (FSI) described diplomatic training and preparation for overseas service. The Presidential Commission on Visiting Forces (PCVF) reported on utilization, treaty coordination, monitoring exercises, and working with local communities; it said the Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP) and visiting-force component determine responsibility for exercise-related injury or damage, with the responsible force or country providing compensation. The UNESCO National Commission of the Philippines (UNACOM) explained its budget use. The committee referred the FY2027 proposal for plenary consideration, subject to further discussion and requested documents.

### DFA appropriations, utilization and unreleased funds

Senator Erwin Tulfo questioned the Department of Foreign Affairs’ (DFA) unused appropriations and requests for funds. He cited about ₱10 billion unused against the 2024 General Appropriations Act (GAA), ₱12.4 billion against the 2025 GAA, and ₱23 billion unused as of June against the 2026 GAA of ₱28.3 billion. DFA Assistant Secretary Emilio T. Fernandez said the 2024 and 2025 budget obligation rates were 83% and 80%; maintenance and other operating expenses and capital outlay had two-year validity and could continue into the following year. DFA Secretary Ma. Theresa P. Lazaro referred the question to Fernandez.

Fernandez attributed unused balances to a DFA headquarters retrofit delayed by failed bidding, lower assistance-to-nationals (ATN) and Legal Assistance Fund use after the Department of Migrant Workers (DMW) took over most overseas Filipino worker (OFW) cases, lower passport demand after passport validity was extended from five to ten years, postponed overseas property projects, and foreign posts that opened late in the year. ATN use fell from ₱826 million in 2023 to ₱639 million in 2024 and ₱498 million in 2025; across 2021–2026, ₱4 billion of the ₱6 billion ATN appropriation had been obligated, or about 67%. Passport appropriations and obligations were ₱4 billion and ₱2.1 billion in 2024, and ₱4.3 billion and ₱1.9 billion in 2025. Fernandez said passport demand could rise again in 2028.

Asked about ₱6.3 billion in 2024 appropriations said to be unreleased by the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), Fernandez confirmed ₱10 billion in unused funds, including ₱4.2 billion in unobligated allotments carried forward and unreleased appropriations mainly for e-passports subject to a special allotment release order (SARO). He identified about ₱6 billion for passports and ₱50 million for the International Commitments Fund within the unreleased amount. The passport funds could remain available as continuing appropriations. DBM OIC Director III Mary Rose P. Aguilar explained that e-passport funds required a special budget request; because DFA’s current provision was not fully used and spilled over into the next year, DBM released requested continuing appropriations while the unreleased balance grew.

For 2026, Tulfo cited a ₱28.3 billion GAA and ₱23 billion unused as of June, and asked for the disbursement position as of August 31. Fernandez reported a budget utilization rate of about 55%, then gave ₱17.5 billion as the amount obligated, clarifying that it was the amount used rather than the amount remaining. The exchange does not establish a disbursement rate. Tulfo also asked why DFA made ₱1.6 billion in budgetary adjustments despite low utilization, including ₱1.5 billion for miscellaneous personnel benefits, ₱77 million for pension and gratuity, and ₱26.1 million from the contingent fund. Fernandez said such adjustments usually met additional personnel-services requirements at posts requested from DBM.

Tulfo questioned utilization of the Legal Assistance Fund, citing ₱364.5 million appropriated and ₱2.4 million used. DFA OIC Undersecretary Ezzedin H. Tago explained that DMW’s Action Fund and Migrant Workers Offices (MWOs) now fund most OFW legal cases. DFA used its fund for 25 cases involving seven OFWs or other overseas Filipinos, and in 2026 for 16 cases involving overseas Filipinos, mostly in countries without MWOs. DFA’s cases include tourists, people in transit, permanent migrants and pilgrims who cannot hire counsel or are in countries without legal aid. Tago said Filipinos in Sabah who are not OFWs fall under DFA outreach. The exchange did not state a disposition changing DFA’s appropriation.

## DFA presentation

### Foreign policy pillars, ASEAN chairship and maritime priorities

Secretary Lazaro described the DFA's work through three pillars: national security; economic security; and the rights, welfare and interests of Filipinos overseas. Bolivar added public and cultural diplomacy and strengthening the department as organizational priorities. For the Philippines' 2026 chairship of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN), under the theme “Navigating Our Future Together,” the DFA expected to host more than 640 meetings nationwide, including 26 at head-of-state level, and anticipated six agreements to be signed. A declaration on maritime cooperation issued at the Cebu summit included a proposal, welcomed by ASEAN member states, for a Philippine-hosted ASEAN maritime center.

Bolivar said the DFA was working to defend Philippine sovereignty, sovereign rights and jurisdiction in the West Philippine Sea. On the tenth anniversary of the South China Sea arbitral award, the Philippines and 10 partner countries issued a statement reaffirming the award's legally binding nature.

### Overseas Filipino assistance and posts without a Migrant Workers Office

Bolivar said that of 10.5 million overseas Filipinos, 2.7 million were overseas Filipino workers (OFWs) directly under the Department of Migrant Workers (DMW); the DFA continued serving other overseas Filipinos and OFWs in places without a resident Migrant Workers Office (MWO). Antonio Atanacio Habulan, Executive Director of the Presidential Commission on Visiting Forces (PCVF), described the PCVF as the lead interagency mechanism for policy coordination, advice, guidance, and assessment to ensure that Status of Forces Agreements (SOFAs) serve the national interest. The PCVF also serves as the Commission’s Secretariat. It works with concerned departments and agencies to support implementation of SOFAs, uphold Philippine laws and state policies, and set guidelines for visiting forces and their civilian components, equipment, ships, vessels, and aircraft entering, moving within, staying temporarily in, and departing the country.
