### Rep. Presley C. De Jesus

Representative Presley C. De Jesus cited ₱56 million as the proposed FY 2027 NEP total for OMB and compared it with ₱50 million in 2026 programs. These were his stated figures; the opening presentation gave a different FY 2027 NEP amount, and this round did not reconcile the difference.

Budget sponsor Representative Lolita T. Asked whether OMB supported transferring its functions and assets to the Intellectual Property Office if the bill passed both houses, Javier first said the Board submitted to Congress’s judgment, then said she personally favored the transfer. Asked about OMB’s primary function, Javier said it continued its work monitoring storage devices, including K-pop media arriving in the Philippines, which OMB regulates and checks before issuing permits.

## Department of Foreign Affairs and attached agencies (DFA)

### Sponsor account

Budget sponsor Representative Sancho Fernando "Ando" F. Oaminal described DFA’s three foreign-policy pillars as national security, economic security, and protection of the rights and welfare of Filipinos overseas. He said the Philippines, as chair of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN), would host more than 640 meetings during the year. He cited the May 2026 Cebu summit’s ASEAN Leaders’ Declaration on Maritime Cooperation, which provides for an ASEAN Maritime Center to be hosted by the Philippines. He also described bilateral and multilateral partnerships as supporting alliances, trade and investment, and people-to-people ties, with maritime security a continuing priority. He identified the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea and the 2016 arbitral award as the country’s twin anchors in the West Philippine Sea.

Oaminal said DFA foreign-service posts assist Filipinos affected by conflict in the Middle East and coordinate with the Department of Migrant Workers (DMW) and other partners on repatriation and related assistance. He said DFA also engages governments and international organizations to safeguard Filipino seafarers and Philippine-flagged vessels in conflict-affected or high-risk areas. DFA remains responsible for non-worker Filipinos overseas, including students, tourists and permanent residents, and takes a lead role where no resident migrant workers’ office is present.

Oaminal reported that in 2026 DFA processed more than 1.5 million passports and provided services through 42 consular offices nationwide. The proposed FY 2027 budget would support preparations for a next-generation Philippine passport with enhanced security, updated contactless-chip technology and an option for additional pages, alongside continued information and communications technology, cybersecurity and data-protection investments. He said new embassies in Accra, Ghana, and Astana, Kazakhstan, were expected to open that year, while a Consulate General in Miami, Florida, was scheduled for 2027. He said the posts would bring services closer to Filipino communities and strengthen political and economic engagement.

### Rep. Presley C. De Jesus

Representative Presley C. De Jesus questioned sponsor Representative Sancho Fernando “Ando” F. Oaminal about overseas voting. De Jesus cited the committee-briefing account that ₱44.5 million had been appropriated for FY 2027 online voting and overseas-voter registration information campaigns; Oaminal confirmed it. Asked about persistently low turnout, Oaminal listed overseas workers’ mobility and work constraints, country restrictions and geographic dispersion, limited awareness of registration and the process, and doubts that votes make an impact. He said the latter was not necessarily distrust of the process; some voters felt their votes would not matter. DFA was conducting and planning more training and information campaigns.

Oaminal said turnout ranged from 24.16% in Asia and the Pacific to 7.9% in North and Latin America. He put the 2025 overseas registration total at 1,241,690 and said approximately 228,000 voted, or 18.4%. He attributed low participation to several factors, including digital literacy, and said DFA’s regional training covered registration and how to vote; the Commission on Elections (COMELEC) was involved. When De Jesus asked for past-year spending data, Oaminal said DFA was still gathering it.

De Jesus asked when Congress would receive a costed proposal for a hybrid or revised overseas voting system, including lessons from the 2025 turnout. Oaminal did not give a delivery date or costed proposal; he described training, information dissemination, direct communication with registered overseas Filipinos, and coordination with COMELEC as planned efforts. He hoped turnout would rise and expected COMELEC to increase its efforts, without giving a numeric target.

De Jesus asked whether overseas voting was funded separately by DFA and COMELEC. Oaminal said DFA had a separate ₱44.4 million for the purpose in 2026, while COMELEC had its own budget, for which he had no figures. Asked about duplication, he said travel costs were charged to each agency’s own office and that DFA and COMELEC had counterpart training expenses; he assured De Jesus that the expenses did not duplicate one another.

De Jesus asked Oaminal to confirm reports that DFA had lodged 39 diplomatic protests against China in 2026 and 296 from 2022 through 2026; Oaminal confirmed both figures. De Jesus also cited an approximately ₱14.48 billion 2027 request for the diplomacy program, which Oaminal confirmed. Asked how DFA assesses whether protests achieve diplomatic results, Oaminal said he believed the notes had helped tone down aggression and tensions, and that China had answered some notes individually and others in combined replies. He said there was no one-to-one correspondence between notes and responses and pointed to fewer reported attacks on Filipino fishermen and the Coast Guard as signs of lower tensions; he attributed that in part to the notes. He did not identify a formal performance framework or a measure of changed operational behavior at sea.

On the September 11 flare incident, Oaminal said DFA was preparing a diplomatic protest but would send it after receiving the complete incident report.

De Jesus cited the combined Consular and Assistance to Nationals (ATN) program as rising from approximately ₱7.47 billion in 2026 to ₱13.63 billion in 2027, an 82.4% increase; Oaminal agreed with that framing. Oaminal described ATN as an insurance-like reserve used according to assistance requests, not an amount that must be spent in full; unused prior-year funds return to the National Treasury. He said DFA requests around ₱1 billion annually for ATN and around ₱200 million for the Legal Assistance Fund.

### Rep. Leila M. de Lima

Representative Leila M. de Lima raised continued dredging around Liwliwa Beach in San Felipe, Zambales, describing reported shoreline damage and effects on resorts, workers and fishing grounds. Deputy Speaker Jose C. Alvarez, responding as DENR sponsor, denied that Liwliwa itself was being dredged, described the site as unstable unclassified forest where occupants had been warned, and referred to notices to vacate or demolish. He said the large vessels he had seen were removing lahar and sand from river mouths for Manila Bay reclamation. He said operators were mostly Filipino, while some used rented Chinese vessels; he also said DENR was verifying China Harbor Engineering Company. De Lima contrasted this with a regional director’s earlier statement that no Chinese firm dredged in Zambales and sought a reliable answer. She cited a Philippine Ports Authority list she said showed 67 vessels dredging in Botolan and San Felipe during 2020–2025, many with Chinese vessel names.

De Lima named Kangling 539, Mao Hua 8 and Andakang 3689 and asked whether the identities, dredging activity and permits had been verified, including reports tying Mao Hua 8 to an interception and Chinese nationals, and Andakang 3689 to China Harbor Engineering. Alvarez said some equipment could be Chinese while a Filipino entity held a permit or chartered a vessel; he referred to Marina permits for inland-water operation and said it was possible for Chinese-operated vessels to operate under such arrangements. He did not confirm that the named vessels had valid dredging permits or resolve whether they dredged in the Zambales rivers; he said that question remained to be checked. De Lima said the sponsor’s account required further verification. The exchange leaves her vessel-identity and permit questions unresolved, alongside Alvarez’s denial of dredging at Liwliwa and his separate account of river dredging for reclamation. De Lima asked whether DENR had assessed whether dredging could worsen known shoreline hazards before issuing or renewing permits; Alvarez reiterated that dredging was at river mouths without providing that assessment.

De Lima described reports concerning mining in Aroroy, Masbate, including alleged effects on waterways, fish, residents’ health and a tailings facility. She asked whether the DENR’s environmental compliance certificate reviews included an independent public-health assessment and Department of Health data. Alvarez did not answer those specific review questions. He said local complaints should receive attention, suggested consulting the governor, and said the DENR would immediately inspect when reports of this kind were brought to it. No completed assessment or inspection result was reported in this exchange.

De Lima questioned the department’s rising unused appropriations and asked why it sought additional FY 2027 funding. She cited department totals of ₱2.03 billion in 2024 and ₱2.23 billion in 2025, and first stated that the Office of the Secretary (OSEC) accounted for ₱1.96 billion, or 88.5% of unused funds; she later referred to ₱1.97 billion unused by OSEC alone in 2025. These are the member’s separately stated figures and were not reconciled in the exchange.

Alvarez described “almost” ₱2 billion as unused in 2025, then identified ₱1.9 billion as unobligated as of December 31, 2025 and carried over as a continuing appropriation. He attributed non-use to failed bidding, changes in Water Resources Management Program project sites, and deferred maintenance and operational work at a sewage treatment plant; he said the funds remained available and mentioned negotiation after repeated failed bidding. The sponsor’s ₱1.9 billion unobligated/continuing-appropriation figure is distinct from de Lima’s ₱1.96 billion and ₱1.97 billion OSEC figures. His explanation did not specifically resolve her question about the additional 2027 request. De Lima also cited a fall in the department’s disbursement rate from 84.4% in 2024 to 81.5% in 2025, and NWRB’s 58.1% rate.

De Lima said the Environmental Management Bureau (EMB) budget was shrinking while air pollution worsened; she cited reports placing pollution at 3.8 times the World Health Organization limit, named Pasay as the most polluted city in 2025, and identified Malabon, Caloocan, Quiapo and Mandaluyong in real-time monitoring. She asked whether the EMB had targeted interventions for these locations. Alvarez acknowledged EMB underperformance but responded with other budget items: ₱59 million for operations of seven newly legislated protected areas, ₱39 million for operating and maintaining nine marine-specific research centers, and ₱34 million in total for an implementation item whose name is unclear in the transcript. He also referred to 2,765 Forest Protection Officers. The exchange did not clearly establish the year or appropriation status of these items or their relationship to EMB’s air-quality budget. This response did not identify an EMB-specific targeted air-quality intervention or explain an even-versus-severity-based allocation. When de Lima asked whether this meant EMB was underperforming, Alvarez said the money was being used.

De Lima nevertheless supported passage of the proposed DENR budget conditionally, asking for concrete, monitorable delivery, improved absorption and enforcement on the ground. Alvarez assured her that her request had been noted by the DENR; no specific measurable commitment or later outcome was stated in this unit.

### Rep. Antonio L. Tinio

Representative Antonio L. Tinio questioned what the Philippines had agreed to in the critical-minerals MOU and whether Philippine law defines “critical minerals.” He contrasted this with the US statutory definition and said the US treated the minerals as critical to its economic and national security, including its defense and technology supply chains. He characterized Pax Silica and the MOU as a US effort to secure supply amid competition with China. Asked to identify a Philippine law or executive order defining or listing the minerals, DENR sponsor Deputy Speaker Jose C. Alvarez said he was not aware of a law. Tinio then stated that there was no executive-order list; Alvarez did not provide one or a Philippine statutory definition.

Tinio objected that the MOU’s “first look” language gives participants an expected first opportunity to invest in critical-mineral assets, and argued that its asset-sale, permitting and price-framework provisions could favor US interests and constrain Philippine choices. Alvarez replied that first look was only an opportunity, the MOU was nonbinding, and it did not surrender sovereignty. On permitting, he said the Executive was reviewing mining-permit documentation and that streamlining would not waive sovereignty or environmental requirements. He also argued that the arrangement could attract private investors to extract and process minerals in the Philippines and create economic activity. Tinio challenged the lack of a guaranteed purchase or investment commitment; Alvarez likewise described the arrangement as nonbinding and subject to negotiation, so no investment or purchase was assured in this exchange.

Tinio also questioned whether the MOU’s price-floor and preferential-trading language could leave the Philippines disadvantaged or steer sales away from other markets. Alvarez favored facilitating investment and trade while retaining sovereignty and environmental safeguards. Their disagreement over how the provisions would operate remained unresolved in the exchange.

Tinio distinguished DENR’s domestic air-quality monitoring and public advisories from the formal information request or consultation mechanism he cited under the ASEAN Agreement on Transboundary Haze Pollution. Alvarez described monitoring, radio and television warnings, and advice to wear a mask, but acknowledged that the formal step had not yet been taken and said it would proceed through ASEAN. Alvarez said the Philippines did not attend the July Bali meeting because DENR was not a member of the subcommittee; the exchange recorded an affirmative response about attending the October Conference of the Parties in Da Nang. When Tinio asked whether the harm to Filipinos would be raised at the November ASEAN Summit and how damages could be pursued, Alvarez did not give a plan or answer the damages question. He asked Tinio to memorialize the case he had presented so DENR could use it.
