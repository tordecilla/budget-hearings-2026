## Rep. Elijah Rumbaoa San Fernando

### Human-centered budget and social-sector delivery

San Fernando questioned how the FY 2027 proposal could be called human-centered while he saw continuing social-sector cuts. DBM said classroom provision had been evaluated and was not zero, health-workforce support remained intact, and the social sector remained the largest sector. It also pointed to a released SARO for free-higher-education arrears and CHED distribution to state universities and colleges; those were DBM's qualifications to the cuts critique, not San Fernando's acceptance of the characterization.

### Job quality, manufacturing, and competitiveness

San Fernando contrasted the government's manufacturing-job target with reported manufacturing and industry losses, and argued that retail and other elementary, low-paying work did not meet the test of quality employment. Balisacan distinguished wage-and-salary work from informal, retail, and trade work; he said the former was generally more regular and better quality, had improved over the longer term, and could recover after the recent slowdown. On San Fernando's call for high-value industrial jobs, Balisacan agreed on the objective and pointed to faster project execution, infrastructure and energy costs, alongside doing-business and institutional-trust constraints.
