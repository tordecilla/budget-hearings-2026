## PCSO presentation

### Mandate, operating model, and 2027 priorities

FY 2027 retail receipts were projected at PHP 68.55 billion, with PHP 67.18 billion in net receipts after estimated printing costs. Under Republic Act No. 1169, PCSO uses sweepstakes, lotteries and similar activities to fund health programs, medical assistance and services, and charitable activities of national character. Its 2027 priorities included landing craft vessels for coastal communities, medical-equipment donations, participation in a radiation-oncology center, new games and betting platforms to sustain charity funding, digital public services, and the transfer of operations to the PCSO Corporate Center in San Marcelino, Manila.

## Rep. Faustino Michael Carlos T. Dy III

### Sports Commission remittances and the Guiao ruling

Speaking as chair of the Youth and Sports Committee, Dy cited Guiao v. PAGCOR and PCSO, G.R. No. 223845, and the ruling concerning PCSO remittances to the Philippine Sports Commission: 30% of the charity-fund proceeds from six sweepstakes or lottery draws per year beginning in 2006. He contrasted that obligation with a reported ₱1.5 million remittance for 2025 and asked whether PCSO had fully complied, the exact accumulated amount due since 2006, how much had been remitted, and when any balance would be settled; he said he had heard an estimate of about ₱30 billion. Reyes said the dispute involved computation based on the old sweepstakes races and PCSO's interpretation of the Supreme Court ruling. Reyes also distinguished PCSO's percentage tied to six races or draws from PAGCOR's 5% obligation under Republic Act No. 6847. The answer did not reconcile the ₱45 million, ₱73 million, and approximately ₱122.419 million figures into one accounting basis, provide the exact accumulated unpaid balance, or give a settlement schedule.

## Rep. Jan "Atty. JP" Padiernos

### Landing craft vessels for island medical transport

Padiernos asked about the procurement and feasibility of PCSO's planned landing craft vessels, including PCSO's mandate, allocation, and the risk of burdening recipient local governments with maintenance costs. General Manager Melquiades Robles said the vessels were intended to make patient transport between island municipalities more effective by allowing an ambulance and patient to continue the trip without an extra vehicle transfer. He said recipient LGUs would secure MARINA licenses; PCSO would begin with around 10 vessels and proceed in later years. The vessels would go to provincial governments, which would schedule their use among municipalities. Robles added that the winning procurement bidder would maintain them for the first three years and train operating personnel.

## Rep. Mikaela Angela B. Suansing

### Rising sales versus taxes, dividends, and remittances

Committee Chair Rep. Mikaela Angela B. Suansing compared figures she described as PCSO sales—about ₱60.4 billion in 2023, ₱61.3 billion in 2024, ₱63.8 billion in 2025, ₱65.9 billion in 2026, and ₱67.2 billion in 2027—with total taxes, dividends, and other remittances that she said were about ₱22.99 billion in 2023, ₱22.26 billion in 2024, ₱21.85 billion in 2025, and ₱11 billion for the first half of 2026. She added that the UHC computation follows its applicable statutory basis. She described 2026 and 2027 as projections; the presentation reported net-receipts estimates near those values, but the hearing did not resolve the exact scope of her series, and the 2026 remittance figure covered only the first half of the year.
