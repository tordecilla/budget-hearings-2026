## Office of the Vice President budget

### OVP budget comparison

Senator Sherwin "Win" T. Gatchalian described the proposed FY 2027 budget of the Office of the Vice President (OVP) as an 11% decrease from the previous year. He viewed its socioeconomic programs as a way to give constituents relief and said the office was spending its budget efficiently.

### Socioeconomic programs: recalibration and beneficiary targets

Senator Imee R. Marcos supported extending traditional courtesy to the Office of the Vice President (OVP), then asked what its budget reduction of more than 11% would mean for programs and beneficiaries. Undersecretary Zuleika T. Lopez attributed much of the change to reprogramming or recalibration. Mag-Negosyo Ta-Day would concentrate on monitoring and evaluating its impact, with reduced beneficiary targets.

OVP Director for Operations Norman Baloro said the Pagbabago a Million Learners campaign was nearing its target of 1 million learners receiving bags, raincoats and dental kits, and expected to reach it before 2028. Marcos congratulated the OVP and asked how many Mag-Negosyo Ta-Day beneficiaries had continued operating businesses, emphasizing the need for monitoring and evaluation. She received no figure before moving to endorse the budget.

## Senator Christopher Lawrence T. Go

### Libreng Sakay: operations and route coverage

Go asked whether Libreng Sakay was continuing. OVP Director Norman Baloro confirmed that it was operational and had nine buses. Two served the Monumento-PITX EDSA Carousel route, and two operated between Commonwealth and Quiapo in both directions. He also listed the PITX-Naic, Cavite route and one bus each in Bacolod, Cebu City, Davao and Tacloban City.

Go urged the OVP to continue the service, which he said he had supported in previous years. He argued that the Vice President should be a working Vice President rather than a spare tire, and emphasized public servants' duty to help people needing social services.

## Senator Joseph Victor "JV" G. Ejercito

### Libreng Sakay and Mag-Negosyo Ta-Day

Ejercito asked whether the OVP would continue Libreng Sakay and Mag-Negosyo Ta-Day. He said higher fuel prices were leaving some operators unwilling to travel because they were losing money, and that continuation could help during the crisis; he said he would support the programs. Undersecretary Zuleika T. Lopez said Libreng Sakay continued daily with nine buses and expected its four-millionth passenger within two weeks. For Mag-Negosyo Ta-Day, the OVP was monitoring and evaluating whether beneficiaries had succeeded in businesses or livelihoods it provided. Ejercito then asked whether the office was sure it wanted to recalibrate the program and, if it was successful, whether recalibration might waste something that could help businesses. Lopez described the recalibration as involving the "program and program mix".

Ejercito affirmed support for OVP social programs despite political noise, expressed hope for national unity and hoped the Vice President would attend plenary.
