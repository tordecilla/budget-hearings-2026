### Rep. Antonio L. Tinio

Representative Antonio L. Tinio of ACT Teachers raised the July 14 incident in which a video of a public-school teacher being cited for riding without a helmet was posted on the Metropolitan Manila Development Authority (MMDA) page and drew widespread attention. Tinio distinguished the teacher’s traffic violation, which he did not contest, from posting the encounter with admonishment in a way the teacher said caused embarrassment and violated his privacy. He asked about the status of Gabriel Christian Go, then head of MMDA’s Special Operations Group Task Force for Road Clearing. Representative Alexandria “Queenie” Gonzales said Go remained an MMDA employee and was under administrative investigation connected to the incident, with a show-cause order and a resolution promised within the year. Gonzales first described him as not suspended, then clarified that he had been removed from field operations after MMDA received a letter from the Teachers’ Dignity Coalition and was monitoring clearing operations from the command center; she cited due-process protections and a five-day training, mentorship and coaching program on traffic management, courtesy, discipline, leadership, and anger and stress management. Tinio confirmed that the investigation concerned this incident and that Go was no longer in the field.

Tinio said the teacher had asked Go not to post the encounter, arguing that the request was clear in context even though it used an incomplete phrase. He said blurring the teacher’s face did not prevent identification by the school and community, and cited the National Privacy Commission and Commission on Human Rights’ position that blurring alone may not protect privacy when a person remains identifiable. Gonzales said the investigation would examine possible data-privacy violations, including the exchange between Go and the teacher. Tinio described prior public criticism of similar enforcement videos and asked MMDA to clarify its rules. Gonzales said MMDA records enforcement and traffic monitoring institutionally, while an employee’s personal posting could go beyond that practice; she said the investigation would determine whether Go crossed that line.

Tinio asked for the written social-media rule after citing public statements that personal posts about operations would be barred and that MMDA’s Public Information Office would publish operational photos and videos. Gonzales initially confused which sharing was barred, then clarified that personal posting was prohibited while institutional sharing was allowed. She promised to furnish Tinio that day with a complete copy of the regulation and Go’s show-cause order. Tinio contrasted this account with public comments by MMDA General Manager Nicolás Torre III suggesting there was no hard-and-fast restriction on personal blogging with MMDA footage. Gonzales said Torre was new and might not know the rule, and promised to remind him and employees. She also distinguished taking footage in a public place, which Chairman Artes had said was not prohibited, from posting it, and said MMDA was consulting the National Privacy Commission. Gonzales confirmed that personal vlogging of official MMDA operations was not allowed, while maintaining that institutional recording for enforcement records was established practice.

Tinio then read from an MMDA position paper responding to House Resolution No. 1370. He said the teacher had requested removal of the video and similar posts that demeaned people or violated privacy and dignity. He quoted MMDA’s categorical denial that it had authorized Go to videotape a valid clearing operation or legitimized content on the private “Dada Ku” channel. Co-sponsor Rep. Monique Yazmin Maria Q. Lagdameo agreed that MMDA had no control over that private channel. Tinio countered that the incident video remained on MMDA’s own Facebook page. Lagdameo said the related institutional post reported clearing operations but asserted that the video itself was not on the page; after Tinio asked MMDA to check its account, Lagdameo said the chairman was verifying and that the video would be taken down immediately if present. The session did not record the result of that check.

Tinio summarized the distinction as permitting institutional recording and posting but barring personal posts, while arguing that the teacher’s privacy deserved respect even where a violation had occurred. He said the teacher could still be identified despite blurring and should not have to face public humiliation to answer for a traffic offense. Lagdameo apologized on the institution’s behalf and promised measures to prevent recurrence. Tinio accepted the apology for the teacher and asked that subjects’ consent be respected when enforcement incidents are used to teach the public. Lagdameo said the suggestion would be considered and that the guidelines would again be conveyed to all employees.

Representative Antonio L. Tinio of ACT Teachers Party-list made a correction at the start of a later interpellation, referring back to his earlier exchange on the Metropolitan Manila Development Authority (MMDA). He retracted his claim that the July 14 post about a traffic ticket issued to a teacher appeared on the MMDA website, clarifying that it appeared only on a personal blog. Tinio apologized to the Chair, Chairman Artes and the MMDA for the incorrect claim; Deputy Speaker Mercedes K. Alvarez noted the correction.

### Rep. Presley C. De Jesus

Representative Presley C. De Jesus of PHILRECA Party-list asked Department of Justice (DOJ) sponsor Representative Keith Micah "Atty. Mike" D.L. Tan about border modernization. De Jesus said the Bureau of Immigration's border management information system enhancement received approximately PHP 3.8 billion in 2025 but disbursed PHP 103.3 million, or 2.7 percent. Tan acknowledged that the three-phase e-gate project was delayed. Phase 1, funded under the 2024 GAA, was awarded on December 18, 2025 after a failed bidding; during rebidding, a donation of e-gates covered some planned units and required changes to the procurement process. Deployment of 47 e-gates was underway at Clark, Mactan-Cebu, Davao and Bohol-Panglao airports. Phase 2 was in procurement and phase 3 still needed technical specifications. The Bureau aimed to obligate phases 1 and 2 in 2026; the phase 3 portion without specifications would be carried over. Tan said the airports De Jesus asked about were already included in 2025 and 2026 funding, with implementation awaited and delivery expected by December 2026.

De Jesus asked whether valid court orders and legally authorized alerts reached immigration counters in real time, and how the Bureau distinguished monitoring alerts from legal restrictions on departure. Tan said an official order was encoded upon receipt. He described alert orders as commonly arising from arrest warrants or requests by other agencies, especially during investigations, while restrictions on departure could arise from other factors. De Jesus then asked how travelers could challenge mistaken identity. Tan described an internal check by immigration officers, with National Bureau of Investigation (NBI) or other law-enforcement clearance when identity remained unclear. He acknowledged that incomplete data could lead to mistaken holds and missed flights, and said the Bureau could issue clearance and wanted modernization to improve cross-checking.

De Jesus turned to the Bureau of Corrections (BuCor) custody and safekeeping program, comparing PHP 8.47 billion in 2026 with PHP 9.81 billion proposed for 2027. He cited a National Prisons congestion rate of about 250 percent in 2025 and eight infrastructure projects worth PHP 2.05 billion whose completion targets had slipped by 10 to 1,456 days. Asked how many usable spaces the 2027 allocation would add, Tan gave a target of 6,210 additional persons deprived of liberty (PDL) capacity across penal farms. He said cell capacity varied by security category: about four people in maximum-security cells, 15 in medium-security cells and 20 in minimum-security cells. Asked how the 2027 congestion target of 160 percent would be reached, Tan said BuCor projects remained underway despite delays; Tan said legal action against contractors was being considered and gave December 2026 or June 2027 target dates for several regional and maximum-security facilities.

Tan said Iwahig covered 32,000 hectares and included maximum-, medium- and minimum-security facilities; most people there were in minimum security and nearing release. De Jesus asked about prison health facilities. Tan said BuCor was struggling because the allowance was only PHP 30 per PDL per day, and the Bureau was seeking an increase to improve care for sick prisoners. De Jesus urged additional funding on human-rights grounds. Asked whether staffing, water, sanitation, health facilities, security equipment and operating expenses were funded with new buildings, Tan said they were included.

De Jesus asked about the DOJ Special Protection of Children Activities, citing a 6.9 percent disbursement rate in 2025 and asking why utilization was low. Tan attributed underspending to delays in member-agency comments and said the United Nations Children's Fund (UNICEF) paid for some partner workshops.

Asked what resources were provided in 2027 for online child sexual abuse or exploitation cases, Tan said the Inter-Agency Council Against Trafficking (IACAT) and child-protection activities were seeking additional funding to at least match 2026 levels as cases became more complex and cross-border. He described an Office of the Secretary request for time-sensitive investigative, operational, intelligence and victim-protection work. De Jesus also cited a 46.9 percent 2025 disbursement rate for anti-trafficking activities. Tan cited delays in consultancy terms of reference, ICT equipment still in procurement, a scanner paid for in early 2026, representation expenses that did not proceed amid natural disasters, and trainings that did not proceed.

De Jesus asked which agencies were investigating the August 18, 2026 Zamboanga school shooting and when DOJ's National Prosecution Service or National Bureau of Investigation would become involved. Tan said the DOJ and NBI were investigating, cases had been filed against the shooter's father and the school security guard, and the department was continuing prosecution in court. De Jesus supported DOJ funding for law enforcement and urged action on online platforms after the attack was livestreamed; Tan said DOJ was coordinating with the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) on such incidents.

### Rep. Sarah Jane I. Elago

### Rep. Renee Louise M. Co

Rep. Renee Louise M. Co used her manifestation before the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP) budget interpellation ended to raise reports of harassment, violence, and legal actions against indigenous communities resisting large mining companies. She cited a case in Kasibu, Nueva Vizcaya, where North Luzon Mineral Resources Corporation sought to exploit ancestral lands; she said NCIP had issued a certificate of non-overlap that the community was contesting through a petition to the agency.

Co also cited residents of Bulalakaw and Ginawang in Benguet affected by Crescent Mining and Development Corporation. She said the host community initially voted against the project, but the voting area was later expanded to include other barangays, diluting the host communities' opposition. She said a case remained pending before NCIP.

Co raised the case of Bugkalot people in Dupaks del Norte, Nueva Vizcaya, who she said had been displaced from the Cordillera highlands to Nueva Vizcaya by mining in the 1980s, before the Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act and before NCIP existed. She said they had no recourse at the time to assert rights to ancestral lands and domains. Co further alleged that communities defending land and the environment faced harassment, red-tagging, violence, and multiple lawsuits, and said NCIP had done little to protect indigenous peoples and advocates. These were Co's accounts and allegations; no sponsor response appears in this unit.

Co said she and others remained unsatisfied with questions raised in the debate, while noting that colleagues had already asked them. Before the House terminated the NCIP interpellation period, she asked that the information requested by members be provided promptly. Rep. Arnan C. Panaligan joined the motion to terminate, and Deputy Speaker Ferjenel G. Biron, M.D., heard no objection and declared the interpellations terminated. This was a procedural closure; no appropriation approval was recorded in this unit.
