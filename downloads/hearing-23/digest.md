## At a glance

- **FY 2027 food-security and irrigation case:** DA presented food security, farmer and fisherfolk livelihoods, productivity, logistics, resilience and accountability as the case for its FY 2027 request. NIA paired its development, repair and climate-adaptation agenda with a national irrigation coverage gap, but said the NEP allocation was far below its proposed budget.

- **Irrigation funding, selection and flexibility:** Across multiple regional rounds, members challenged low or zero allocations, delayed repairs and the basis for project priorities. NIA repeatedly cited limited fiscal space and ongoing Tier 1 commitments, while asking to return to a program-based appropriation structure that could redirect funds from delayed projects to validated, implementation-ready work with oversight.

- **Farm roads, post-harvest systems and disclosure:** Members described a large farm-to-market-road backlog, missing access roads, delayed releases, high per-kilometre costs and questions about project coordinates and unusually large projects. - **Regional commodity programs and agency follow-through:** Members sought support for abaca, coconut, palm oil, banana, sugar, high-value crops, fisheries and aquaculture, often pairing local project requests with questions about agency execution. Officials expressed support for selected proposals, studies, coordination and future requests, but many funding levels, legislation, procurement actions and local allocations remained unresolved. ## Department of Agriculture and National Irrigation Administration

### Food security and producer livelihoods as the budget test

DA Secretary Francisco P. Tiu Laurel Jr. said the FY 2027 request must deliver both sufficient, safe, nutritious and affordable food and a decent, sustainable livelihood for farmers and fisherfolk. He linked the request to productivity, farm and fishery infrastructure, lower post-harvest losses, logistics and market access, appropriate technology, stronger value chains, climate resilience, monitoring and accountability.

### Agricultural conditions and production bottlenecks

Undersecretary Asis G. Perez reported 3.1% agriculture, forestry and fisheries gross-value-added growth in 2025 after a 1.5% contraction in 2024, while the sector’s share of the economy was 7.9%. He cited 10.44 million people in the RSBSA as of July 15, 2026, an ageing producer base, small and fragmented farms, and persistent poverty among farmers and fisherfolk. The presentation identified shrinking agricultural land, inadequate irrigation, low soil productivity, high input costs, low mechanization and climate exposure as linked constraints. It said about 2.2 million of 3.13 million irrigable hectares were served, leaving roughly 29.75% still needing irrigation; 82% of rice and corn areas had low-to-moderate soil productivity.

### Infrastructure, logistics and legislative reform agenda

Perez described small-scale irrigation and canal work that created more than 21,400 hectares of new service area and restored more than 3,600 hectares, alongside expanded soil-health laboratory capacity. He argued for investment in drying, milling, storage, processing, roads, ports, cold-chain facilities and ice plants to reduce post-harvest and logistics losses. The presentation reported a US$11.12 billion agricultural trade deficit in 2025, said exports remain concentrated in a few commodities, and presented livestock and poultry self-sufficiency under the Animal Industry Reform Law as part of reducing import dependence. It also asked Congress to pair budget support with agriculture-extension and other structural reforms, with enhanced productivity, increased income and poverty reduction as the stated key result areas.
