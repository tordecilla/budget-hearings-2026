## Office of the Presidential Adviser on Peace, Reconciliation and Unity (OPAPRU) budget

### Proposed program components and reported utilization

The proposed FY2027 allocation was described as supporting normalization and transformation programs, Payapa at Masaganang Pamayanan (PAMANA) infrastructure in geographically isolated and conflict-affected communities, other peacebuilding initiatives, and agency operations and mandatory costs. OPAPRU sought favorable consideration of the proposed budget.

Within the proposed FY2027 program, PAMANA had a PHP 4.9 billion allocation for infrastructure across Bangsamoro and adjacent areas and Communist Party of the Philippines-New People's Army-National Democratic Front (CPP-NPA-NDF), Rebolusyonaryong Partido ng Manggagawa-Pilipinas-Revolutionary Proletarian Army-Alex Boncayao Brigade (RPMP-RPA-ABB), and Cordillera communities. The stated distribution was PHP 1.6 billion for the Moro Islamic Liberation Front (MILF) peace process, PHP 624 million for the Moro National Liberation Front (MNLF), PHP 1.5 billion for local conflict transformation, and PHP 1.1 billion combined for RPMP-RPA-ABB and the Cordillera-Bodong Administration-Cordillera People's Liberation Army (CBA-CPLA). These were allocations at that stage, subject to further validation of project implementation, readiness and program compliance; PAMANA would also prioritize completing projects funded in FY2024–2026.

For comparison, OPAPRU reported that it obligated 97% (PHP 6.9 billion) of its PHP 7.1 billion FY2025 appropriations as of December 31, 2025. As of September 30, 2026, it had utilized 73% (PHP 6.1 billion) of its PHP 8.3 billion FY2026 agency appropriation, including PHP 4.6 billion obligated for PAMANA infrastructure; the balance supported regular operations and ongoing normalization and transformation programs.

### Decommissioning numbers, normalization benefits and implementation delays

Senator Sherwin "Win" T. Gatchalian asked how many Moro Islamic Liberation Front (MILF) combatants remained to be decommissioned, how many still needed educational assistance and what challenges were delaying normalization. OPAPRU Presidential Assistant Wilben M. Mayor said 26,145 had been decommissioned and about 14,000 remained, but the government and MILF panels were still awaiting the list for that group. OPAPRU Secretary Mel Senen S. Sarmiento put the original MILF figure at 40,000 and said 26,145 had completed phases 1–3, leaving 13,855 for phase 4. Sarmiento said PHP 100,000 had been given in cash, with the rest of the socioeconomic package supplied through regular government services. He explained that decommissioning proceeds in parallel with government delivery of agreed commitments, with weapons released commensurately.

Sarmiento said the Comprehensive Agreement on the Bangsamoro and Bangsamoro Organic Law did not state a monetary amount for the socioeconomic package, but a later commitment set it at PHP 1 million per decommissioned combatant. OPAPRU mainly monitors delivery by other agencies; data privacy and the absence of data-sharing agreements made beneficiary validation difficult and slowed implementation. The existing package also fit the needs of older combatants poorly. He said a government–MILF Peace Implementing Panel meeting in Kuala Lumpur approved five of seven items, including an adjustment to make the package more responsive, at the September 25 meeting; Sarmiento said further work and an additional proposal were still needed, with phase-four weapons decommissioning hoped for by 2027. He traced the delay in bilateral normalization work to the vacancy after the government panel chair resigned in February; activities stopped at the MILF’s request until Sarmiento was appointed chair on July 15.

Gatchalian calculated that applying the PHP 1 million package to the roughly 14,000 remaining combatants would imply PHP 14 billion, assuming all received it. This was his estimate of the normalization commitment, not an approved appropriation. When he asked what OPAPRU had allocated for decommissioning, Sarmiento declined to disclose the working proposal while it remained under development following MILF concurrence on September 25; he offered a later briefing during a recess. The public exchange did not establish an approved decommissioning allocation.
