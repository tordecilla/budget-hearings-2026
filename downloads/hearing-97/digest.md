### Unused appropriations and modernization funding

### Defense priorities and force posture

Teodoro described the DND and AFP as focused on deterring aggression in the West Philippine Sea and other areas, sustaining internal-security gains, and improving presence and logistics across the country's 7,600 islands.

The Army was assessing operational areas and strategic basing; the Philippine Navy needed platforms, dockyards and berthing facilities; the Philippine Air Force needed to sustain and upgrade systems; and the Philippine Marine Corps was assessing force structure, equipment, mobility and deployability. ### Spending disparity and resilience

In a separate interjection, Lacson said China's defense expenditure was 52 times the Philippines' and stated it as US$335 billion, or roughly PHP 2.1 trillion. He asked how the country could compensate for the disparity and improvise. The spoken peso conversion was not reconciled with the US-dollar figure.

Answering Lacson's question about compensating for the capability disparity, Teodoro said the Philippines should become a less inviting target by building resilience across its land and maritime domains. He described land as 20% of the country's territory, then gave a 20-to-30% range, and contrasted it with an 80% maritime domain; he said maritime development was needed for land resilience. He said the reported Chinese figure understated spending because some resources went to the maritime militia and Coast Guard, and offered to discuss options in executive session.

Lacson said the Philippines could not match China's warships and missiles and urged a less costly but effective program inspired by Ukraine, alongside alliances rather than reliance on allies alone. He said the strategy was deterrence rather than retaliation: the Philippines would not match China pound for pound but would seek to make the decision to act as unfavorable as possible.
