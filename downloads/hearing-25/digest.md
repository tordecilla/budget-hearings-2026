## At a glance

- **DA and NIA funding priorities:** NIA warned that its reduced irrigation allocation could delay ongoing work, while PFDA sought money for both existing and new fish ports. DA presented a prioritized set of farm, fisheries and food-system additions. Members asked for ranked proposals for amendment review; none was appropriated in this hearing.

- **Irrigation flexibility with safeguards:** NIA asked to return to a program-based budget format and to relax a 2026 special provision that it said made it difficult to shift from stalled projects to ready work. Members recognized implementation constraints but insisted that any new rule retain validation, transparency and accountability safeguards. The committee requested NIA’s proposed language and prioritized list for later budget-amendment review.

- **Food security, rice buying and market logistics:** Members examined palay buying under EO 100, NFA storage, Sagipsaka implementation, farm-to-market roads, food hubs, KADIWA and the role of public markets. DA described available rice-purchase funds, expanded warehouse capacity, food-hub plans and KADIWA sites, while several requested reports, cost details, supplier inquiries and a broader food-logistics plan remained pending.

- **Climate resilience, fisheries and local production:** The discussion linked drought and disaster recovery to crop insurance, replanting inputs, soil testing, solar irrigation, fish ports, cold storage, seaweed, aquaculture and coastal market access. Agency officials described ongoing work, proposals and expected future facilities in Eastern Samar, Palawan and other areas, but many district-specific projects, funding levels and delivery dates remained conditional or unfunded.

The record preserves material differences in figures and leaves clinical, funding, market-access and implementation outcomes open.

- **Closing action:** After the final member remarks, Senior Deputy Speaker Ferdinand L. Hernandez moved to terminate the DA and NIA budget hearing.
