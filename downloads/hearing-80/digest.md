## Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev)

### Rep. Sarah Jane I. Elago

Rep. Sarah Jane I. Elago questioned the proposed economy's reliance on household consumption and the effect of VAT on purchasing power. She cited projected FY 2027 VAT collections of PHP860 billion and asked whether the budget implied an increase of PHP97.6 billion, or 12.8 percent. Appropriations Committee sponsor Rep. Bella Vanessa B. Suansing said the Department of Finance (DOF) was better placed to answer the VAT revenue projection. Turning to production, Elago said agriculture, forestry, and fishing contributed 1.8 percent to GDP growth from 2023 to 2025, compared with 19.8 percent for industry, and argued that budget support for local agriculture, fuel assistance, and food self-sufficiency was inadequate. Suansing attributed the sector's weak performance to external shocks, including El Nino and typhoons, as well as persistent low productivity, declining farm sizes, and variable yields.

Elago asked what would help people remain above the poverty line after DEPDev reported that 8.8 million people had moved above it between 2021 and 2025. Suansing confirmed the figure and explained that it meant their incomes crossed the poverty threshold. Elago questioned whether that alone meant a stable or sufficient income and cited poverty thresholds of PHP16,842 for a five-person family in the National Capital Region (NCR) and PHP14,634 outside NCR, describing the NCR threshold as PHP488 per day. She said minimum wages in 12 regions were below DEPDev's threshold, and that many of the four million jobs generated since 2020 were underemployed. She argued that emergency assistance received much more funding than livelihood and micro, small, and medium enterprise programs, and pressed for decent wages, stable jobs, and added household income so families would not fall back into poverty.

On the NAIA public-private partnership (PPP), Elago asked whether affected stakeholders had been consulted before approval, how many consultations took place, and whether recommendations were included in the concession agreement. Suansing said consultation occurs during the feasibility study; DEPDev was gathering the project details and agreed to submit them. Elago raised concerns about large retailers' role in public markets and the effect on small vendors; Suansing said market administration was within local government jurisdiction. On PPP monitoring, fee changes, and grievances, Suansing said oversight depends on each project and contract, but grievances could be reported to the PPP Center. She confirmed that the Center was handling complaints about privatization projects and promised to furnish the case information. Elago called for the House to review the PPP Center's work.

### Rep. Renee Louise M. Co

Rep. Renee Louise M. Co asked sponsor Rep. Bella Vanessa B. Suansing to explain the Philippine Development Plan statement that education policies had undermined better-performing private schools, as reflected in declining private-school enrollment. Suansing attributed the statement to the Plan being drafted during the pandemic, when many private schools closed, and agreed it was not a development indicator; she said she would provide a detailed justification after the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev) retrieved its basis. Co raised the constitutional complementarity of public and private education, sought a written explanation and possible revision, and warned that public-private classroom or commercial-space projects could be used to justify cuts to public education. She also asked how the government would meet the Plan and Education 2030 spending target: the Plan cited education spending of 4-6% of GDP, while Co said the NEP ratio was about 3.95%. She pointed to reduced year-on-year budgets for 49 of 113 state universities and colleges and asked what DEPDev had done to advance the target. Suansing said education remained a priority, but DEPDev recommends policy and does not control sector budget allocations; she cited its recommendations through EDCOM and its technical-secretariat role, without giving a path to the spending target. Co requested that the Department report the economic and social effects of missing the targets.

Co then asked whether DEPDev had assessed development trade-offs from the Philippines-United States Critical Minerals Memorandum of Understanding, which she viewed as potentially giving the United States preferential access and constraining other partnerships. Suansing said DEPDev was not involved in the proposed memorandum and had not reviewed its effects. Co connected the agreement to Executive Order 122 and asked whether DEPDev had contributed to that framework or assessed its effects; Suansing said no assessment had yet been conducted and DEPDev had not been brought into implementation. She assured Co that DEPDev would examine the concerns once involved. Co requested findings on the agreement and follow-on actions, including their development effects, to accompany the requested education explanation, and questioned whether decisions served Philippine interests. No assessment or substantive answer on the trade-offs was provided in this exchange.

### Rep. Sergio C. Dagooc

Rep. Sergio C. Dagooc pressed the Department of Economy, Planning, and Development (DEPDev) over the age of data supplied for his question about electricity and oil costs as shares of production and service costs. He said he needed those shares to judge whether a proposed tax reduction would reach consumer and pump prices. Although he acknowledged DEPDev's timely response to his budget-briefing request, the figures supplied through the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) were based on 2018 input-output data. He argued that electricity prices had changed too much for those shares to guide present budget and investment priorities, and questioned whether the old data underlay DEPDev's approach to high power costs and Agus-Pulangi rehabilitation. Dagooc asked who in DEPDev prepared the reply and who at PSA supplied the figures. He also challenged the frequency of PSA's business and industry survey, invoking its duty to provide timely, accurate statistics and warning that stale inputs could distort DEPDev's economic models. She attributed survey timing to the interagency PSA Board, chaired by the DEPDev secretary.

Dagooc rejected the survey-cycle explanation as an answer to whether the figures were current enough for economic planning and whether the timing met PSA's charter. He pressed the agency's budget justification, then cut short his questioning of DEPDev and PSA to leave time for other members. The offered preliminary figures and the requested preparer identities were not supplied during this round.

Rep. Sergio C. Dagooc manifested continuing frustration with how the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) fulfilled its charter, saying its data-gathering problems and a six-year survey cycle were hard to reconcile with the annual utility surveys he completed as a cooperative manager. ## Department of Budget and Management (DBM)

### Rep. Iris Marie Demesa Montes

4K Rep. Iris Marie Demesa Montes questioned the Department of Budget and Management (DBM) over the proposed 2027 health allocation and what she saw as a threat to universal health care (UHC) services. Budget sponsor Rep. Bella Vanessa B. Suansing attributed the health-budget decline to Health Facilities Enhancement Program utilization and implementation delays, a return of that program and medical-assistance funding to National Expenditure Program levels, and a one-time PHP60 billion return to the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth) that could not recur. Montes compared PhilHealth's PHP129 billion current-year allocation with PHP74 billion proposed for 2027. Suansing answered that the current-year figure included the one-time return: excluding it gives PHP69 billion, making the proposed allocation PHP5 billion higher on that basis. Montes still questioned whether the underlying public contribution would support primary care, outpatient and catastrophic benefits; the sponsor did not give service-by-service targets in this exchange.

Montes then sought the sources of the proposed PhilHealth allocation and an accounting of past sin-tax, Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corporation (PAGCOR) and Philippine Charity Sweepstakes Office (PCSO) remittances. After initially calling one component a national subsidy, Suansing corrected the breakdown to PHP55 billion from sin tax and PHP19 billion from PAGCOR and PCSO shares. Montes contrasted PhilHealth's arrears figure with the smaller DBM figure; Suansing specified that DBM's amount covered PAGCOR and PCSO, leaving the broader figures unreconciled. At Montes's request, the sponsor committed to submit DBM's official itemized arrears and remittance accounting, including legacy premium-subsidy obligations. On Montes's question about unremitted earmarked collections, Suansing said UHC revenue is earmarked under law but lacks a cash-tracking mechanism, and relayed DBM's position that the variance remains in the general fund. Montes argued that government must honor those earmarks and timely finance PhilHealth rather than leave contributors bearing the gap.

Montes also asked where proceeds of an ADB-JICA UHC policy loan went, how its first three subprograms were financed and repaid, and whether their health-system targets had been met. Suansing explained that a policy loan enters the general fund and is attributed to UHC programs across the Department of Health (DOH), including facilities and medical assistance, rather than exclusively to PhilHealth.
