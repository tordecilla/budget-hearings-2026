### Project selection and convergence-program changes

Dizon said DPWH had discarded the parametric formula previously used to prepare project priorities. For FY 2027, district and regional offices were directed first to finish unfinished projects, then fund repair and maintenance of critical infrastructure, close gaps in incomplete systems such as flood-control works and bypass roads, and address immediate community needs through basic infrastructure.

DPWH moved the former SIPAG projects into BIP and said it had removed other convergence programs from its budget so the responsible departments could identify their own priorities. Tourism-related roads were to be lodged with the Department of Tourism (DOT), roads leading to airports or ports with the Department of Transportation (DOTr), and roads leading to economic zones with the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI); the former TICAS program of the Department of National Defense (DND) had already been removed. Those agencies could still ask DPWH to implement a project through a memorandum of agreement and transfer funds after enactment. The presentation said the remaining DPWH programs included water systems under the Rainwater Collection and Springs Development Act, the Quick Response Fund for calamities, and Ligtas Pinoy Centers.

### 2026 procurement and implementation status

Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) Secretary Vivencio B. Dizon reported that implementation had fallen behind in 2026. He attributed the delay to the absence of DPWH's usual early procurement, with allotment releases arriving only in late May or early June; stricter procurement documentation; and changes at the Department of Trade and Industry's Philippine Contractors Accreditation Board (PCAB), where more than 1,500 contractor licenses remained pending. He said contractors without current licenses could not bid or receive awards, forcing some bidding processes to restart. Dizon said DPWH would work with PCAB to speed licensing.

Dizon reported that 13,553 of 17,765 nationwide projects, or 76% by project count, had been awarded or completed procurement and were awaiting formal award under the election ban. He said the President had signed the postponement of barangay elections, but publication was still pending before awards could proceed. Separately, he said 66% of DPWH's 2026 capital-outlay allocation, PHP 305.79 billion of PHP 459 billion, had been awarded. He explained that the percentages differed because large consolidated projects, including Maharlika Highway segments, remained in procurement. Some Special Allotment Release Orders (SAROs) were still pending completion of procurement requirements; Dizon said DPWH expected to release them soon, begin bidding and monitor implementation. He also acknowledged low budget utilization due to delayed procurement.

### Urgent road, drainage, bridge and pumping-station needs

Dizon requested PHP 700 million for repair, reconstruction or construction at road slips and collapses, including slope protection on primary and secondary national roads. He described recurring failures, including a collapse on the road between Bukidnon and Davao, which he said was not caused by a typhoon and had been repaired using savings. He said the Quick Response Fund (QRF) could not cover that repair because it was not a disaster. He also described a vessel striking the Navotas seawall, which led to severe flooding; because it was a man-made event, QRF and disaster funds were unavailable, so DPWH used savings to repair the damage. He cited a similar seawall problem in Legazpi City, Albay.

Dizon said DPWH sought the same PHP 700 million amount for critical drainage repairs or upgrades outside routine maintenance and for road-safety measures during the months before contractors can repair dangerous road damage. He invited the Senate to add safeguards for these requests and said DPWH would follow them.

DPWH also requested PHP 500 million to replace bridges deemed immediately necessary. Dizon cited the collapse of the old Pigatan Bridge and a temporary replacement at Kabagan-Santa Maria Bridge while a Commission on Audit case and investigation remained unresolved. He said 600 of DPWH's 9,000 bridges had capacity below 10 tons, and described temporary replacements able to carry vehicles up to 40 tons. DPWH separately sought an unspecified lump sum to retrofit or strengthen permanent bridges while replacement funding is pending.

Dizon then requested PHP 150 million for urgent rehabilitation of pumping stations. He said 35 of 71 stations in the National Capital Region were inoperable when he arrived and DPWH had repaired 19 using savings and other sources. The request covered stations nationwide, including areas such as Bulacan, Pampanga and urban areas in the Visayas.

### Eight DPWH budget red flags and accountability response

Senator Sherwin "Win" T. Gatchalian credited the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) with adopting more transparent and granular budget descriptions, feasibility studies and certificates of implementability; he said reforms begun with the department the prior year carried into the 2026 General Appropriations Act (GAA). He said he and the committee still found red flags in the proposed 2027 National Expenditure Program (NEP), and would submit a detailed list so DPWH could correct the entries before the committee report. He described these as flags for review and said he was assuming they were not intentional. His team said it manually checked the list item by item with accounting-graduate interns from the Pamantasan ng Lungsod ng Valenzuela, whom he called "Valenzuela Intelligence," rather than relying on artificial intelligence alone.

Gatchalian identified eight categories. Missing station numbers affected 2,570 projects worth about PHP 83 billion. Duplicate project entries accounted for 189 projects worth about PHP 3 billion; examples included similarly titled seawalls, drainage, water-supply and multipurpose-building work, sometimes with spelling differences. Projects reappearing from 2026 to 2027 accounted for 258 projects worth about PHP 20 billion. He said some repeat entries may reflect phasing, but suggested labeling a first phase "construction" and the next "completion" to make the distinction clear.

Projects without coordinates totaled about 3,500 projects worth about PHP 83 billion, including remote flood-control work, multipurpose buildings and water-supply projects. Lump-sum entries across programs were about PHP 14 billion; Gatchalian noted that DPWH had explained these as contingency funds for repairs needed during the year. Overlapping or fragmented entries totaled 533 projects worth about PHP 63 billion. Examples included projects with the same starting station but different endpoints and a single contiguous project in Misamis Occidental split into separate PHP 135 million entries. Unspecified work scopes covered 62 projects worth PHP 1.5 billion, including multipurpose-building items that did not say whether the facility was an evacuation center, covered court or another type. Conflicting work types covered 20 projects worth PHP 386 million, including construction and rehabilitation listed for the same road in the same year.

Gatchalian gave an overall total of 7,107 projects worth about PHP 251 billion. He said many reforms had cleaned up earlier lists, but items still escaped scrutiny, and asked district engineers and regional directors to clean their data before it reached central DPWH.

DPWH Secretary Vivencio B. Dizon said the department had added layers of protection and requirements for district engineers and regional offices. He said each district engineer signed a Certificate of Implementability attesting that entries had no duplicates, previous funding or overlaps. If a listed issue could not be explained, DPWH would review it with the Senate Committee on Finance; Dizon said the department would prosecute a district engineer if a signed certification proved false. He also said some apparent duplication could be explained by phased projects, but DPWH would clean up entries that could not be justified. Gatchalian said district and regional staff should resolve problems before submission to central office, called the written certification an accountability measure, and said he would provide the committee's data to DPWH.
