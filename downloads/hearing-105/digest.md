## At a glance

- **Scrutiny, public trust, and participation:** Civil-society organizations challenged discretionary spending, project insertions and limited public access to budget records, and asked for a regular role in budget decisions. Senate Committee on Finance Subcommittee A Chair Senator Joseph Victor "JV" G. Ejercito described plans to improve transparency and participation and said concerns could be brought to agencies and colleagues. He said he could not promise adoption of every proposal but committed to seeking agency answers and bringing concerns to colleagues..

- **Health financing and patient access:** Health advocates urged stronger Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth) coverage, release of earmarked revenues and restoration of prevention and community services, while questioning discretionary Medical Assistance for Indigent and Financially Incapacitated Patients (MAIFIP). Ejercito and Senator Panfilo "Ping" M. Lacson discussed increased subsidies and changes to funding rules. Senate President Senate President Sherwin "Win" T. Gatchalian distinguished earmarking from appropriation; Action for Economic Reforms Representative Atty. Paula Mae B. Tanquieng argued that the earmarked funds must reach PhilHealth in full.

- **Education, social protection, and child protection:** Organizations sought Department of Education (DepEd) funding for learning recovery, school meals, counseling and inclusion, alongside child-protection services. Parent leaders urged protection of the Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program (4Ps) and long-term support. Ejercito said he would try to restore or augment mental-health funding and take the education proposals into account.

- **Transport, infrastructure, water, and climate resilience:** Transport and climate advocates sought safe walking and cycling, stronger public transport and water infrastructure aligned with drought risk. Ejercito pledged to seek restoration for the Metro Manila Subway Project and North-South Commuter Railway, and said a meeting would determine foreign-assisted project needs, including loan proceeds and government counterpart funding.

- **Inclusive services, cultural heritage, and agriculture:** Disability, heritage and agriculture presenters pressed for accessible services, usable public data, conservation and farmer-led sustainable production. Responses included support for heritage preservation and agricultural programs; the proposed funding and reallocation plans remained requests for consideration.

- **Closing and plenary consideration:** At closing, Ejercito said civil-society papers had been made available for committee deliberations and listed agency budgets that were deemed submitted for plenary consideration after not being taken up at committee level. This procedural step did not approve those budgets. He thanked participants and adjourned the briefing.

## Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) budget

### Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) budget review

Ejercito said the FY 2027 DPWH budget in the General Appropriations Bill (GAB) was more than ₱11.49 billion above the National Expenditure Program (NEP), while project counts rose from 11,420 in the NEP to 16,244 in the GAB. ### DPWH water infrastructure allocation and drought exposure

Aksyon Klima Pilipinas National Coordinator John Leo Algo compared the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) water-infrastructure project allocation with drought projections from the Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration (PAGASA). He said the October 2026 map showed drought exposure in areas including Bicol and Eastern Visayas, with Western Visayas and Mindoro also exposed in the January 2027 projection, and asked whether they received the largest shares of DPWH water-infrastructure funding; he answered no. He pointed to rainwater harvesting and solar water pumps and argued that government projects should use needs-based, multi-probabilistic assessment, which he said was required under Section 40 of the 2026 General Appropriations Act (GAA) and reflected in the National Expenditure Program (NEP). Senator Joseph Victor "JV" G. Ejercito replied that, in earlier DPWH and National Irrigation Administration (NIA) budget hearings, he had recommended long-term water retention and impounding alongside irrigation, so rainy-season water could be stored for dry periods. He said concrete-lined riverbanks speed water flow and limit groundwater and aquifer replenishment, and hoped that would guide the approach.

## Cross-agency social assistance budget

### FY2027 ayuda allocations and reform proposals

People's Budget Coalition (PBC) Co-convenor Adolfo Jose Montesa said the coalition identified more than ₱1 trillion in 2027 budget red flags: ₱210 billion in soft pork through patronage-driven ayuda, ₱695 billion in hard pork through politicized infrastructure, and ₱112 billion in unprogrammed appropriations, which he called shadow pork. 

Montesa said the National Expenditure Program (NEP) proposed ₱72 billion for AICS (Assistance to Individuals in Crisis Situation), MAIFIP (Medical Assistance for Indigent and Financially Incapacitated Patients), TUPAD (Tulong Panghanapbuhay sa Ating Disadvantaged/Displaced Workers), Presidential Assistance for Farmers and Fisherfolk (PAFF), and Tulong Dunong, while the House of Representatives' version provided ₱137 billion for ayuda. He said the House had expanded programs lodged in the budgets of the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), Department of Health (DOH), and Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), while reducing health promotion, immunization and health-facility funding to increase MAIFIP.

The coalition asked the Senate to redirect House ayuda increases toward rules-based programs, including MAIFIP to PhilHealth, Tulong Dunong to the Tertiary Education Subsidy, and AICS to the Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program (4Ps); prohibit legislators' and staff's guarantee or referral letters and reserved allocations; establish processing timelines, emergency priority lanes, alternatives for applicants lacking complete documents or PhilSys IDs, and a public appeals process; and make civil-society participation a regular written part of the Senate budget process.
