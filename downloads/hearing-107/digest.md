- **Funding priorities and delivery:** Senators sought passport-funding restoration, stronger tourism infrastructure, expanded school feeding and learning recovery, and support for science facilities. Classroom delivery, staffing and fund utilization were central to the education debate.

E-liquid testing laboratories and standards: Senator Pia S. Cayetano asked how e-liquids and consumables were being tested. Aquino said DTI could reject flavors marketed to or attractive to minors under the existing law and had done so. Cayetano asked for a broader risk-based standard; the exchange did not produce a commitment to adopt one.

## Philippine Space Agency (PhilSA)

### Senator Imee R. Marcos

February 2027 pilot launch and the planned spaceport: Marcos asked about an announced launch that she understood might occur in April 2027. Aquino clarified that PhilSA planned a separate rocket launch in February 2027, unrelated to the SpaceX press release. He described the liquid-rocket launch as a pilot to develop personnel, technology, regulation, safety procedures and infrastructure for Philippine launch capability. The Cagayan spaceport remained a project for the coming years; the February launch would be an early phase of that effort.

Sovereign satellite data and domestic capability: Marcos questioned the need to build domestic launch capability when she said the U.S. National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) could provide remote-sensing imagery at no cost and cited an earlier facility that was little used. Aquino said reliance on foreign satellites and companies limited Philippine control and could require payment; he described sovereign access to satellite data and local infrastructure and expertise as goals. He said satellite data could support weather and defense needs, agriculture, environmental monitoring, natural-resource management, disaster risk reduction and national security, rather than an astronaut program.

Space-food research and Philippine crops: Marcos asked about reports of Philippine carabao mangoes being used for space missions. Aquino said the work was space-food research: experiments to test whether food endemic to the Philippines could be used by other nationalities in their space-food programs. He described Philippine food being used in space as a possible economic benefit, not a confirmed mango mission.

Commercial launch discussions, site concerns and information to the Senate: Marcos asked whether reports of SpaceX taking over islands for commercial rocket launches were true. Aquino said PhilSA was in early discussions with SpaceX and Rocket Lab to explore a Philippine launch platform; he did not confirm a takeover or agreement. He relayed that PhilSA cited the Pacific location, eastbound launch path and CEZA infrastructure, including an airport and seaport, as reasons the site might be suitable. Marcos disputed the readiness of the Lal-lo airport and raised security concerns. Aquino said potential rent could benefit the country, agreed that the activity should be checked to ensure it was not for weapons, and relayed the agency's official answer that there were no talks about warfare and that the effort was commercial. Marcos asked that information be shared with the Senate if an agreement arose that would require treaty ratification.
