## At a glance

- **Department and sponsor presentation:** Rep. Bella Vanessa B. Suansing presented the Department of National Defense (DND) and attached agencies, describing external and internal defense, Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP) modernization, veterans’ benefits and disaster response. Her opening presentation gave budget categories but no aggregate department amount.

- **Maritime security, drones and exercises:** Rep. Presley C. De Jesus asked about West Philippine Sea surveillance, fisher support, Balikatan and other exercises, drone integration and cost-sharing. Suansing described coordination and modernization priorities but did not establish a 50–50 exercise-cost arrangement.

- **Infrastructure, gender programs and military-access costs:** Rep. Sarah Jane I. Elago questioned Tatag ng Infrastruktura para sa Kapayapaan at Seguridad (TIKAS) project spending and completion records, gender and development programming, Women, Peace and Security activities, Enhanced Defense Cooperation Agreement (EDCA) costs and effects on fisherfolk. The project figures and status categories were not fully reconciled, and no cumulative EDCA total was supplied.

- **Drone policy, partnerships and campus rights:** Rep. Renee Louise M. Co pursued the delivery of the Toboso post-operations report, then asked about a disputed drone video, school partnerships, scholarships, military presence on campus and alleged harassment of University of Santo Tomas (UST) student Raven Rosales. DND later delivered the requested post-operations report during the round; its investigation of the drone allegation remained separate.

- **Modernization and pensions:** Rep. Antonio L. Tinio questioned AFP modernization funding, public reporting and the military and uniformed personnel pension burden. Suansing said the modernization proposal fell below the department’s stated need, agreed pension reform required study and supported further evaluation.

- **Foreign influence and defense posture:** Tinio challenged foreign-influence narratives concerning Chinese-Filipino schools and argued that U.S. state-linked voices also required scrutiny. Citing conflict risks to military and economic sites, he advocated greater self-reliance and regional cooperation. Suansing took note of his concerns and urged support for modernization and archipelagic defense.

- **Army readiness and personnel concerns:** Rep. Sergio C. Dagooc challenged Army readiness results and described personnel concerns. Suansing attributed the gaps to new standards and evolving threats; Dagooc urged review and supported modernization.

## Agency budget proposals

### Department of National Defense and attached agencies

AFP modernization. Suansing described a ₱50 billion FY 2027 National Expenditure Program (NEP) proposal for the AFP Modernization Program and said it fell short of DND’s stated ₱126.96 billion need. She identified ₱35 billion for Horizon 2 and 3 projects and additional multi-year contract obligations in FY 2027, plus ₱21.2 billion in existing multi-year contracts due under the FY 2026 General Appropriations Act (GAA). She summarized these servicing obligations as ₱56 billion. Tinio gave two unreconciled historical modernization totals: ₱469.393 billion for 2013 through the proposed FY 2027 period, then ₱489.5 billion after adding FY 2027. The aggregate FY 2027 DND and attached-agencies NEP allocation and original agency-submission total were not stated; the modernization need is distinct from that missing submission total.

Budget categories. Suansing described the DND proposal as covering personnel and operating needs, AFP modernization capital outlay, veteran pensions and benefits, and civil defense. She appealed for immediate passage.

Military and uniformed personnel (MUP) pensions. Tinio cited cross-government pension totals of ₱145 billion for FY 2026 and ₱154 billion for proposed FY 2027, and cumulative funding of ₱1.295 trillion from 2014 through proposed FY 2027. These figures cover MUP services across government, not DND or AFP alone. Tinio said the national budget bears all pension costs. Suansing agreed the burden was unsustainable but said reform required further study; she wanted AFP personnel distinguished from civilian uniformed services in any burden-sharing proposal and said DND was open to reform subject to assessment. No timeline or adopted reform was stated.

Convergence program and construction projects. Elago cited ₱38.03 billion in combined DND–Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) Tatag ng Infrastruktura para sa Kapayapaan at Seguridad (TIKAS) program funding for 2019–2025. A DND submission listed Philippine Army TIKAS projects valued at ₱14.496 billion for 2021–2026 and Philippine Air Force projects valued at ₱4.806 billion for 2019–2025. Elago said the spending exceeded the Department of Science and Technology’s (DOST) entire budget for FY 2027. Elago said the project lines appeared in DPWH’s Basic Infrastructure Program (BIP) after having been listed under TIKAS. She separately cited a San Antonio, Zambales barracks and boot-camp project with a ₱56.91 million contract, ₱60 million in the 2024 GAA and a ₱6.5 million FY 2027 NEP line; The contract ran from September 2024 to July 2025; DPWH showed 66.81% accomplishment. For a married-quarters rehabilitation project, she cited a ₱28.67 million contract, ₱30 million in the 2025 GAA and a ₱25 million FY 2027 NEP line; DPWH showed 0% accomplishment. Suansing said DND submitted no FY 2027 TIKAS projects and had no TIKAS project list. Elago requested a project-by-project accounting; Suansing asked her to furnish the details for review and said DND would raise the awards concern with DPWH. The project amounts and the multi-year TIKAS totals have different scopes and are not summed into a DND-wide NEP figure.

Gender and development (GAD). Elago cited a Philippine Commission on Women (PCW) 2024 assessment that 80.6% of DND’s Women, Peace and Security (WPS) allocation went directly to related activities, programs and expenditures. She said the Philippine Army ranked fifth in GAD allocation at 15.37%, against a general minimum of at least 5%. The discussion also covered WPS programming and service-member protections; these utilization and allocation percentages are prior-year policy evidence, not proposed FY 2027 appropriations.

Other requested amounts. Elago asked for cumulative government spending under the Enhanced Defense Cooperation Agreement (EDCA), but Suansing gave no cumulative figure. She said the FY 2027 proposal covered regular camp expenses without an EDCA-specific line item, and Elago requested a breakdown. De Jesus asked for a drone appropriation; Suansing gave no amount, saying governance systems would be funded first and agreeing that analytical personnel, communications and maintenance could yield more benefit than equipment alone. No amount for either question was established.

Members expressed support for the DND budget and pressed for stronger funding and oversight. The joint majority–minority motion to terminate interpellation was carried without objection; no increase or restoration was adopted in this record.

## Department of National Defense (DND) and attached agencies

### DND mandate and operating context

Suansing described the Department of National Defense (DND) as responsible for guarding against external and internal threats to national peace and security. ### Rep. Antonio L. Tinio

Tinio argued that the public could not readily find official DND or Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP) information explaining how modernization funds were used. He pointed to a GAA special provision requiring quarterly financial and physical accomplishment reports through the Unified Reporting System (URS) or other electronic means and AFP website posting. In his review of available posts, he said the reports showed limited financial detail—such as obligations and disbursements—and physical-target completion percentages, and he cited gaps in quarterly website postings for 2023–2026. He asked whether DND was complying with the legal posting requirement.

Suansing said DND submitted reports to Congress, the Senate and the President. She explained that the website reports appeared periodically because new equipment was not purchased every quarter, and that some materials were classified or subject to operational-security requirements. Tinio replied that the required financial and physical reports were aggregate figures rather than sensitive equipment details and argued that the posting requirement still applied. Asked for a commitment, Suansing said DND would look into ways to be more transparent and disclose more information; she did not promise complete posting of every report or resolve the disagreement over website compliance.

Tinio asked whether DND agreed that the pension requirement had become a heavy and unsustainable burden. Suansing answered yes, then qualified the response: DND believed proposed reform required further study, wanted AFP personnel segregated from civilian uniformed services in any burden-sharing scheme, and was open to reforming the current system depending on assessment and evaluation. Tinio pressed whether reform could be expected before the administration’s final two years; Suansing again said further study was needed. Tinio interpreted that as no assured reform within the current administration, but the sponsor did not make a categorical promise either for or against implementing reform by then.

Tinio defined foreign information manipulation and interference (FIMI) as coordinated conduct by foreign states or state-linked actors intended to distort the information environment, deepen polarization and undermine democratic institutions. He discussed Secretary Teodoro’s appearance on a podcast about the Indo-Pacific and focused on maritime commentator Ray Powell and the SeaLight Initiative. Tinio attributed claims about Chinese-Filipino schools and universities serving as conduits for Chinese FIMI—including a claim that Batanes belonged to China—to Powell’s study, while urging that the claims be objectively assessed and that long-established institutions and communities not be unfairly targeted or “China-tagged.” He cited the study’s discussion of Chinese-Filipino schools, mainstream Philippine schools and universities, and alleged ties to Chinese institutions; those allegations are Tinio’s account of the study, not findings established by this exchange.

Tinio argued that scrutiny should apply equally to foreign influence from the United States. He cited material he identified as U.S. Navy reporting that the Office of Naval Research supported the creation of Stanford’s Gordian Knot Center for National Security Innovation, from which he described SeaLight as a spin-off. He characterized Powell and SeaLight as state-linked actors and said media appearances should disclose that connection, while emphasizing that Powell remained free to speak. Tinio framed the point as a call for independent foreign policy and caution against accepting U.S. narratives uncritically, as well as opposition to stigmatizing the Chinese-Filipino community.

Tinio used reports about the Iran war to warn that military bases and civilian economic infrastructure associated with the United States could face attack in a regional conflict. He cited a report of 172 attacks on non-military infrastructure in six Gulf Cooperation Council states, including oil and gas ports, airports, data centers, power stations and refineries, as well as attacks on U.S. bases. He then asked how the Philippines would defend its own sites as U.S. access expanded through Enhanced Defense Cooperation Agreement (EDCA) and non-EDCA locations, including Subic, and through the Luzon Economic Corridor and what he called Pax Silica. He referred to Subic, Clark, Batangas and Laguna and raised risks to the country’s economic infrastructure. His account relied on reports and statements he presented during the exchange; the sponsor did not offer a site-by-site threat assessment.

Tinio cited the Heritage Foundation’s “Tidal Wave” logistics report, which he said projected that U.S. ammunition supplies could last less than a week and fuel less than a month in an all-out war with China. He also described a Defense Priorities proposal for a second-island-chain strategy, a non-intervention position on Taiwan, and ending U.S. mutual-defense alliances with South Korea, Australia, the Philippines and Japan. He said that proposal implied a U.S. withdrawal toward Guam and questioned continued reliance on the U.S. Mutual Defense Treaty, advocating greater Philippine self-reliance and cooperation with Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) neighbors instead. In the final response, Suansing said DND took note of his concerns and argued that they were precisely why the country should support AFP modernization and the Comprehensive Archipelagic Defense Concept to protect Philippine territory. Her answer did not detail a response to each cited report or Tinio’s specific concern about the sites and treaty.

### Rep. Sergio C. Dagooc

Dagooc questioned Philippine Army readiness results in the Land Forces Defense Program performance report. Tactical units reached 64% against an 81% target for prescribed readiness condition; ready reserve units reached 28% against a 79% target. The report attributed part of the shortfall to a revamp of the readiness rating system intended to identify and address capability gaps. Dagooc asked whether changing the system had lowered reported accomplishment and whether the Army’s actual fighting capability had been overstated in earlier years. Suansing said standards had been adjusted as threats and mission areas evolved, and assured him that earlier performance indicators had not been overstated.

Dagooc asked what concrete steps would ensure the revamp improved readiness rather than only changing measurement on paper. Suansing described a continuing process of upgrading, rescoping and rescaling against current threats and the targets the Army had set. Asked why the actual figures fell so sharply, she said the figures were measured under new standards, creating a gap; she also cited a wider set of domains, including cyber, as contributing to the difference between actual performance and target. Dagooc said some uniformed personnel were dissatisfied with their ratings and felt their training was being treated as substandard despite long service, and asked the department to review those concerns. He voiced support for the DND and Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP) budget and urged implementation of the modernization program. Dagooc then moved to terminate the period of interpellation and debate.
