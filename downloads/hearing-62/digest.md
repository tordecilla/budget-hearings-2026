## National Irrigation Administration (NIA) budget

### Restoration cuts and drought readiness

At the opening of the hearing on the National Irrigation Administration (NIA) FY2027 budget, Senator Francis Kiko Pangilinan, chair of Subcommittee G of the Senate Committee on Finance, said funding for restoration of irrigation systems was down 58 percent. ### Project-list stability and proposed release safeguards

Pangilinan asked how NIA could keep project lists stable enough for early procurement when the budget changed during congressional deliberations. Marcos described lists being altered again in the House and bicameral conference, while Guillen proposed organizing NIA’s appropriations by program and sub-program, with a validated project list, rather than making each individual project a rigid line item. NIA said a complete list could identify locations, beneficiaries, approved allocations, procurement status and periodic accomplishments while preserving flexibility to adjust projects within the authorized program.

Department of Budget and Management (DBM) Director Elena Regina S. Brillantes said project specifics and amounts needed to match between the NEP and GAA. She illustrated the problem with a hypothetical 10-kilometer project listed at ₱10 million in the original list but funded at ₱8 million in the GAA: NIA’s ₱10 million approved budget for the bid would exceed the amount available. The exchange identified the list-and-amount mismatch as a cause of rework; it did not settle a final process.

Pangilinan returned to the project-list problem and asked DBM for a workable alternative to both a lump sum and project details that become obsolete. Brillantes said NIA remained subject to DBM evaluation during execution; the NEP and GAA used program and sub-program structures, but the evaluated project list was not attached to the allotment release. Her recommendation was a special provision making an evaluated, NIA Board-approved project list an integral part of the Special Allotment Release Order (SARO), so released funds would be tied to listed projects. Marcos asked DBM to submit its recommendation in writing for the Finance Committee and bicameral conference.

Guillen said NIA needed early procurement because irrigation work must pause when water is being delivered and delaying modifications until January could undermine implementation. He asked for flexibility without an unrestricted lump sum. Marcos questioned whether DBM already received and evaluated NIA’s validated, shovel-ready lists; Brillantes distinguished evaluation from attaching the list to the SARO, and explained that project lists had ceased to accompany releases after the shift to program-expenditure classification. Pangilinan said the committee would study the wording and test the process with NIA before plenary. No final provision or reform was adopted in this exchange.

### Central Luzon flood storage, irrigation and dam plans

### Sugar areas and interagency flood and water management

Asked about irrigation support for Negros' sugar industry amid drought and the RSS pest, Guillen identified a proposed Tanjay-Bais Dam and said NIA was coordinating with the Sugar Regulatory Administration (SRA) to identify projects for funding. NIA offered technical expertise and project proposals while the SRA could provide funding, similar to NIA's proposed coordination with the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) on flood-mitigation projects.

Guillen described a Cagayan project where DPWH's flood-control project office built a sabo dam and NIA built the irrigation canal. He said NIA had also proposed shovel-ready sabo dams to DPWH, which could implement them. River walls, he argued, can accelerate water flow, while sabo dams can slow water, support irrigation, control sediment and recharge groundwater. NIA could provide project locations and plans without controlling implementation funds. Ejercito and Pangilinan criticized river-wall and slope-protection spending where it did not protect communities and emphasized integrated river-system and water-resource management.

Pangilinan asked whether a formal interagency organization existed for Central Luzon; Guillen said NIA had a general memorandum of agreement with DPWH. Pangilinan proposed taking an initiative with Pampanga Governor Lilia "Nanay" Pineda, municipal leaders, Nueva Ecija and Central Luzon officials, NIA and DPWH; Guillen added the need to coordinate with the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples and DENR approvals. Pangilinan asked the agencies to note the proposal. Guillen argued that adding irrigation to flood projects can bring economic returns for farmers alongside social and environmental benefits, making projects more sustainable. Pangilinan called for revisiting the country's integrated water-resource management arrangements, including whether a Department of Water was needed; no new coordinating body was established in the exchange.
