### NBI hazard pay, eligibility and cross-agency parity

Senator Panfilo "Ping" M. Lacson asked Department of Justice (DOJ) budget sponsor Senator Francis Kiko Pangilinan about the National Bureau of Investigation (NBI) hazard-pay request. Pangilinan said NBI hazard pay was PHP 4,000 per month and the requested increase was up to PHP 15,000 per month. He said the computation followed what uniformed personnel receive; Lacson cited the Philippine National Police (PNP) rate of PHP 540 monthly hazard pay plus PHP 3,000 combat-duty pay for personnel engaged in combat operations. Lacson worried that setting NBI pay at PHP 15,000 could prompt a larger PNP workforce to seek the same amount, creating a recurring benefit under the 2027 General Appropriations Act (GAA) if adopted. He said the request was not opposed in principle but raised concerns about the legal basis, parity and tight fiscal space. Pangilinan acknowledged the recurring comparison of benefits across agencies.

Pangilinan said the proposed NBI benefit covered restoration for technical and forensic personnel and an increase for agents and special investigators, citing 680 personnel. Lacson clarified that 680 was not the NBI's full complement; Pangilinan gave 2,700 as total strength, while the 680 figure referred to personnel performing investigations and related field, forensic, technical, motor-pool or security work. Pangilinan said only those in active investigations would receive the hazard pay, and agreed that the entitlement would move with personnel if assignments changed. He added that the 680 were board-passers, such as lawyers, accountants, nurses and doctors, and said their professional level could justify a different classification. Lacson questioned how eligibility would be calculated when duties changed, and said he wanted the eligible populations and costs for the PNP, Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP) and other uniformed services clarified before extending comparable benefits. He also noted that Bureau of Corrections personnel received no hazard pay; Pangilinan agreed the issue should be discussed and resolved reasonably.

Pangilinan proposed a meeting among Senate and sponsor staff and the NBI and DOJ teams to reconcile the concerns. He agreed to discuss a reasonable approach and noted that other groups with comparable duties might seek the same benefits. The hearing records no decision approving the NBI increase or extending hazard pay to other agencies. Lacson also said the Armed Forces used the same PHP 540 hazard-pay and PHP 3,000 combat-duty standards as the PNP, and that any increase should remain reasonable.

Zubiri said some agricultural workers on smaller farms and plantations worked a full day but received half the daily wage; he gave PHP 250 paid against an agricultural rate of PHP 450 and said workers were asked to sign half-day records. He urged DOLE to investigate, conduct proactive inspections rather than wait for complaints, interview workers privately, and publicize workers’ rights, including that full-day work should receive full-day pay. He also raised piece-rate arrangements, saying workers could lose when rates were set too low, and reminded farmers and landowners to pay 13th-month pay even to workers who had worked only three months, on a proportional basis. Villanueva said DOLE was monitoring the piece-rate system and cited its nationwide toll-free 1530 line, available 24/7, for complaints.

Wage board increases and publication dates: Villanueva said regional wage boards had approved increases, citing publication dates and amounts rather than stating that every increase was already effective: Central Visayas, September 28, PHP 42; CALABARZON, October 10, 2026, PHP 25 to PHP 45; Western Visayas, publicized November 4, PHP 40 to PHP 45; and SOCCSKSARGEN, November 6, PHP 41. He said Ilocos Region was scheduled for publication on November 28 with an increase of PHP 35 to PHP 40, and Cagayan Valley publication by November 28 with an increase of around PHP 30 to PHP 40. He presented these as wage-board actions and upcoming publications to help minimum-wage earners.

Wage complaints, emergency employment, and student support: Villanueva said DOLE launched a nationwide online wage-earner complaint program that day. Citing a 269,000 decline in agriculture and forestry workers from August to September 2026, he described Tulong Panghanapbuhay sa Ating Disadvantaged/Displaced Workers (TUPAD) emergency employment and the DOLE Integrated Livelihood Program (DILP) for affected households, alongside DOLE monitoring of piece-rate work and the 1530 toll-free line available nationwide 24/7. He said the Special Program for Employment of Students (SPES) could help students remain in school by providing emergency support, and supported more TESDA scholarship programs in anticipation of El Niño’s effects. Zubiri emphasized labor welfare together with education and training as longer-term responses. Villanueva gave a labor-force figure of 50 million. Zubiri framed the appeal against what he described as 2.1 percent growth and a risk of stagflation.

DOLE and attached agencies deemed submitted; session suspended: After Senator Zubiri moved that the Department of Labor and Employment, its listed offices and attached agencies, and TESDA be deemed submitted, Senate President Sherwin "Win" T. Gatchalian stated that the DOLE budget and its attached agencies were deemed submitted. Zubiri then moved to suspend deliberation on House Bill No. 10858; the motion was approved. The Senate suspended its session until 1:00 p.m. Thursday, November 26, 2026, in Gatchalian's final ruling; Zubiri had corrected his motion from morning to afternoon.
